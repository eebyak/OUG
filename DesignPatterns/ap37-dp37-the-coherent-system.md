## AP-37 · Antipattern: The Decoupled Architecture

**Context — where this appears**
Any institution in which governance structures, strategy, and implementation were designed at different times, by different people, for different purposes, and have never been reconciled into a coherent whole. Extremely common in established institutions; also a risk in founded institutions that design governance and strategy in separate workstreams.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
A new strategic priority is announced, and staff nod in meetings but nothing in their day-to-day work changes, because the committees that would need to act on it were never told they had a role. People describe the strategy document and the org chart as belonging to "different conversations" that happen to be about the same institution. A governance reform is implemented with genuine energy, and six months later nobody can point to a strategic document it was meant to serve.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
A new strategic priority is adopted. The governance structures were designed for a different strategy. The new priority cannot be effectively pursued because the committees, mandates, and decision processes were built around the old one. Alternatively, governance is redesigned without updating the strategy it is meant to carry. The layers are internally coherent but mutually misaligned. Each layer blames the others for dysfunction that is architectural rather than behavioural.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
The institution expends significant energy navigating the gap between its governance architecture and its strategic intent. Reform efforts address one layer while leaving the others unchanged, which is why they produce less change than expected. Strategy documents describe an institution that the governance architecture cannot produce. Governance processes enforce constraints that the strategy no longer requires. The misalignment is invisible to those inside it because each layer appears internally coherent.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
Treat governance architecture, strategy, and implementation as a single design object reviewed together, not as three separate workstreams that happen to share an institution. Whenever one layer changes, require the implications for the other layers to be assessed in the same governance cycle, not discovered later as dysfunction.

## DP-37 · Design Pattern: The Coherent System

*Structural inverse of: The Decoupled Architecture*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution, at founding or during a strategic pivot, that wants strategy to actually be implementable by the governance structures it has — rather than describing an institution those structures cannot produce.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To ensure governance architecture, strategy, and implementation stay mutually coherent as each one changes, so that reform in one layer is never quietly absorbed or blocked by an unreformed layer elsewhere.

**Structure — what must be built**
The institution treats governance architecture, strategy, and implementation processes as a single design object that must be coherent at every level simultaneously. When one layer changes, the implications for the other layers are assessed and addressed in the same governance cycle. The institution has a named mechanism — a governance review or design authority — that holds the system coherence mandate.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
Most applicable at founding or at major strategic pivots. In founded institutions, coherence can be built in from the start. In established institutions, achieving coherence requires a governance reform process that touches all layers simultaneously — which is the most politically difficult kind of reform and the most structurally necessary. **The OUG's Design Signature is the record that verifies system coherence across the founding architecture** — the book's own words.

**Result — what the institution gains**
Strategy can be implemented because the governance structures were designed to carry it. Governance reforms produce visible change because they are connected to strategy. When the environment changes and strategy must adapt, the governance implications are assessed immediately rather than discovered later through dysfunction. The institution can describe the relationship between each governance element and the strategic goal it serves.
