# Pattern Pair 39: Strategy Formation: Received Instruction versus Built Ownership

## AP-39 · Antipattern: The Extracted Strategy

**Context — where this appears**
Any institution in which strategy is formed by a small group of senior leaders — with or without consultant support — and then communicated downward to the people responsible for implementing it. The dominant model in most institutions, including those that use the language of participation while treating the strategy as essentially pre-decided.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
Staff can recite the strategy's headline priorities but cannot explain why those particular priorities were chosen over the alternatives that were considered. The strategy launch event is well attended and quickly forgotten. Six months later, implementation has stalled in several units, and the explanation given is "people don't understand the strategy" rather than "people were never part of forming it."

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
Senior leadership forms the strategy, often in a retreat or intensive planning cycle. The result is communicated through town halls, emails, or documents. People at operational levels understand what is expected of them. They do not understand why specific choices were made, what alternatives were considered, or how the strategy connects to the problems they experience daily. They implement instructions rather than pursuing a shared understanding.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
Implementation requires sustained energy expenditure to overcome the resistance that comes from people who were not involved in the strategy's formation. The strategy's authors must continuously re-explain and re-motivate. When the strategy encounters unexpected obstacles, the people who must adapt to them lack the strategic understanding to adapt intelligently — they can only report up and wait for instruction. The institution moves slowly because its operational capacity is not aligned with its strategic direction.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
Form strategy through structured, in-person engagement with practitioners across the institution — harvesting ideas, consolidating them into guiding principles, and validating the result iteratively with the same groups who contributed — so that ownership, not just awareness, is what implementation can draw on.

## DP-39 · Design Pattern: The Owned Strategy

*Structural inverse of: The Extracted Strategy*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution forming or renewing its strategy that wants implementation to be carried by genuine understanding rather than by instructions issued from above.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To make strategy something practitioners helped build and therefore understand well enough to adapt intelligently when circumstances change, rather than something they merely received.

**Structure — what must be built**
Strategy emerges from a structured process in which ideas are harvested from practitioners across the institution through in-person engagement, consolidated into guiding principles, iteratively validated with the same groups who contributed, and reduced to a manageable set of measurable goals. The process is explicitly designed to produce ownership, not only consensus. People know how their contribution shaped the result.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
Requires leadership willingness to accept that the strategy formation process is itself a significant governance investment — taking up to two years — and that the speed lost in formation is recovered many times over in implementation. In-person engagement is not optional: survey-based participation extracts data without building shared understanding. Applicable at founding and at major strategic renewal cycles. The distinction between genuine participation and the participation performance (the survey that is analysed and then filed) must be maintained.

**Result — what the institution gains**
Implementation proceeds faster because the people responsible for it understand the strategy's logic rather than only its directives. Unexpected obstacles are addressed intelligently at the level where they arise. The institution's operational capacity and strategic direction are aligned because the same process that formed the strategy also built the understanding required to pursue it. Leadership energy is directed toward enabling rather than overcoming.

> **Note:** this is the direct theoretical source for the OUG's own [Strategy Formation Protocol](../strategy/strategy-formation-protocol.md) — matching down to specific details: the seven-phase harvesting-and-validation structure, the explicit "up to eighteen months" timeline (the Protocol's own figure, close to this pattern's "up to two years"), the requirement for in-person over survey-based participation, and the explicit rejection of "participation performance." Not a loose inspiration — the Protocol operationalises this exact pattern.
