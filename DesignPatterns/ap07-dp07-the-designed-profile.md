# Pattern Pair 7: Learner Diversity and Profile Design

## AP-7 · Antipattern: The Average Learner Fallacy

**Context — where this appears**
Any higher education institution that designs its curriculum, pacing, support structures, and progression requirements around a single implicit model of what a student is: full-time, campus-present, recently school-educated, without significant external obligations, and pursuing a degree as a primary life activity.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
The institution describes its programmes as open to all, yet its deadlines, contact hours, assessment formats, and progression gates assume a specific life situation. Students who do not match the implicit profile manage — but they manage despite the system, not because of it. When these students struggle or leave, the institution interprets this as individual failure rather than design mismatch.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
Institutional design is an act of imagination. When institutions imagine a student, they imagine the student who has historically been present — typically young, full-time, campus-based, without caregiving responsibilities. This imagined student becomes the design subject for all structural decisions: timetabling, assessment scheduling, module sequencing, contact hour requirements. The imagined student is then treated as the default, and every departure from that default becomes a request for accommodation rather than evidence of a design gap.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution and its learners**
A significant proportion of students who could succeed intellectually do not succeed institutionally — because the institutional structures were not designed for them. The institution loses the learning contribution these students would have made. It also reproduces the social stratification it claims to address: the students most likely to match the average learner assumption are those with the most conventional educational histories and the fewest external obligations.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
The institution explicitly defines which learner profiles it is designing for — not as exceptions or accommodations, but as primary design subjects. Each structural decision (deadline, format, pacing, access requirement) is tested against multiple profiles before implementation. The question is not "does this work for a standard student?" but "does this work for all the profiles this institution serves?"

## DP-7 · Design Pattern: The Designed Profile

*Structural inverse of: The Average Learner Fallacy*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution that genuinely intends to serve a diverse learner population — including working adults, caregivers, career changers, and people without traditional academic histories — and that wishes to design its structures for this diversity rather than accommodate it as an afterthought.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To make learner profile diversity a primary input to institutional design, so that the structures the institution builds are genuinely useful to the full range of people it serves — not optimised for one profile and tolerable for the rest.

**Structure — what must be built**
The institution maintains an explicit Learner Profile Map: a documented set of the distinct profiles it designs for, including their characteristic time constraints, prior learning backgrounds, decision-making horizons, and support needs. Every significant structural decision — module pacing, assessment format, progression gate design, support service hours — is reviewed against the full profile map before implementation. Accommodation requests are treated as evidence of design gaps, not individual needs.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
New programmes specify their target profiles alongside their learning outcomes. Programme reviews include a profile-alignment audit: for each structural feature, which profiles does it serve well, and which does it disadvantage? When a feature disadvantages a defined target profile, redesign is required. The institution tracks completion and achievement data disaggregated by profile, making structural misalignment visible rather than attributing it to individual difference.

**Result — what the institution and its learners gain**
The institution's structures are genuinely useful to the full range of people it serves. Students who do not match the historically dominant profile do not experience themselves as exceptions requiring accommodation — they experience themselves as people the institution was designed for. Completion and achievement gaps between profiles narrow over time, not because standards have lowered but because structural barriers have been removed.
