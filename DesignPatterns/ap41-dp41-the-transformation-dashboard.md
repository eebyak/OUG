# Pattern Pair 41: Performance Governance: Present Health versus Developmental Trajectory

## AP-41 · Antipattern: The Monitoring Trap

**Context — where this appears**
Any institution that governs its performance primarily through Key Performance Indicators measuring current system health, without performance instruments that orient governance toward future development. Common in institutions that have invested in data infrastructure and reporting systems without distinguishing between health monitoring and transformation management.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
Every governance meeting opens with a dashboard of current metrics and closes with small adjustments to current operations. Nobody in the room can point to an instrument that tracks whether the institution is becoming what its own strategy says it wants to become. Ambitious language in the strategic plan has no corresponding entry anywhere in the reporting the institution actually reviews.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
Leadership reviews KPIs regularly. The institution knows whether it is performing at or below current benchmarks. It does not know whether it is moving toward where it needs to be. Every governance discussion begins with current performance and ends with adjustments to current operations. No governance instrument asks the question: are we becoming the institution that our stated purpose requires? The KPI system optimises what is measured, which is the present state of the institution rather than its developmental trajectory.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
The institution improves at what it already does rather than developing the capabilities it will need. Strategic priorities stated in aspirational terms have no governance instrument through which to exert ongoing pressure on resource allocation, decision-making, or capability development. Leadership energy is absorbed by current performance management rather than by institutional development. The gap between what the institution is and what it has committed to become is never directly measured, so it never closes.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
Maintain a second, explicitly separate performance layer — objectives and key results reviewed on a strategic cycle — alongside ordinary KPI monitoring, so that governance has a standing instrument for asking not only "are we healthy?" but "are we becoming what we said we would become?"

## DP-41 · Design Pattern: The Transformation Dashboard

*Structural inverse of: The Monitoring Trap*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution whose performance governance currently consists only of KPIs tracking present-state health, with no instrument asking whether it is developing toward its stated purpose.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To give governance a standing instrument for transformation, not only for monitoring — so ambition stated in strategy documents has somewhere to actually exert pressure on resource allocation and decision-making.

**Structure — what must be built**
The institution maintains both a health monitoring layer (KPIs, reported continuously) and a transformation management layer (OKRs, set at strategic cycle intervals and reviewed quarterly). The governance calendar explicitly distinguishes between health review sessions and transformation review sessions. Each OKR has a named owner, a measurable key result, and a governance body that is accountable for the institutional conditions that enable progress toward it.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
Requires the governance bodies to accept the distinction between the two layers and to treat OKR review as a qualitatively different kind of governance conversation from KPI review. In institutions where all performance review is experienced as accountability for current results, OKRs are quickly assimilated into the KPI framework and lose their transformative orientation. The distinction must be protected by governance design rather than only by cultural intention.

**Result — what the institution gains**
Leadership has a direct governance instrument for asking whether the institution is moving toward its stated purpose, not only whether it is performing its current functions adequately. Resource allocation and capability development decisions are connected to transformation targets rather than only to operational maintenance. The institution can demonstrate over time not only that it met minimum standards but that it developed toward its own purpose — which is the question the Developmental Audit asks.

> **Note:** the OUG's own [OKR Framework & Governance Dashboard](../strategy/okr-framework-dashboard.md) is this design pattern by another name — its entire founding argument ("KPIs answer the question: how are we doing? OKRs answer the question: where do we want to go, and are we getting there?") is the same KPI/OKR distinction specified here, down to the same terminology. The source text's closing line even points directly at the [Developmental Audit Framework](../quality/developmental-audit-framework.md) as the document that asks whether the institution developed toward its own purpose.
