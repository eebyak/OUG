# Pattern Pair 40: Feedback: Annual Discovery versus Continuous Signal

**The Closed Loop → The Adaptive Cycle**

## AP-40 · Antipattern: The Closed Loop

**Context — where this appears**
Any institution in which strategy is formed, communicated, and then implemented without a structured mechanism for detecting when implementation is diverging from strategic intent. Most institutions have annual or biannual strategy reviews; very few have continuous feedback mechanisms that make divergence visible between review cycles.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
Problems in implementation are known informally, sometimes for months, before the annual review makes them official. People closest to the misalignment have no channel for raising it other than waiting for the scheduled review or escalating informally through personal contacts. The same gap between strategy and reality reappears in successive annual cycles because nothing changed in how divergence gets detected between reviews.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
Strategy is implemented by responsible units without structured reporting on whether implementation is producing the intended outcomes. Divergence accumulates between strategy and reality. The annual review reveals that targets were not met, priorities drifted, or circumstances changed in ways that the strategy did not anticipate. Corrections are made in the annual cycle. The same divergence recurs in the next cycle because the underlying feedback architecture has not changed.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
The institution discovers strategic failures annually rather than detecting them continuously. Corrections are made after significant cost has been incurred rather than at the point of divergence. The people responsible for implementation cannot raise misalignments between their work and strategic goals because there is no channel through which to raise them. Strategic learning is slow because feedback is infrequent. The institution's ability to adapt to changing circumstances is bounded by its review cycle rather than by the speed of change.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
Give every responsible unit a defined reporting rhythm that makes the relationship between its work and strategic goals visible continuously, and aggregate findings on a dashboard reviewed between formal cycles — so that divergence is corrected while it is still cheap, not discovered a year later as an accomplished fact.

## DP-40 · Design Pattern: The Adaptive Cycle

*Structural inverse of: The Closed Loop*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution whose only mechanism for detecting strategic drift is an annual or biannual review, leaving divergence to accumulate silently between cycles.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To make strategic divergence visible as it happens, not once a year, so it can be corrected while it is still cheap rather than discovered as an accomplished fact.

**Structure — what must be built**
Each responsible unit has a named reporting rhythm that makes the relationship between its work and strategic goals visible on an ongoing basis. Findings are aggregated on a dashboard that is reviewed by governance bodies between annual cycles. When a unit identifies a misalignment, there is a named process for escalating it and a named body responsible for assessing whether the misalignment requires a strategic correction. The process of raising a misalignment is framed as a contribution, not a failure.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
Requires both technical infrastructure (the dashboard, the reporting format) and cultural permission (the norm that raising a correction is valuable rather than career-damaging). The technical infrastructure is the easier half. The cultural permission requires leadership behaviour that consistently responds to corrections with engagement rather than defensiveness. Most applicable at founding; retrofitting it into institutions with established review-cycle cultures requires sustained leadership modelling.

**Result — what the institution gains**
The institution detects strategic divergence early and corrects it cheaply. Strategic learning is continuous rather than annual. Responsible units have a channel through which their operational knowledge reaches governance decisions. The institution can adapt to changing circumstances between formal review cycles without waiting for the annual process. Corrections become part of normal governance rather than exceptional governance responses.

> **Note:** the OUG's own [OKR Framework & Governance Dashboard](../strategy/okr-framework-dashboard.md) — and specifically its **Bug Protocol** (Flag → Diagnose → Classify → Act → Learn, triggered when a Key Result scores below 0.4) — is a direct, named implementation of the Adaptive Cycle this pattern specifies, including the explicit framing of a missed target as institutional learning rather than individual failure, which matches this pattern's "framed as a contribution, not a failure" almost word for word.
