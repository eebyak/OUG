# Pattern Pair 38: Decision Process: Individual Judgement versus Named Sequence

**The Unsequenced Decision → The Prepared Decision**

## AP-38 · Antipattern: The Unsequenced Decision

**Context — where this appears**
Any governance process in which significant decisions can be made without passing through a defined sequence of preparation, alignment, and validation. Present in most institutions where governance has evolved informally and decision processes are role-dependent rather than decision-type-dependent.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
The same type of decision gets made in a different way each time depending on who happens to raise it and who happens to be in the room. People can describe who made a recent significant decision but not what process, if any, it went through. When a decision turns out badly, the retrospective conversation is about the individual's judgement, not about what knowledge should have been assembled before the decision was made.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
A decision arises. The role-holder with the most immediate authority acts on it. There is no named preparation body that should have assembled the relevant knowledge first, no coordination body that should have assessed implications across domains, no validation body that should have confirmed the decision meets institutional criteria. The decision is made quickly by whoever happens to hold relevant authority, with whatever information happens to be available.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
Decisions that require distributed knowledge are made by people who do not have that knowledge. Errors are discovered in implementation rather than in preparation. The same errors recur because the absence of a preparation stage means the same knowledge gaps reappear in each decision cycle. The institution's decision quality is bounded by the individual capacity of the person in the decision role rather than by the collective intelligence of the system.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
Name a fixed sequence for every significant decision type — a body that prepares it, a body that aligns it across domains, a body that decides, and a body that validates — so decision quality depends on the sequence rather than on which individual happened to be holding relevant authority that day.

## DP-38 · Design Pattern: The Prepared Decision

*Structural inverse of: The Unsequenced Decision*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution whose significant decisions currently vary in process depending on who is involved, rather than on a defined sequence tied to the decision type.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To make decision quality a property of the process rather than of the individual who happens to hold authority when the decision arises.

**Structure — what must be built**
Every significant decision type has a named sequence: a body that prepares it (assembling the relevant knowledge), a body that aligns it (assessing cross-domain implications), a body that decides, and a body that validates. The sequence is decision-type-dependent: operational decisions have shorter sequences than strategic ones. The preparation body's role is to ensure that when the decision body convenes, it has the knowledge it needs.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
Requires mapping decision types before designing decision processes. The investment is modest: a structured analysis of the institution's recurring significant decision types and the knowledge required for each. Most applicable at founding. In established institutions, introducing preparation stages for decisions that previously went straight to a decision body requires cultural as well as structural change: the decision body must accept that its role is synthesis rather than expertise.

**Result — what the institution gains**
Decision quality improves because decisions are made with complete rather than partial knowledge. Errors discovered in preparation are cheaper to correct than errors discovered in implementation. The institution builds institutional knowledge through the preparation process — the preparation body's work is a record of what was known about this decision type, which accumulates over time into institutional memory.

> **Note:** the OUG's own [Governance & Decision Framework](../governance/governance-architecture.md) — with its explicit decision domains and staged governance layers (G1–G4) — is the institution's working implementation of the Prepared Decision this pattern specifies.
