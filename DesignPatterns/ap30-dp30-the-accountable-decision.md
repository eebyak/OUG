# Pattern Pair 30: Joining Participation to Accountability

**The Participation Decoy → The Accountable Decision**

*Derived from: GP-7 — Participation and Decision-Making Authority Are Distinct and Must Be Connected to Accountability.*

[← Back to the pattern catalogue](index.md)

---

## AP-30 · Antipattern: The Participation Decoy

**Context — where this appears**
Any governance structure in which broad stakeholder participation is formally present but structurally disconnected from decision-making authority and accountability for outcomes. The Participation Decoy is the complement to [the Participation Façade (AP-22)](ap22-dp22-the-quality-mechanism.md): where the Participation Façade describes the absence of genuine consultation influence on decisions, the Participation Decoy describes the absence of accountability for those who participate in making them.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
Many stakeholders are involved in governance processes — committees are populated, forums are convened, working groups produce recommendations. Yet when a decision produces a poor outcome, no individual or body can be identified as accountable for it. The decision is attributed to a process rather than to a person or body. Problems are transferred between units — each of which participated in the original decision — without any unit accepting responsibility for the outcome. Stakeholders who participated in the decision feel neither responsible for its consequences nor empowered to change course.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
Participation and accountability are separated by design in most collegial governance systems. Participation is protected by academic freedom norms and democratic governance principles. Accountability is concentrated in formal leadership roles, which means that leaders bear responsibility for decisions they did not make alone while those who shaped the decisions bear no formal accountability. This separation creates an incentive for broad participation in decisions without genuine ownership of their consequences — which means that participation is often performative, undertaken to satisfy governance requirements rather than to genuinely contribute to and be responsible for outcomes.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
The institution is ungovernable in any direction that requires sustained accountability. Decisions are made but nobody owns their implementation. Problems persist because those who could change them experienced the decision that produced them as something that happened to them — a process they contributed to but do not feel responsible for. Change agents are exhausted by governance processes that consume their energy without producing the accountability that would make change durable.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
Every governance decision names both those who participated and those who are accountable for its implementation. The decision record specifies: who contributed, who decided, who is responsible for what the decision produces, and when accountability for outcomes will be reviewed. Participation without accountability is as structurally deficient as accountability without participation.

## DP-30 · Design Pattern: The Accountable Decision

*Structural inverse of: The Participation Decoy*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution that wishes its governance processes to produce genuine accountability for outcomes — not merely procedural compliance with participation requirements — so that the right to contribute to decisions is structurally connected to the obligation to take responsibility for their consequences.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To design governance processes in which participation and accountability are structurally joined — so that those who shape decisions are also accountable for what those decisions produce, and so that the institution can learn from outcomes rather than simply producing processes.

**Structure — what must be built**
Every significant governance decision produces a Decision Record with four named elements: who participated (with their perspective or expertise noted), who decided (the body or person with formal authority), who is accountable for implementation (named, not collective), and when accountability for outcomes will be reviewed. The Decision Record is published internally and available to all governance participants. When a decision produces a poor outcome, the accountability review identifies the named accountable party rather than attributing the outcome to the process. The accountable party is expected to respond — with analysis, with a revised approach, or with an honest account of why the outcome was not what was intended.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
When a curriculum reform is approved, the Decision Record names the Academic Steward accountable for its implementation — not the committee that approved it. When a partnership is admitted to the federation, the Decision Record names the Partnership Office lead accountable for its quality performance — not the process that admitted it. When the accountability review arrives, the named party presents to the governance body that made the original decision. The review asks: what did this produce, why, and what follows?

**Result — what the institution gains**
The institution accumulates genuine governance knowledge: what kinds of decisions produce the outcomes they intended, under what conditions, with what kinds of accountability structures. Participation remains broad and genuine because it is understood to carry genuine consequence — contributing to a decision means being part of a system that will account for what the decision produced. The governance culture shifts from process compliance to outcome ownership.

> **Note:** this pattern's Decision Record structure is functionally the same accountability loop as [DP-22's Decision Account](ap22-dp22-the-quality-mechanism.md) — DP-22 closes the loop between *consultation input* and *decision*; DP-30 closes the loop between *decision* and *accountable implementation*. Together they cover the full path from voice to outcome. The OUG's own [Committee Information Architecture](../governance/committee-information-architecture.md) and [Code of Collegial Conduct](../governance/code-of-collegial-conduct.md) implement elements of both.
