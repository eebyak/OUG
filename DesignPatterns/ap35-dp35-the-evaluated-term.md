# Pattern Pair 35: Leadership Accountability: Silent Tolerance versus Structured Review

## AP-35 · Antipattern: The Unreviewed Leader

**Context — where this appears**
Any governance architecture in which senior leadership roles are held for extended periods without systematic evaluation of role performance against defined objectives. Standard in most German higher education institutions, where the *Beamtenrecht* tradition makes formal performance review of professors rare and the collegial governance culture makes evaluation of leadership functions politically difficult.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
Leadership performance is discussed informally, in corridors and among peers, but never in a forum with the authority to act on what is said. Underperformance is tolerated for years because no single incident is severe enough to trigger a formal process, even as the accumulated pattern is well known. When a leadership role finally does change hands, the reasons given publicly rarely match what colleagues privately understood to be the actual reasons. Nobody can point to a document stating what the role-holder was supposed to achieve.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
A leadership role-holder sets their own priorities, defines their own success criteria, and operates without structured feedback from the governance bodies responsible for their appointment. Performance that is poor but not catastrophically so produces no governance response because there is no mechanism through which ordinary underperformance becomes visible. Poor leadership persists because the institution has no instrument for detecting it that is less drastic than a formal removal process, which requires a crisis rather than a correction.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
The institution accumulates the cost of mediocre leadership over extended periods without correction. Leadership roles become personal fiefdoms measured by the role-holder's own account of their performance. The governance bodies formally responsible for leadership quality cannot exercise that responsibility because they have no structured information about performance. When leadership transitions finally occur, institutional memory is lost because there was no ongoing documentation of goals, decisions, and their outcomes.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
Attach a named set of goals, timelines, and evaluation criteria to every leadership appointment, and build mid-term review into the term structure as a routine event rather than a response to crisis. Give the review real consequences — renewal, conditional renewal, or transition planning — so that ordinary underperformance becomes visible and addressable long before it requires a crisis to surface.

## DP-35 · Design Pattern: The Evaluated Term

*Structural inverse of: The Unreviewed Leader*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution whose senior leadership roles currently operate without structured, periodic review against stated objectives, and whose governance body responsible for appointments is willing to receive and act on performance information.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To make leadership performance visible on a regular cycle, so that correction is possible well before underperformance accumulates into a crisis that only a removal process can address.

**Structure — what must be built**
Every senior leadership role appointment includes an agreed set of goals, timelines, and evaluation criteria. Mid-term reviews are built into the term structure rather than triggered by problems. The governance body responsible for appointment receives structured performance reporting at the mid-term and end-of-term review. The review has four named outcomes: full renewal, conditional renewal with specified changes, transition planning, or immediate transition.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
Requires the governance body responsible for leadership appointments — **in the OUG's case, the Hochschulrat for G1 and G2 roles** — to be willing to receive, process, and act on performance information. The political resistance is significant in institutions where leadership evaluation is culturally associated with personal attack. The pattern is most easily introduced at founding, when norms are being set. Retrofitting it into institutions with established cultures requires sustained executive will.

**Result — what the institution gains**
Leadership quality becomes visible and therefore improvable. The institution can detect underperformance and respond with correction rather than only with crisis intervention. Role-holders know their performance is evaluated against stated criteria, which changes the incentive structure: the role is a responsibility to be performed rather than a position to be maintained. Institutional memory is preserved through the structured documentation that the evaluation process requires.

> **The book connects this pair to AP-23 directly.** A short note accompanying this entry in the source states: *"AP-35 and AP-23 (The Accidental Leader) are sequential failure modes. AP-23 describes the governance failure of appointing people to leadership roles without developing their capacity for the role. AP-35 describes what happens after that appointment: the absence of any mechanism for detecting whether the appointment is working. An institution can partially address AP-23 by introducing leadership development programmes while leaving AP-35 entirely intact — which means developing leaders and then providing no feedback about whether the development is being applied. Both patterns must be addressed together for the leadership architecture to function."* See [AP-23/DP-23, The Professionalised Vocation](ap23-dp23-the-professionalised-vocation.md).
