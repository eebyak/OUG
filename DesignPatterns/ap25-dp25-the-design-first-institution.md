# Pattern Pair 25: Design Method: Purpose Before Feasibility

**The Constraint-First Institution → The Design-First Institution**

*Derived from: GP-1 — Design Before Constraint — "constraints should shape implementation, not purpose."*

[← Back to the pattern catalogue](index.md)

---

## AP-25 · Antipattern: The Constraint-First Institution

**Context — where this appears**
Any institution — or institutional reform process — in which the first and primary question is what is currently possible given existing funding, staffing, legal frameworks, and political conditions. Most common in mature institutions with long institutional histories, and in reform processes led by people who are simultaneously responsible for maintaining current operations.

**Symptom — what it looks like from inside**
Reform proposals are evaluated by whether they are feasible within current constraints before they are evaluated by whether they would serve the institution's purpose better. Design conversations begin with the budget and end with aspirations rather than beginning with needs and ending with implementation plans. When asked what the institution would build if it could design freely, participants immediately return to current constraints without being able to sustain the hypothetical. The institution's imagination is bounded by what it already is.

**Mechanism — the structural logic that produces it**
Constraints are real and visible. The cost of violating a constraint is immediate: a budget is exceeded, a regulation is breached, a political relationship is damaged. The cost of allowing constraints to define purpose is deferred and invisible: the institution never discovers what it could have become, and its reforms produce incrementally better versions of what it already was rather than structural responses to what its learners actually need. Rational actors in institutional settings will therefore always default to constraint-first thinking unless the design process explicitly protects against it.

**Consequence — what it costs the institution**
The institution consistently produces reforms that are feasible but insufficient. Each reform cycle improves existing arrangements at the margin rather than addressing the structural conditions that make the existing arrangements inadequate. Over time, the gap between what the institution offers and what its target population needs widens — not because the institution is failing to reform, but because its reform method is incapable of producing the structural distance from its own past that would be required to close the gap.

**Counter-design — what prevents this structurally**
The founding design conversation is constitutionally separated from the implementation conversation. Purpose is established before feasibility is assessed. The institution maintains a standing design forum — separate from operational governance — in which structural questions can be explored without immediate constraint filtering. Constraints are introduced in the second phase of any design process, as shapers of implementation, not as filters on purpose.

## DP-25 · Design Pattern: The Design-First Institution

*Structural inverse of: The Constraint-First Institution*

**Context — where this applies**
Any institution at a founding moment, or any institution undertaking a structural reform process, that wishes to produce designs capable of genuine structural distance from its own inherited arrangements.

**Intent — what this pattern achieves**
To protect the design conversation from premature constraint filtering, so that institutional purpose is established from learner need rather than from what current resources and political conditions make feasible — while ensuring that constraints are rigorously addressed in the implementation phase.

**Structure — what must be built**
The institution maintains a two-phase design process. Phase 1 — the Design Phase — is explicitly constraint-free: the question is what the institution should be, derived from what its target population needs, without filtering by what is currently possible. Phase 2 — the Implementation Phase — is explicitly constraint-engaged: the question is how the design established in Phase 1 can be approached given real resources, legal frameworks, and political conditions. The boundary between the phases is governed: participants who introduce constraints in Phase 1 are redirected, not silenced. Participants who avoid constraints in Phase 2 are challenged.

**Implementation — what this looks like in practice**
When a new programme is proposed, the first evaluation criterion is whether it serves the institution's defined purpose for its defined target population — not whether it can be staffed with current personnel. When a governance reform is proposed, the first question is whether it would produce the governance outcome the institution needs — not whether it is compatible with current committee structures. Constraints are documented separately as implementation challenges to be solved, not as reasons to reduce ambition. The founding documents specify which elements of the design are purpose commitments and which are implementation approaches — so that when constraints force changes to implementation, the purpose commitment remains visible and intact.

**Result — what the institution gains**
The institution can produce designs that are genuinely different from what it inherited. Implementation is constrained — it always is — but the design is not. Over time, the institution accumulates a record of what it intended and what constraints forced it to modify, which is itself a governance instrument: it makes the distance between aspiration and reality visible rather than allowing that distance to close silently through accumulated constraint-first thinking.

> **Note:** the OUG's own [Strategic Constitution](../strategy/strategic-constitution.md) implements this pattern directly — its Section 7, "Deliberate Non-Decisions and Open Design Space," is the same two-phase logic: bind purpose and principle constitutionally, leave governance bodies, organisational structure, and technology choices as open design space to be resolved later without weakening the constitutional commitments.
