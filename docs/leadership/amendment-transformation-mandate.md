## Amendment: Rector's Transformation Mandate — GP-5 Gap Closure

> **Purpose of this amendment.** The OUG Stress Test against GP-5 (Responsibility for Transformation Must Be Explicitly Assigned) found that the Rector's mandate includes institutional development but does not constitutionally protect transformation responsibility from operational absorption. Under operational pressure, transformation is the function most likely to be deprioritised. The Named Transformer pattern (DP-28) requires either a distinct transformation role or an explicit protection mechanism within the Rector's governance profile.
>
> This amendment does not create a separate Named Transformer role. Instead it specifies the protection mechanism within the Rector's existing mandate: the Transformation Reserve, the Transformation OKR set, and the Hochschulrat's annual transformation accountability review. Together these three mechanisms constitute a Named Transformer architecture within the Rector role — protecting transformation responsibility without requiring a separate appointment.

### IIIa. The Rector's Transformation Mandate

The Rector holds the primary institutional responsibility for structural transformation at the OUG. This responsibility is distinct from executive management — it is the mandate to drive the changes identified in the strategic plan that require structural rather than operational responses. The distinction matters because structural transformation and operational management compete for the same resource: the Rector's time, attention, and political capital. Without an explicit mechanism to protect the transformation mandate from being absorbed by operational demands, transformation will consistently be deprioritised.

**The Transformation Reserve.** The Rector's Proactive Capacity allocation (as specified in the Leadership and Stewardship Profiles) includes a minimum 20% designated as Transformation Reserve. The Transformation Reserve is time explicitly protected for transformation mandate activities — strategic design conversations, cross-institutional reform coordination, gap closure implementation, and the governance work that structural change requires. The Transformation Reserve is not available for operational escalations or administrative catch-up.

The Transformation Reserve allocation is reported quarterly to the Hochschulrat alongside the standard Proactive Capacity audit. If the Transformation Reserve has been materially consumed by operational demands in any quarter, the Rector reports this explicitly with an account of what consumed it and how the transformation mandate will be protected in the following quarter. The Hochschulrat may request a remediation plan if the Reserve falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters.

**The Transformation OKR Set.** In every OKR cycle, the Rector maintains a dedicated Transformation OKR set — a minimum of one Objective and two Key Results specifically addressing structural transformation priorities identified in the current strategic plan. Transformation OKRs are distinct from Operational OKRs: they address changes to governance structures, institutional capabilities, or delivery architecture, not improvements to existing operations.

Transformation OKRs are reviewed separately from Operational OKRs at the quarterly Hochschulrat meeting. The review asks three questions: is the transformation progressing at the intended pace, what structural obstacles have emerged, and what does the Rector need from G1 to maintain progress? This is not a performance review — it is a governance conversation about whether the institution is creating the conditions for its own transformation.

**The Annual Transformation Accountability Review.** At the Hochschulrat's annual strategic session, the Rector presents a Transformation Accountability Report: a structured account of the structural changes committed to in the current strategic period, the progress made in the past year, and an honest assessment of what has been achieved, what has been deferred, and why. The Report distinguishes between deferrals caused by operational pressure (a governance signal requiring discussion) and deferrals caused by external constraints (an implementation reality requiring revised timelines).

The Transformation Accountability Report is published alongside the Annual Academic Quality Report. It is part of the institutional record — available to Senate members, to partner institutions, and to the public. Publishing the transformation record is the accountability mechanism that makes the protection of transformation capacity visible rather than internal.

## Amendment C — New Governance Instrument: Incentive Conversion Statement

*Reason: GP-6: before any structural response to an exogenous incentive, leadership must convert it into an internal narrative*

*Reference from the Rector's Transformation Mandate (Section IIIa).*

The Incentive Conversion Statement is the governance instrument the Rector produces before any significant structural response to an exogenous incentive — a BMBF funding programme, an EU call, a policy mandate, a competitive mechanism, a reputational opportunity — is approved by the Hochschulrat.

This amendment originally specified the instrument in full as an Appendix B here. That specification has since been consolidated into a single, fuller standalone document to avoid two divergent copies of the same governance process: **[Incentive Conversion Statement](../governance/incentive-conversion-statement.md)**, in the Governance domain. That document is now the canonical source — the three questions it answers, the Conversion Outcome Note, and the full five-step governance process. This amendment's role is simply to record that GP-6 is what motivated the instrument's creation, tied specifically to the Rector's Transformation Mandate in Section IIIa above.
