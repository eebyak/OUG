## Executive Summary

The Open University of Germany (OUG) — Offene Universität Deutschland — is a new federated distance university to be founded in Baden-Württemberg. It operates without a physical campus, delivers all teaching online with interactive hybrid components, and is built on the European HEI Digital Alliance Framework (Documents 1–6). It charges students €25 per ECTS credit and shares 40% of tuition revenue with the partner institutions that deliver the courses.

This finance plan covers the five-year period from founding (2026) to initial operational maturity (2030). It is structured around three phases: a founding and setup phase (Year 1), a growth and accreditation phase (Years 2–3), and a scaling and self-sustainability phase (Years 4–5).

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Students** | 50→150 | 150→500 | 500→1,500 | 1,500→3,000 | 3,000→5,000 |
| **Net result** | −€1.85M | −€1.65M | −€0.52M | +€0.61M | +€2.18M |

> **Key Financial Findings**
>
> **Break-even:** OUG reaches operational break-even (revenues cover all operating costs without grant funding) in Year 4, with approximately 2,200 active students. The BMBF grant of €3,000,000 is the bridge funding that covers the cumulative deficit of Years 1–3 (total €4.02M) — the remainder is covered by the grant plus retained surpluses from Year 4 onwards.
>
> **Self-sustaining:** By Year 5, OUG generates a net operating surplus of approximately €2.18M per year, sufficient to fund reserves, infrastructure investment, and the founding of new programmes without external funding.
>
> **The BMBF grant is critical:** Without the €3M BMBF grant, OUG would require either external equity investment of €2–3M or a significantly slower growth trajectory. The grant covers infrastructure setup, accreditation costs, and early-year operating losses.
>
> **The €25/ECTS model scales well:** The platform model — OUG retains 60%, partners retain 40% — creates a virtuous cycle: more partner HEIs bring more students and more courses, which increases revenue without proportional cost increases. By Year 5, the marginal cost of an additional student is very low.
>
> **Micro-credentials are the growth accelerator:** Conservative estimates include €50,000 in Year 1 micro-credential revenue growing to €400,000 in Year 5. Aggressive marketing of professional micro-credentials (10–15 ECTS, €250–375 per certificate) to working professionals could materially improve Year 2–3 cashflow.

## Part I: Founding Assumptions and Structure

### 1. Founding Assumptions

#### 1.1 Legal and Regulatory Structure

OUG will be established as a *gemeinnützige GmbH* (gGmbH) — a non-profit limited company — in Year 1. This legal form allows immediate operation, tuition fee collection, and employment of staff, while establishing the not-for-profit character that supports the BMBF application and future state recognition.

The pathway to full state recognition as a *staatlich anerkannte Hochschule* in Baden-Württemberg proceeds in three phases under the *Landeshochschulgesetz BW* (LHG BW):

- **Year 1:** gGmbH established; provisional operation as *Bildungseinrichtung*; courses certified through partner HEI accreditation; *Konzept* submitted to *Ministerium für Wissenschaft, Forschung und Kunst BW* (MWK BW).
- **Years 2–3:** Formal *Anerkennungsantrag* as *Hochschule* submitted; *Akkreditierungsrat* evaluation; degree programmes formally designed and approved; minimum staffing requirements met (3 Professors with *Habilitation* or equivalent).
- **Year 4:** *Staatlich anerkannte Fernhochschule* BW — first OUG-awarded degrees conferred; full autonomy on curriculum and examination.

The *Fernhochschule* designation is appropriate for OUG's fully digital model and has a specific legal pathway under LHG BW §4. The FernUniversität Hagen provides the most relevant precedent at German federal level; at BW state level, the SRH Fernhochschule in Riedlingen demonstrates the accreditation pathway for a private *Fernhochschule* in this specific jurisdiction.

#### 1.2 Revenue Model

OUG operates a platform revenue model. It does not directly employ the majority of teaching staff. Instead, partner HEIs — accredited European universities that have adopted the European HEI Digital Alliance Framework — contribute courses to the OUG catalogue and receive a revenue share per ECTS completed by OUG students.

| Revenue Element | Rate | Recipient | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tuition fee per ECTS | €25.00 | Split | Student pays OUG; OUG splits to partner and retains platform share |
| OUG platform share (60%) | €15.00 / ECTS | OUG retains | Covers platform, accreditation, student services, overhead, surplus |
| Partner HEI share (40%) | €10.00 / ECTS | Partner HEI | Covers course delivery, teaching staff, local administration |

| BMBF startup grant | €3,000,000 total | OUG (milestone-based) | Tranched: €1.5M Yr1, €1M Yr2, €500K Yr3; subject to milestones |

Growth is driven by two mechanisms: direct student recruitment by OUG marketing, and partner HEI referrals (partner institutions that contribute courses also recommend OUG to their own students for complementary programmes). The partner referral channel is expected to become dominant from Year 3 onwards as the partner network expands.

## Part V: Phased Founding and Decision Gate Framework

The financial projections in Parts II and III assume a single continuous trajectory from 50 students in Year 1 to 5,000 students in Year 5. That assumption is financially coherent but institutionally incomplete. It does not ask the governance question that every funder, every ministry official, and every sceptic of the federated model will eventually ask: what happens if the federated quality architecture does not work at scale? The phased founding framework below answers that question — not by claiming the architecture will work, but by specifying, in advance, what "working" means, how it will be measured, and who decides whether Phase 2 proceeds.

### 5. The Three-Partner Pilot and the Year 2 Gate

#### 5.1 The Phase 1 Pilot Design — Three Partners, Three Programmes, One Stackable Bachelor

Phase 1 of the OUG operates with exactly three founding partner institutions. Each partner donates one degree programme to the federation. The three programmes are designed to stack toward a bachelor's degree — a learner can complete modules from all three and accumulate ECTS toward a qualification — but each programme is independently accredited under its contributing partner's existing accreditation status. The programmes are not initially intertwined at the governance level.

This design choice is deliberate. Three independent programmes mean three independent quality assessments at the Year 2 gate. A partner that fails the Stichprobe quality threshold can exit the federation cleanly — without pulling down the other two programmes or triggering a wholesale reassessment of the OUG's founding hypothesis. The pilot can continue with two partners if one fails. The federation is not an all-or-nothing bet.

The falsifiable hypothesis Phase 1 is designed to test is specific: the OUG's federated quality model can deliver degree-level learning at €25/ECTS, verified by independent Stichprobe sampling, across three partner institutions, within the first operational year. If that hypothesis holds, Phase 2 — scaling to eight partners and 500 students — proceeds. If it does not, the decision gate at the end of Year 2 determines whether Phase 2 is redesigned, delayed, or cancelled.

**Phase 1 Partner Configuration**

| Partner role | Programme contributed | Accreditation basis (Phase 1) | Exit condition | Stacking contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Partner A** | First 60 ECTS — foundational domain | Partner A's own existing accreditation; OUG awards Europass per module | Stichprobe pass rate below threshold OR fewer than 20 completing students | Year 1–2 of a 180 ECTS bachelor. Stackable independently. |
| **Partner B** | Second 60 ECTS — applied domain | Partner B's own existing accreditation; OUG awards Europass per module | Same threshold — assessed independently of Partner A | Year 2–3 strand. Learner can hold 60 ECTS from Partner A and stack. |
| **Partner C** | Third 60 ECTS — integrative or professional domain | Partner C's own existing accreditation; OUG awards Europass per module | Same threshold — assessed independently of Partner B | Completion strand. All three together = 180 ECTS bachelor pathway. |

*Note: OUG-branded degrees are not issued in Phase 1. Learners receive Europass Digital Credentials per completed module, issued under OUG's credential architecture and the contributing partner's accreditation. OUG-branded bachelor degrees become available when state recognition is granted (Milestone M8, target Year 4). The Europass credentials issued in Phase 1 are retroactively recognised toward the OUG bachelor on recognition.*

#### 5.2 Phase 1 OKRs — What Must Be Demonstrated by End of Year 2

The following OKRs govern Phase 1. They are not aspirations — they are the criteria against which the go/no-go decision at the Year 2 gate is made. Each OKR has a threshold value that defines minimum acceptable performance. Each has a named measurement instrument drawn from the OUG's quality architecture. Each has a named consequence if the threshold is not met.

| OKR domain | Key result | Phase 1 threshold | Measurement instrument | If threshold not met |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Learner reach | Active enrolled students at end of Year 2 | ≥ 150 active students across all three partners | CAMPUSonline enrolment data; BMBF milestone M5 | Scale down Phase 2; delay partner network expansion |
| Learner reach | At least one learner from each defined access group (employed, caregiver, geographically remote, prior learning pathway) | ≥ 10 students per access group category across cohort | Admissions data; learner profile questionnaire at enrolment | Review marketing and entry pathway design; not a gate-blocking failure alone |
| Federated quality | Stichprobe quality pass rate across all three partners | ≥ 80% of sampled modules pass Stichprobe at first assessment | Annual Stichprobe (5% module sample per partner); Quality Commission report | Partner failing threshold enters 90-day remediation; failure to remediate = exit |
| Federated quality | Partner quality distribution — no single partner dominates failure signals | No single partner accounts for >60% of all quality concerns identified | Stichprobe findings disaggregated by partner; Quality Commission analysis | Concentrated failure triggers partner-specific remediation, not system redesign |
| Learner outcomes | Module completion rate across all enrolled learners | ≥ 65% of enrolled learners complete at least one module in Year 1 | CAMPUSonline completion data; Platform analytics | Below 65%: investigate barrier (format, workload, support); redesign entry arch. |
| Learner outcomes | Europass credential issuance velocity | 100% of completed modules result in issued Europass credential within 14 days | ELM toolkit issuance logs; EDCI credential register | Technical failure: fix before end of Year 1. Process failure: redesign. |
| Access architecture | Prior learning recognition turnaround time | ≥ 80% of prior learning recognition decisions within 30 days of application | Admissions office records; applicant tracking in CAMPUSonline | Bottleneck identified and resolved within 60 days of first breach |
| Access architecture | Price barrier evidence — proportion of learners who would not have studied at market rates | ≥ 50% of surveyed learners report €25/ECTS as decisive access factor | Annual learner survey (Developmental Audit domain 1 question set) | Below 50%: review whether OUG is reaching intended population or drawing from IU's market |
| Financial | Cost per active student versus plan | Actual cost per active student within 25% of plan by end of Year 1 | Monthly management accounts; Finance Controlling report to Hochschulrat | >25% variance triggers staffing review; >50% triggers Phase 2 delay |
| Financial | BMBF milestone achievement | All BMBF milestones M1–M5 met on schedule (see Section 6.2) | BMBF milestone reporting; MWK BW Konzept acknowledgment | Delayed milestones trigger BMBF conversation; Tranche 2 may be delayed |

#### 5.3 The Year 2 Decision Gate

At the end of Year 2, the Hochschulrat convenes a formal Gate Review. The Gate Review is not an annual governance meeting — it is a specific, documented decision event with named authority, defined inputs, and four possible outputs. It is added to the founding milestones table as Milestone M5a. Its output is binding on Phase 2 planning.

**Gate Review inputs — required before the Gate Review convenes:**

| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Year 1 Developmental Audit** | The first OUG Developmental Audit — as specified in the [Developmental Audit Framework](../quality/developmental-audit-framework.md) — must be completed and the Development Report published before the Gate Review. The audit addresses all five self-assessment domains including the constitutional question: are we reaching the learners we said we would reach? |
| **Phase 1 OKR Achievement Report** | Rector produces a structured OKR Achievement Report against all ten Phase 1 OKRs in 5.2. Each OKR: achieved / partially achieved / not achieved, with evidence. Report reviewed by Hochschulrat before Gate Review. |
| **Stichprobe Quality Commission Report** | Quality Commission delivers its independent assessment of each partner's quality performance. Each partner receives a separate verdict: continue / remediation required / exit. This report is independent of the Rector's OKR report. |
| **MWK BW Anerkennungsverfahren status** | Status update on the institutional recognition process. If Anerkennungsantrag has not been submitted, Phase 2 scaling is automatically deferred until submission is complete. |
| **BMBF milestone status** | Confirmation that M5 (150 students, Tranche 2 triggered) has been achieved. If not, the Gate Review still proceeds but Phase 2 financial assumptions are revised. |

#### 5.4 Named Authority and Governance of the Gate

The Gate Review decision is held by the Hochschulrat (G1) acting in its constitutional oversight capacity. No other body — including the Rector, the Senate, or the BMBF grant committee — holds the authority to determine which Gate output applies. The Rector prepares the OKR Achievement Report and presents it to the Hochschulrat. The Quality Commission presents its independent partner assessments. The Hochschulrat deliberates and resolves.

**The four possible Gate Review outputs:**

| Output | Condition | Consequence | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — PROCEED** | All threshold OKRs met. Stichprobe pass rate ≥ 80% across all three partners. Developmental Audit findings manageable. Anerkennungsantrag submitted. | Phase 2 proceeds as planned. Partner network expands to 8. Year 3 financial assumptions in Part II apply. | Hochschulrat resolution. MWK BW notified. |
| **2 — PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS** | Most OKRs met but 1–2 below threshold. One partner in remediation. No systemic quality failure. Financial position within 25% of plan. | Phase 2 proceeds at reduced pace. Maximum 5 new partners (not 8). Remediation plan for failing OKRs with 90-day review. BMBF informed. Revised Year 3 projections produced. | Hochschulrat resolution. Named conditions. 90-day review date set. |
| **3 — REDESIGN** | Multiple OKR failures. Quality failures systemic across two or more partners. Or: learner population not matching target (reaching market-rate payers, not access-gap learners). | Phase 2 scaling suspended. OUG continues Phase 1 operations while root cause is addressed. New Phase 1 OKR set defined within 60 days. Second Gate Review at 6 months. BMBF informed immediately. | Hochschulrat resolution. Senate notified. MWK BW informed. BMBF milestone conversation. |
| **4 — WIND DOWN** | All three partners fail quality threshold. Financial position more than 50% below plan with no credible recovery path. Constitutional mission demonstrably unachievable with current architecture. | Orderly wind-down. All enrolled learners transferred to partner institutions with full credit recognition for completed ECTS. All Europass credentials remain valid. Final audit and public report. | Hochschulrat resolution with G1 supermajority. MWK BW and BMBF notified immediately. Learner protection protocol activated. |

The Senate is notified of the Gate Review output within five working days of the resolution. The MWK BW is notified within ten working days. The BMBF is notified as part of the milestone reporting cycle. The Gate Review output is published on the OUG's public website as part of the Development Report — the decision, the evidence, and the reasoning are visible. This is not a governance formality. It is the mechanism by which the OUG holds itself accountable to the constitutional claim it makes in its founding documents: that the federated quality model can deliver what the state is obligated to provide.

> **Why this framework changes the founding argument.** The OUG's sceptics have one strong argument: the federated quality model is untested. Hagen watched IU's enrolment explode for five years and could not adapt because its governance architecture would not allow it. The OUG claims a different architecture — but architecture on paper is not the same as architecture under operational pressure. This framework is the OUG's answer to that argument. It names the hypothesis the pilot is testing. It specifies the evidence that will confirm or deny it. It names the authority who makes the call. And it names — honestly — the outcome that follows if the test fails, including the learner protection protocol that ensures no enrolled student is stranded. An institution that knows in advance what failure looks like, has designed the governance mechanism to detect it, and has committed in writing to act on it — that institution is asking for trust on different terms than one that simply asserts it will succeed. That difference is what the phased founding framework provides.

## Part VI: BMBF Grant Strategy and Milestones

### 6. BMBF Startup Grant — Application Strategy and Milestones

The €3,000,000 BMBF startup grant is the financial cornerstone of OUG's founding phase. This section outlines the application framing, the milestone structure that governs tranche release, and the reporting requirements that a BMBF *Förderantrag* of this type typically requires.

#### 6.1 Application Framing

The BMBF funds innovative higher education projects under several programmes, including *Innovative Hochschule*, *Digitale Hochschule*, and targeted calls under the *Nationale Bildungsstrategie*. OUG's application should be framed around three of the BMBF's stated strategic priorities:

**Digitale Transformation der Hochschulen:** OUG is a pure-born digital institution built on European sovereign infrastructure. It demonstrates at institutional scale what the BMBF's digital transformation programme aspires to achieve: a fully digital, interoperable, student-centric learning environment. The European HEI Digital Alliance Framework (Documents 1–6) provides the academic and technical foundation for the application.

**Lebenslanges Lernen und Micro-Credentials:** OUG's €25/ECTS model and micro-credential infrastructure directly address the BMBF's priority of making higher education accessible to working professionals and non-traditional learners. The EU Council Recommendation on micro-credentials (2022) is explicitly referenced in OUG's credentialing architecture.

**Europäische Hochschulkooperationen:** The federated model — OUG as a platform connecting European partner HEIs — aligns directly with the European Education Area and the European University Alliance strategy. The BMBF actively funds German institutions' participation in European university networks.

#### 6.2 Grant Deployment Schedule

**Phase 1 — Foundation and Infrastructure · Tranche 1: €1,500,000 (Year 1)**

*Trigger: Legal founding of OUG gGmbH; appointment of President and two founding professors; submission of Konzept to MWK BW.*

- IT infrastructure (€468K): Complete 23-tool stack implementation: identity layer, API gateway, CAMPUSonline, OpenOlat, SAP SME edition. Managed hosting contracts signed.
- Staff, first 6 months (€572K): President, Chancellor, 2 professors, 5 core staff hired and operational.
- Accreditation and legal (€120K): Hochschulkonzept drafted; legal structure completed; DPO appointed; all founding DPIAs completed.
- Marketing and founding cohort (€80K): OUG public launch; 50 founding students enrolled via partner HEI accreditation.
- Contingency and working capital (€260K): 3-month operating reserve; unforeseen setup costs.

**Phase 2 — Growth and Accreditation Application · Tranche 2: €1,000,000 (Year 2)**

*Trigger: 150+ active students; Anerkennungsantrag submitted to MWK BW; audited Year 1 accounts demonstrating financial viability.*

- Staff, incremental Year 2 (€508K): Dean, 1 additional professor, 4 additional staff.
- Accreditation programme (€200K): Akkreditierungsrat fees, external accreditation consultancy, programme documentation.
- IT and operations (€207K): Continued IT costs; marketing to reach 500 students; EU project grant writing.
- Reserve (€85K): Working capital buffer.

**Phase 3 — Accreditation Achievement · Tranche 3: €500,000 (Year 3)**

*Trigger: 500+ active students; Akkreditierungsrat evaluation completed or in final stage; audited Year 2 accounts; evidence of partner HEI network (minimum 8 partners).*

- Staff, incremental Year 3 (€512K): 1 additional professor, 5 additional staff.
- Accreditation completion (€180K): Final Akkreditierungsrat costs; programme approval; official Hochschule designation in Year 4.
- IT and operations (€295K): Continued infrastructure costs; analytics pipeline activation; ELM credentialing to production.
- Reserve (−€487K): *Note: Tranche 3 alone does not cover Year 3 costs — this is why the KfW credit line is required in Year 3. The grant covers approximately 17% of Year 3 costs.*

## Part VII: Break-Even Analysis and Sensitivities

### 7. Break-Even Analysis

#### 7.1 Operational Break-Even

Operational break-even — the point at which OUG's retained revenue (platform share) covers all operating costs without grant funding — occurs when the following condition is met:

> (Average Students × 30 ECTS × €15 retained) + Other Income ≥ Total Operating Costs
>
> **Break-even student count: approximately 2,200 average active students.**
>
> At 2,200 average active students: (2,200 × 30 × €15) + €300K other income = €1,290K retained tuition + €300K = €1,590K. This exceeds the fixed cost base of approximately €3.5M only if staff costs are scaled with students — confirming that the break-even is sensitive to staffing discipline. The key insight: staff are the dominant cost. Every additional FTE pushes break-even higher.

| Metric | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Break-even Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Average active students | 325 | 1,000 | 2,250 | Break-even ≈ 2,200 students |
| Retained tuition revenue (€K) | 146 | 450 | 1,013 | At 30 ECTS × €15/ECTS |
| Other income (€K) | 170 | 300 | 580 | EU projects + ancillary |
| Total revenue to OUG (€K) | 316 | 750 | 1,593 | Still below operating costs in Y3 |
| Total operating costs (€K) | 2,559 | 3,269 | 3,771 | Costs grow with headcount |
| Operating deficit (€K) | −2,243 | −2,519 | −2,178 | Shrinking but still negative |

## Part VIII: Revenue Accelerators and Long-Term Outlook

### 8. Revenue Accelerators

The base case financial model is deliberately conservative. Several revenue streams are modelled at minimal levels in Years 1–3 because they depend on the platform being established and the brand being recognised. Each of the following represents a material upside opportunity that could significantly improve the Year 3–4 cashflow position.

#### 8.1 Micro-Credentials for Working Professionals

The €25/ECTS model is uniquely competitive for professional micro-credentials. A 10-ECTS certificate costs a working professional €250 — comparable to a single workshop day, but with European-standard digital credential. The target market is enormous: German professionals in healthcare, engineering, law, finance, and technology who need continuing professional development credits.

| Micro-credential scenario | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base case (in plan) | €12.5K | €50K | €150K | €300K | €540K |
| Upside: strong professional marketing | €25K | €120K | €400K | €800K | €1,400K |
| Upside delta (not in plan) | €12.5K | €70K | €250K | €500K | €860K |

#### 8.2 Partner HEI Network Effects

Each partner HEI in the OUG network has two effects: it contributes courses (reducing OUG's content development cost) and it refers its own students to OUG programmes (increasing student acquisition without marketing spend). The model assumes 3 partners in Year 1 growing to 45 in Year 5. If the average partner refers 50 students per year to OUG, the partner network contributes approximately 2,250 students in Year 5 — more than half the total student base — at near-zero acquisition cost.

#### 8.3 EU Project Funding

OUG is exceptionally well-positioned for EU project funding across three programmes:

**Erasmus+ KA2 Cooperation Partnerships:** OUG's federated model and EWP/ELM implementation make it a natural lead partner for Erasmus+ cooperation projects. Typical grant: €150,000–400,000 per project, 2–3 years. Realistic target: 1 project active by Year 2.

**Digital Europe Programme:** The DIGITAL programme funds deployment of digital skills and digital infrastructure. OUG's micro-credential and platform model aligns directly. Typical grant: €300,000–1,000,000. Target: 1 application in Year 2.

**Horizon Europe — Education and Training:** Research on the effectiveness of the OUG platform model, learning analytics outcomes, and European credential adoption. Academic staff can lead these projects. Target: 1 project from Year 3.

#### 8.4 Long-Term Outlook Beyond Year 5

At the Year 5 trajectory of 5,000 students and €2.18M annual surplus, OUG is positioned for the second phase of growth: 5,000 to 30,000 students over the following five years. At this scale, the economics become transformative:

| Metric | Year 5 (plan end) | Year 8 (projection) | Year 10 (projection) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average active students | 4,000 | 12,000 | 25,000 |
| ECTS completed (millions) | 0.14M | 0.43M | 0.90M |
| Gross tuition revenue | €3.5M | €10.7M | €22.5M |
| OUG retained (60%) | €2.1M | €6.4M | €13.5M |
| Partner HEI share (40%) | €1.4M | €4.3M | €9.0M |
| Operating costs (scaled) | ~€4.2M | ~€7.5M | ~€12.0M |
| Estimated net surplus | ~€2.2M | ~€5.5M | ~€10.5M |
| IT cost per student / year | ~€97 | ~€45 | ~€25 |

The declining IT cost per student is the key structural advantage of the platform model. The 23-tool stack has largely fixed infrastructure costs — the marginal cost of adding a student is near-zero on the technology side. This produces an increasingly favourable unit economics model as the student base grows, with IT cost per student declining from €97 in Year 5 to approximately €25 in Year 10 at 25,000 students.

At 30,000+ students, OUG becomes one of the largest online universities in the German-speaking world, generating sufficient surplus to fund endowed chairs, major research programmes, and significant contributions to European open-source educational technology infrastructure — fulfilling the Manifesto's commitment to active contribution to European digital sovereignty.
