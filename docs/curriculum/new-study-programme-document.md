## I. Programme Rationale

Computer Science in the Digital Health context is one of the most consequential fields for Germany's near-term future. The healthcare sector is undergoing a digital transformation of fundamental scope — from electronic patient records and clinical decision support systems to AI-driven diagnostics and distributed health data infrastructures. This transformation requires professionals who combine deep technical competence with an understanding of the human, ethical, and organisational dimensions of health systems.

The New Study programme was developed at DHBW in response to exactly this need. It is a rigorously designed, competency-based Bachelor's programme that integrates foundational computer science theory with practical software construction, data and AI skills, and explicit attention to the societal and professional dimensions of computing. The OUG adopts this programme without modification of its academic content, under a partnership arrangement that allows OUG-enrolled students to attend DHBW classes while receiving their degree from OUG.

> The adoption model is legally and academically clean: DHBW delivers the teaching, OUG awards the degree, and the quality of both is governed by their respective accreditation frameworks. OUG students are governed by OUG rules — its Prüfungsordnung, its student rights charter, its modular credit system — while accessing DHBW's established teaching infrastructure.

What makes this programme architecturally significant for the OUG's accreditation case is not just its content — it is its design. New Study was built from the beginning on a modular, stackable principle: every module is 5 ECTS, self-contained, and assessable as a standalone micro-credential. Students who complete a cluster of related modules receive a stack certificate. When they have accumulated sufficient stacks across streams, the door to the Bachelor Thesis and Capstone opens. The degree is earned by accumulation — a genuinely novel pathway structure that is simultaneously more accessible and more rigorous than a traditional fixed-cohort programme.

## II. The Stacking Model: From Micro-credential to Bachelor

The New Study programme is built on a stacking principle that is structurally distinct from traditional degree programmes and represents the OUG's approach to lifelong learning made concrete. Understanding this model is essential for accreditation reviewers assessing how the programme functions.

### The Three Levels of the Stack

**Level 1 — Module / Micro-credential.** Every module is 5 ECTS. Every completed module earns a micro-credential — a digitally signed, Europass-compatible certificate documenting the specific competencies acquired. Micro-credentials are portable: they can be submitted to employers, recognised by other institutions, or aggregated toward higher qualifications. Each module has defined learning outcomes mapped to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF) and to ESCO skill taxonomies.

**Level 2 — Stream Stack.** Each of the six streams in the programme constitutes a coherent Stack — a cluster of related modules that together develop a specific competency profile. A student who completes all modules in the Data, AI & Intelligent Systems stream, for example, receives an AI Stack certificate, even if they have not yet completed modules from other streams. Stacks are valuable standalone qualifications for professionals who need deep expertise in one domain.

**Level 3 — Bachelor Degree.** When a student has accumulated modules across streams meeting the threshold requirements (see Section III), has completed the Capstone Project, and has submitted and passed the Bachelor Thesis, they are awarded the B.Sc. Computer Science (Digital Health Hub) by the OUG. The degree certifies not just disciplinary depth but interdisciplinary integration — the combination of streams is what produces a computer scientist, not any single stream alone.

### Europass Integration

Every module completion at OUG triggers the issuance of a European Digital Credential (EDC) through Europass. The credential contains: the module title and code, the ECTS value, the specific learning outcomes achieved, the assessment method and grade, the issuing institution (OUG), and the date of award. Credentials are issued to the student's Europass wallet and are machine-readable, tamper-proof, and verifiable by any employer or institution in the EU.

This integration means that OUG students build a verifiable, portable record of their competencies throughout their studies — not only at the end. A student who completes ten modules and then pauses their studies has ten documented credentials. A professional who takes three modules for upskilling purposes has three credentials that go directly into their professional record. The degree, when awarded, is an EDC that aggregates all previous credentials.

## III. Degree Requirements and the Threshold Principle

The New Study programme does not follow a fixed module sequence. Students choose their entry points, pace, and progression through streams based on their prior knowledge, professional context, and learning goals. The Threshold Principle defines the conditions under which the Bachelor pathway — Capstone and Thesis — becomes accessible.

| Stage | Requirement | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Completion of any module with no formal prerequisites. No minimum qualification required beyond standard university entrance (Hochschulzugangsberechtigung or equivalent). Prior learning recognition available. | Module micro-credential issued; ECTS accrued; Europass credential published. |
| Stack Completion | All modules within a stream completed. Stream-specific competency thresholds met across the three learning levels (L1: Knowledge, L2: Application, L3: Synthesis). | Stream Stack Certificate issued. Counts toward degree threshold. |
| Threshold for Capstone | Minimum 120 ECTS completed across at least 4 streams, including at least one module from each of: Mathematical & Algorithmic Foundations, Programming & Software Construction, and Research, Professional & Societal Practice. | Eligibility for Capstone Project registration unlocked. |
| Capstone Project | 10 ECTS project demonstrating integrative application of competencies across streams. Evaluated by multidisciplinary panel. Minimum grade: sufficient (ausreichend, 4.0). | Capstone Certificate issued. Thesis eligibility unlocked. |
| Threshold for Thesis | All 150 ECTS stream modules completed (or equivalent through prior learning recognition). Capstone passed. | Thesis registration open. |
| Bachelor Thesis | 10 ECTS original scholarly work. Supervised by OUG-affiliated academic. Examined by two examiners. Minimum grade: sufficient (4.0). | B.Sc. Computer Science (Digital Health Hub) awarded. European Digital Credential issued. Europass Diploma Supplement generated. |

### Prior Learning Recognition

The modular, threshold-based structure of New Study is explicitly designed to accommodate prior learning. Professionals who have developed substantial competencies through work experience, other educational programmes, or independent study may apply for credit recognition through the OUG's Assessment & Recognition Committee. Recognition is competency-based, not credential-based: the question is whether the student can demonstrate the learning outcomes of the module, not whether they hold a certificate.

This provision is critical to the OUG's mission. A software engineer with five years of professional experience who has never studied computer science formally may already hold substantial competency across several streams. Recognition allows them to enter the programme at the level their competence warrants, rather than repeating material they already know.

## VI. Competency Architecture

The New Study programme is competency-based in a technically precise sense. Each module maps its learning outcomes to a defined set of competency domains, and the programme as a whole is designed so that different stream combinations produce different but coherent graduate profiles. This is not a traditional elective system — it is a deliberate architecture for differentiated competency development.

### Competency Domains

The programme uses eleven competency domains mapped to ESCO skill taxonomies and EQF descriptors:

| Code | Domain | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| PROG | Programming & Software Development | Core coding, software construction, version control, development environments |
| SOFT | Software Engineering & Architecture | Design patterns, requirements engineering, software quality, architecture |
| NETW | Networks, Systems & Infrastructure | OS, networking, cloud, distributed systems, infrastructure security |
| DATA | Data Engineering & Analytics | Databases, pipelines, data modelling, analytics, visualisation |
| AI | Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning | ML algorithms, deep learning, NLP, AI systems design |
| METH | Methods & Scientific Practice | Research methods, modelling, experimentation, formal methods |
| ETHI | Ethics, Law & Professional Responsibility | AI ethics, data protection, professional standards, legal frameworks |
| UXHI | UX, HCI & Design | Human-centred design, accessibility, interaction design, usability |
| COMM | Communication & Collaboration | Technical writing, presentation, teamwork, professional communication |
| RESE | Research & Innovation | Scientific enquiry, literature review, knowledge transfer, applied research |
| PERS | Personal & Professional Development | Self-regulation, learning strategies, career development |

### Three Graduate Profiles

The stream combination a student completes shapes their graduate profile. Three primary profiles are identified for accreditation purposes; these are not mutually exclusive, and students whose stream choices cross these profiles develop a correspondingly interdisciplinary competency set.

- **Programming & Systems Profile.** Strong in PROG, SOFT, NETW. Stream emphasis on Programming & Software Construction and Systems, Platforms & Security. Graduate role: software engineer, systems architect, DevOps engineer, security specialist.
- **Data & AI Profile.** Strong in DATA, AI, METH. Stream emphasis on Data, AI & Intelligent Systems and Mathematical & Algorithmic Foundations. Graduate role: data scientist, ML engineer, AI systems designer, health data analyst.
- **Human-Centred & Research Profile.** Strong in UXHI, ETHI, RESE, COMM. Stream emphasis on Human-Centred & Societal Computing and Research, Professional & Societal Practice. Graduate role: UX researcher, digitalisation manager, applied researcher, technology ethicist.

## VII. OUG Governance of the Adopted Programme

The adoption of the New Study programme from DHBW is not a licence arrangement or a sub-contracting agreement. It is a specific instance of the OUG's federated model in which DHBW acts as a Type B Programme Partner: a partner institution contributing sustained teaching to a defined OUG programme, with all associated governance rights.

### What DHBW Provides

- Teaching delivery — lectures, seminars, laboratory sessions — for all modules in the curriculum.
- Teaching staff — qualified academic contributors with subject expertise meeting OUG's academic contributor requirements.
- Physical and digital infrastructure — lecture spaces, laboratory facilities, and digital learning platforms where used.
- Academic expertise in curriculum maintenance — subject knowledge for keeping module content current.

### What the OUG Governs

- **Degree award** — the B.Sc. Computer Science (Digital Health Hub) is awarded by OUG, not by DHBW. The credential belongs to OUG.
- **Academic standards** — programme learning outcomes, assessment criteria, examination standards, and grading scales are governed by OUG's G3 Academic Governance layer.
- **Student enrolment and rights** — OUG students are enrolled with OUG. Their student rights, complaints procedures, and academic records are governed by OUG's Prüfungsordnung and Student Charter.
- **Quality assurance** — programme review, external examination, and quality monitoring are conducted by OUG's Quality Assurance and Academic Governance Framework.
- **Micro-credential issuance** — Europass credentials for each module are issued by OUG, not by DHBW.

### The 80% Learning Outcome Principle

The adoption is formally governed by the requirement that more than 80% of the programme's learning outcomes are shared between the DHBW New Study programme and the OUG version. This threshold ensures that the OUG programme is genuinely the same programme in academic substance, while allowing OUG to add its own governance requirements, its Europass credential architecture, and its modular stacking framework on top of an academically equivalent foundation.

The remaining capacity (less than 20%) is used for OUG-specific additions: the formal Europass credential issuing process, the Prior Learning Recognition procedure integrated into every module, and the competency mapping to ESCO taxonomies that enables skills-based transparency for employers and learners.

> This programme demonstrates something that policy has debated for years and the OUG makes concrete: a modular, stackable, Europass-integrated Bachelor's degree in which every module is a standalone credential, every stack is a recognisable qualification, and the full degree is both academically rigorous and genuinely accessible to working adults. This is not an experiment. It is operational.
