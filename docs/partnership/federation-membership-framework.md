## Part One — Federation & Membership Framework

### § 1 Purpose and Scope

The Open University of Germany (OUG) is a state Hochschule under the LHG (Grundordnung § 1), operating a federated model in which partner institutions contribute teaching capacity within OUG-approved academic frameworks (Federated Legal Model § 4). This framework defines: the categories of participation available to partner institutions, individual academics, learners, alumni, and societal actors; the rights and duties attached to each; and the specific governance mechanisms — already established elsewhere in this repository — through which each category actually participates.

**This is a correction of scope from the previous version.** The source document this framework was migrated from described OUG as a membership federation in which institutions hold voting rights and nominate organ representatives directly. That model does not describe a state Hochschule under the LHG — OUG's organs (Rektorat, Senat, Hochschulrat) have LHG-fixed composition (Grundordnung §§ 3, 5, 6), and institutions, as such, cannot hold seats in them. This framework now describes participation as it actually works: individually earned for academics, structurally designed for institutions and society, never allocated to an institution as a bloc vote.

### § 2 Principles of Federation

1. **Academic Authority with Delegated Delivery.** OUG holds academic authority for its degrees, curricula, and examination regulations (Grundordnung § 5 Absatz 1; Federated Legal Model § 5). Teaching delivery may be delegated to accredited partner institutions.
2. **Institutional Participation, Individually Exercised.** A partner institution's participation is structural — access to the credential architecture, a seat in the Programme Development Forum, a voice in External Cooperation Councils — but **governance voting rights, where they exist at all, attach to individuals, not institutions**, and only through the specific mechanisms in § 6 below.
3. **Weight of Voice Tracks Seriousness of Engagement, Not Employment Status.** **This corrects the previous version's "Parity of Academic Voice... not determined by employment status" principle**, which was too flat. Participation weight is not determined by *where* someone is employed, but it is legitimately shaped by *how* they are engaged — a Lehrauftrag-based guest contributor and an academic released under a formal Kooperationsvereinbarung do not hold identical standing, and should not (see § 6).
4. **Agility through Federation.** The federation enables partner institutions to extend and adapt educational offerings without structural duplication or legal fragmentation.
5. **Transparency of Rights and Duties.** Participation in OUG governance implies defined responsibilities, accountability, and contribution to shared academic standards.

### § 3 Categories of Participation

**§ 3.1 Partner Institutions.** Accredited higher education institutions that enter into a formal Partnership Agreement (Kooperationsvertrag) with OUG and contribute teaching provision to OUG programmes. Rights and duties are specified in the [Partner Membership Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/partnership/partner-membership-framework.md) — this document does not restate them. Partner institutions do not hold votes in any OUG organ, regardless of the scale or duration of their contribution; their structural participation runs through the Programme Development Forum, External Cooperation Councils, and — where relevant to a specific Mission Hub's domain — the [Mission Hub Advisory Panel](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/mission-hub-advisory-panels.md).

**§ 3.2 Individual Academics.** Academic staff contributing to OUG programmes participate through one of three engagement models, each carrying different governance standing — specified in full in the [Federated Legal Model](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/federated-legal-model.md) §§ 7–8 and not restated here. In summary: Lehrauftrag-based contributors hold advisory standing (Community Circle, Programme Development Forum); academics engaged under a Freistellungsvereinbarung hold the full active-and-passive Wahlrecht already granted by Grundordnung § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3, subject to the open § 10 Absatz 1 LHG question flagged in that document; OUG-employed academics hold full, uncontested membership.

**§ 3.3 Learners.** Two distinct categories, not restated in full here but distinguished precisely because conflating them was the exact error being corrected:

- *Enrolled degree students* — governed by the Grundordnung's Studierendenschaft provisions (§ 11), forming the Verfasste Studierendenschaft under § 65 LHG. Only immatrikulierte students hold this specific statutory standing.
- *Non-degree lifelong learners* — participants in stackable, non-degree learning units, legally grounded in **Kontaktstudium** under § 31 LHG, which sits explicitly outside the Studiengang rules (§ 31 Absatz 5 Satz 2), and results in a Zertifikat on completion of an Abschlussprüfung — the statutory basis for a "badge" that marks a milestone without implying a finished degree. Kontaktstudium participants are not, and under § 65 LHG cannot be, members of the Verfasste Studierendenschaft; this is a distinction of legal category, not a lesser form of participation. Their standing in OUG's own community runs through § 3.4 below.

**§ 3.4 Alumni and Lifelong Learners.** Individuals who have completed an OUG degree or OUG-certified learning unit — degree graduates and Kontaktstudium/non-degree completers together, since both are, in the sense this framework cares about, still learners rather than departed former members. **This is a genuinely useful category the previous version of this document introduced, and it has no home in the Grundordnung** — appropriately, since it is not a governance-voting category under the LHG (§ 65 confines the Verfasste Studierendenschaft to immatrikulierte students specifically). Alumni and lifelong learner participation runs through the Learner and Alumni-for-Life Communities channel (Governance Architecture § 4.3), explicitly distinct from the formal constitutional standing of the Verfasste Studierendenschaft, and — where relevant to a specific Mission Hub's mission domain — through that Hub's Advisory Panel, where an alumnus working in the field is a natural, valuable panel member. This is also, not incidentally, the same statutory provision (§ 31 Absatz 5 LHG) that permits Kontaktstudium delivery in cooperation with institutions outside the higher-education sector entirely — see the [Presence Architecture](../curriculum/presence-architecture.md) for where that possibility is developed.

**§ 3.5 Societal and External Actors.** Municipalities, regions, industry, professional organisations, and civil society. Participation runs through External Cooperation Councils (institution-wide, annual — Governance Architecture § 4.3) or a Mission Hub Advisory Panel (Hub-specific, annual, Duty to Voice — see that document). **This corrects the previous version's "External Partners" category**, which described the same actors without connecting them to either of these two mechanisms, since neither existed yet when that section was drafted.

### § 4 Rights and Duties of Partner Institutions

Not restated here — governed in full by the [Partner Membership Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/partnership/partner-membership-framework.md) (Sections 2–3, rights and duties; Section 5, Task Architecture). **This corrects the previous version's independent rights/duties list**, which duplicated and in places contradicted the Partner Membership Framework's more precise and more recently converted specification — for example, the previous version described "co-award or parallel-award degrees where contractually agreed" as a standing partner right; the actual governing rule is the review-triggered, non-automatic threshold in [Degree and Examination Authority](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/degree-examination-authority.md) §§ 9–10.

### § 5 Academic Leadership

**This corrects the previous version's claim that OUG academic leadership roles "may be held by individuals employed at member institutions" and are "elected by the Senate."** Academic leadership — Rektor, Prorektoren, Kanzler/in, Stewards of Missions — is governed exclusively by Grundordnung §§ 3–4 (Rektorat, including the joint Hochschulrat–Senat Findungskommission process) and § 8 (Mission Hub Stewards, elected from hauptberuflich professors only). Two points from the previous version do not survive this correction:

1. **A Steward of Missions cannot be a co-opted (kooptiert) academic from a partner institution "as such"** — this is a hard statutory floor (§ 22 Absatz 4 Satz 3 LHG: *"Ein kooptiertes Mitglied kann als solches nicht zur Dekanin oder zum Dekan bestellt werden"*), not a policy choice this framework could waive even if it wanted to.
2. **The Rector is not elected by the Senate alone.** The process is the joint Hochschulrat–Senat Findungskommission and joint election specified in Grundordnung § 4.

### § 6 Financial Principles

Funding flows within the federation follow: student-based public funding and tuition are routed to OUG for OUG-enrolled students; OUG retains a defined overhead share for governance, quality assurance, and infrastructure; the majority of funds are transferred to teaching-providing partner institutions; allocation is based on transparent, published criteria (ECTS delivered, student numbers). **Final authority over this model rests with the Rektorat (G2), execution and legality validation with the Kanzlerin/Kanzler (G4), and Senate consent is required specifically and only where a financial decision would materially affect academic standards or programme viability** — per Federated Legal Model § 11(4) and Governance Architecture § 3.7, not restated in further detail here.

### § 7 Relationship to Further Regulations

This framework is complemented by the Grundordnung, the Governance & Decision Framework, the Federated Legal Model, the Prüfungsordnung, the Partner Membership Framework, and individual Provider Agreements. Where this framework and the Grundordnung conflict on any matter of organs, elections, or terms, the Grundordnung prevails without exception — this framework has no independent authority to define those matters.

### § 8 Entry into Force and Amendments

This framework enters into force upon adoption by the competent OUG organ. **Amendments follow the [Amendment Procedure](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/amendment-procedure.md)** — this corrects the previous version, which specified its own qualified-Senate-majority amendment process, duplicating a procedure this repository already defines centrally.

## Part Two — Constituent Groups and Participation Map

**This Part replaces the previous "Statute on Organs, Participation, and Elections."** That statute independently defined OUG's organs, their composition, election procedures, and terms of office — all of which the Grundordnung already defines, and in several places defined them *differently* from the Grundordnung (institutional and alumni Senate seats that don't exist in law; a 4-year Rector term against the Grundordnung's 6–8 years; 2-year Senate terms against 4 years; Deans "employed by member institutions," which the LHG's co-optation restriction forecloses). Duplicating a statute invites exactly this kind of drift. What follows is only what that statute contained that is **not** already owned by the Grundordnung or the governance documents.

### § 9 Constituent Groups (Non-Statutory Taxonomy)

This taxonomy names who participates in OUG's academic life. It is descriptive, not a source of voting rights — voting rights arise only where the Grundordnung or the Federated Legal Model's § 7–8 tiering grants them.

| Group | Description | Actual participation mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Academic Staff (OUG-employed) | Hauptberuflich professors and staff | Full Grundordnung membership and Wahlrecht |
| Academic Staff (Kooperationsvereinbarung / Freistellung) | Federation academics under Model B | Federated Legal Model § 8(5)–(6): full Wahlrecht per Grundordnung § 12 Absatz 1 Nr. 3, subject to the flagged § 10 Absatz 1 open question |
| Academic Staff (Lehrauftrag) | Federation academics under Model A | Community Circle advisory voice, Programme Development Forum |
| Learners | Enrolled students | Verfasste Studierendenschaft (Grundordnung § 11); Senate student seats (§ 5 Absatz 6 Nr. 3) |
| Alumni and Lifelong Learners | Graduates and completers of OUG learning units | Learner and Alumni-for-Life Communities (Governance Architecture § 4.3); Mission Hub Advisory Panels where domain-relevant |
| Partner Institutions | Accredited teaching-contributing institutions | Programme Development Forum; External Cooperation Councils; Mission Hub Advisory Panels |
| Societal and External Actors | Municipalities, industry, civil society | External Cooperation Councils (institution-wide, annual); Mission Hub Advisory Panels (Hub-specific, annual); Hochschulrat's five external seats (institution-wide, statutory, G1) |

### § 10 Research Groups

**Retained from the previous version — this is a genuinely additive concept not duplicated elsewhere in this repository.** Within each Mission Hub, Research Groups may be established as thematic, voluntary, cross-institutional collaborative units: formed by academic staff affiliated with partner institutions, operating across institutional boundaries, aligned with the hosting Mission Hub's mission scope. Membership is open and voluntary, subject to academic relevance and capacity. Research Groups are recognised by the Mission Hub's Steward of Missions, report periodically to the Hub, do not constitute independent legal entities, and do not exercise examination or degree-awarding authority. This is distinct from — and, based on the available descriptions, not the same structure as — the Research Cluster model referenced elsewhere in OUG's research architecture; reconciling the two, if reconciliation is needed, is flagged as an open item rather than resolved here.

### § 11 Conflicts and Interpretation

Conflicts arising within or between constituent groups, or between OUG and partner institutions, are routed through the [Conflict Escalation Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/conflict-escalation-framework.md) — **this corrects the previous version's independent three-stage conflict resolution process** (informal resolution, Rectorate mediation, Senate decision), which predates and duplicates the six-route framework this repository now uses consistently.

### § 12 Amendment

Governed by the [Amendment Procedure](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/amendment-procedure.md) — not restated here.
