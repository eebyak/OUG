## Part One: Federation & Membership Framework

### 1. Purpose and Scope

The Open University of Germany (OUG) is constituted as a federated degree-awarding institution. Its academic mission, governance, and quality assurance are realised through the structured participation of member universities and associated institutions.

This framework defines: the categories of membership within OUG; the rights and duties associated with each category; the principles of participation in OUG governance structures; and the mechanisms by which academic authority, teaching provision, and institutional responsibility are distributed.

This framework forms the basis for all subsequent regulations concerning governance organs, elections and appointments, committee composition, and financial and academic delegation.

### 2. Principles of Federation

OUG is founded on the following principles:

1. **Academic Authority with Delegated Delivery.** OUG holds academic authority for its degrees, curricula, and examination regulations. Teaching delivery may be delegated to accredited member institutions.
2. **Institutional Participation over Individual Ownership.** Membership in OUG is institutional. Individual academic participation is derived from institutional membership, not direct employment by OUG.
3. **Parity of Academic Voice.** Academic participation in OUG governance is not determined by employment status, but by delegated responsibility and elected stewardship.
4. **Agility through Federation.** The federation enables member institutions to extend and adapt educational offerings without structural duplication or legal fragmentation.
5. **Transparency of Rights and Duties.** Participation in OUG governance implies defined responsibilities, accountability, and contribution to shared academic standards.

### 3. Categories of Membership

**3.1 Full Member Institutions.** Legally recognised higher education institutions that are accredited under applicable higher education law, enter into a formal Federation Agreement with OUG, contribute teaching provision to OUG degree programmes, and participate actively in OUG governance structures. Full Member Institutions have voting rights in designated OUG organs, the right to nominate and elect representatives, and eligibility to host OUG students and deliver OUG modules.

**3.2 Associate Member Institutions.** May include higher education institutions, research organisations, international partner universities, or other accredited educational bodies. Associate Members may deliver specific modules or programmes and may participate in advisory or academic bodies, but do not hold full voting rights unless explicitly defined.

**3.3 Individual Academic Participants.** Academic staff (e.g. professors, lecturers, examiners) participate in OUG through delegation by their employing member institution, and by appointment or election to specific OUG roles. OUG does not require direct employment relationships with teaching staff. Academic responsibility within OUG is exercised through mandated roles, not employment contracts.

### 4. Rights of Member Institutions

Member Institutions have the right to: participate in OUG academic governance according to this framework; nominate candidates for OUG organs and committees; deliver accredited teaching within OUG programmes; receive financial compensation for teaching provision; and co-award or parallel-award degrees where contractually agreed.

Participation rights are proportional to the scope of teaching provision, the number of enrolled OUG students, and the level of institutional commitment.

### 5. Duties of Member Institutions

Member Institutions commit to: compliance with OUG academic regulations and quality standards; recognition of OUG examination authority; provision of qualified teaching and examination staff; participation in quality assurance and evaluation procedures; and transparency regarding student numbers and teaching delivery.

Failure to meet these duties may result in remedial measures, suspension of teaching delegation, or termination of membership.

### 6. Participation in OUG Governance Structures

Participation in OUG organs and committees is based on institutional membership status, elected or appointed representation, and clearly defined mandates. Representation may include academic representatives (e.g. professors), institutional representatives, and, where appropriate, student or learner representatives.

Detailed rules on composition, voting rights, term lengths, and election procedures are defined in the Statute on Organs and Elections (Part Two, below), which builds directly on this framework.

### 7. Academic Leadership and Secondment

Academic leadership roles within OUG (e.g. Rector, Vice-Rectors, Deans) are elected or appointed according to OUG statutes, may be held by individuals employed at member institutions, and are exercised through secondment or partial mandate.

Financial compensation for such roles is borne proportionally by OUG according to scope of responsibility, time commitment, and institutional arrangements.

### 8. Students and Enrollment Models

Students enrolled in OUG programmes are academically affiliated with OUG, subject to OUG examination and degree regulations, and may be administratively hosted by a member institution.

Dual enrollment models are permitted, including parallel enrollment at OUG and a member institution, recognition of identical examinations, and coordinated degree awarding.

### 9. Financial Principles

Funding flows within the federation follow these principles: student-based public funding and tuition are routed to OUG for OUG-enrolled students; OUG retains a defined overhead share for governance, quality assurance, and infrastructure; the majority of funds are transferred to teaching-providing member institutions; allocation is based on transparent criteria (e.g. ECTS delivered, student numbers).

### 10. Relationship to Further Regulations

This framework is complemented by the Statute on Organs and Elections, the Examination Regulations (Prüfungsordnung), the Quality Assurance Framework, and individual Provider Agreements. In case of conflict, this framework prevails with respect to membership rights and duties.

### 11. Entry into Force and Amendments

This framework enters into force upon adoption by the competent OUG organ. Amendments require qualified approval as defined in the Statute on Organs and Elections.

## Part Two: Statute on Organs, Participation, and Elections

### Section 1 — Purpose and Scope

This statute regulates the constituent groups of the Open University of Germany (OUG), their roles and participation rights, and their eligibility for representation in OUG organs and committees. It builds on the Federation & Membership Framework and specifies how participation is operationalised at the level of persons and groups.

### Section 2 — Constituent Groups of OUG

OUG is constituted through the participation of distinct groups, reflecting its character as a federated, degree-awarding institution with delegated teaching provision and lifelong learning pathways. Participation rights derive from academic responsibility, institutional contribution, or learner status, not from employment by OUG.

**§2.1 Academic Staff (Professors and Equivalent Academic Staff).** Individuals who hold a professorial or equivalent academic position at a Full or Associate Member Institution, are formally delegated to teach, examine, or fulfil academic roles within OUG programmes, and meet the qualification requirements defined by OUG academic regulations. Academic staff constitute the academic core of OUG governance — they safeguard academic standards, contribute to curriculum development and quality assurance, and are eligible for election or appointment to academic organs and leadership roles. For the purposes of this statute, the term *professor* includes functionally equivalent academic roles as recognised by OUG.

**§2.2 Learners (Students).** Individuals who are enrolled in one or more OUG degree programmes or certified learning pathways, subject to OUG examination regulations, and recorded in OUG's learner registry. Learners may be degree-seeking students or non-degree-seeking participants in stackable microcredentials. Learners form a distinct group with representation rights in designated organs, reflecting their role as active participants in the academic process.

**§2.3 Alumni and Lifelong Learners.** Alumni are individuals who have successfully completed an OUG degree programme. Lifelong learners are individuals who have completed one or more OUG-certified learning units and remain registered in OUG systems for continued learning and recognition. Alumni and lifelong learners together form a group representing continuity of learning, long-term programme relevance, and external perspective on academic quality. Their participation is primarily advisory but may include voting rights where defined.

**§2.4 Institutional Representatives of Member Institutions.** Individuals formally nominated by Full or Associate Member Institutions to represent institutional interests within OUG. They act on behalf of their institution, represent structural, strategic, and resource-related perspectives, and ensure alignment between OUG and member institutions. Institutional representatives may participate in governance bodies concerned with strategy, finance, federation matters, and portfolio coordination.

**§2.5 External Partners.** Include representatives of municipalities and regions, industry and professional organisations, civil society and public institutions, or other strategic partners of OUG. External partners do not hold academic authority and do not participate in examination decisions, but may contribute expertise and perspective. Their participation is advisory and supports societal relevance, transfer, and impact.

**§2.6 OUG Academic Leadership.** Includes individuals holding roles such as Rector / President, Vice-Rectors, Deans (Mission Hub Stewards), and other leadership positions defined by OUG statutes. These roles are exercised through election or appointment, may be held by individuals employed at member institutions, and are based on delegated authority rather than employment by OUG. Academic leadership forms a functional group for the purpose of eligibility and responsibility, not a separate constituency.

### Section 3 — Principles of Group Participation

1. **Academic Primacy.** Academic decisions are made with decisive participation of academic staff.
2. **Learner Voice.** Learners participate in governance affecting learning conditions and quality.
3. **Institutional Balance.** Member institutions participate without dominating academic decision-making.
4. **Advisory Inclusion.** External partners contribute without undermining academic autonomy.
5. **Delegated Authority.** Leadership authority is exercised on behalf of the federation, not individual institutions.

### Section 4 — Organs and Academic Structures of OUG

**§4.1 Overview of OUG Organs.** OUG operates through a set of organs and academic structures that reflect its federated nature, its separation of academic authority from teaching delivery, and its commitment to openness, participation, and quality. OUG organs are constituted at three levels: central governance organs; academic mission-based structures; and advisory and coordinating bodies. Each organ derives its legitimacy from the constituent groups defined in Section 2 and operates within the principles set out in the Federation & Membership Framework.

**§4.2.1 Senate (Community Circle).** The central academic deliberative body of OUG. Responsible for safeguarding academic standards, approving academic regulations and curricula, defining strategic academic principles, and ensuring coherence across Mission Hubs. The Senate represents the academic community of the federation and is not a managerial body — it does not execute day-to-day operations but provides normative and strategic academic guidance.

**§4.2.2 Rectorate / Presidency.** The executive academic leadership of OUG. Responsible for external representation, implementation of academic strategy, coordination of Mission Hubs, and oversight of quality assurance and accreditation. The Rector and Vice-Rectors exercise authority on behalf of the federation and may hold concurrent positions at member institutions.

**§4.3.1 Role and Purpose of Mission Hubs.** Mission Hubs are the primary academic units of OUG and functionally replace traditional faculties. Each Mission Hub is organised around a coherent academic domain or mission, holds responsibility for curriculum development and maintenance, coordinates teaching contributions across member institutions, and hosts research and innovation activities aligned with its mission. Mission Hubs are not tied to physical locations and may span multiple institutions, regions, and countries.

**§4.3.2 Deans as Mission Hub Stewards.** Each Mission Hub is led by a Dean, who acts as a steward rather than a hierarchical manager. The Dean coordinates academic activity within the hub, facilitates collaboration among participating academics, represents the hub in OUG governance bodies, and ensures alignment with OUG academic principles. Deans are elected according to the procedures defined in Section 6 and may be employed by member institutions.

**§4.4.1 Establishment of Research Groups.** Within each Mission Hub, Research Groups may be established as thematic, collaborative academic units. Research Groups are formed by academic staff affiliated with member institutions, operate across institutional boundaries, and are aligned with the mission and academic scope of the hosting Mission Hub. Membership in a Research Group is open and voluntary, subject to academic relevance and capacity.

**§4.4.2 Purpose and Function of Research Groups.** Research Groups serve to facilitate collaborative research activities, support joint publications and grant applications, foster interdisciplinary exchange, and integrate research insights into teaching and curriculum development. They provide a structured environment in which professors and researchers from different institutions can find each other, collaborate, and build sustained academic agendas within the OUG framework.

**§4.4.3 Governance of Research Groups.** Research Groups are recognised by the Mission Hub Dean, operate under the academic principles of OUG, and report their activities periodically to the Mission Hub. They do not constitute independent legal entities and do not exercise examination or degree-awarding authority. Coordination roles within Research Groups may be appointed or elected internally.

**§4.5 Advisory and Coordinating Bodies.** OUG may establish additional councils or committees to support specific functions, including but not limited to portfolio governance, quality assurance, digital infrastructure, or external partnerships. The composition and mandate of such bodies are defined by separate regulations or by decision of the competent OUG organ.

### Section 5 — Composition of Organs

**§5.1 General Principles of Composition.** The composition of OUG organs reflects the federated character of the Open University of Germany and is governed by: Academic Primacy (academic staff constitute the decisive group in academic organs); Federated Balance (member institutions participate without exercising dominance over academic decision-making); Learner Participation (learners participate in matters affecting learning conditions and quality); Functional Representation (representation is linked to responsibility and contribution, not employment by OUG); Transparency and Proportionality (composition rules are transparent and proportionate to the mandate of each organ).

**§5.2 Composition of the Senate (Community Circle).** The Senate is composed of representatives of the following groups: Academic Staff (Professors and equivalent) — majority of seats; Learners (Students) — minority of seats; Alumni / Lifelong Learners — advisory or voting seats as defined; Institutional Representatives — limited seats, non-dominant; Academic Leadership — ex officio, without majority control.

**Voting Principle.** Academic staff must hold more than half of the voting rights in the Senate. External partners do not hold voting rights in the Senate.

**§5.3 Composition of the Rectorate / Presidency.** The Rectorate consists of the Rector / President, Vice-Rectors, and, where applicable, additional academic leadership roles. Members are elected or appointed according to Section 6, may be employed by member institutions, and act exclusively in the interest of OUG while exercising their mandate. The Rectorate does not constitute a representative body and therefore does not follow group-proportional composition rules.

**§5.4 Composition of Mission Hubs.** Each Mission Hub includes academic staff affiliated with the hub (delegated by member institutions), the Dean (Mission Hub Steward), learner representatives where appropriate, and associated contributors (e.g. researchers, practitioners). Mission Hubs are academically led units — decision-making authority within Mission Hubs rests primarily with academic staff.

**§5.5 Composition of Research Groups.** Consist of academic staff from one or more member institutions, potentially including postdoctoral researchers and doctoral candidates. Research Groups are self-organising within the framework of the Mission Hub, do not require proportional representation, and do not include non-academic voting members.

**§5.6 Composition of Advisory and Coordinating Bodies.** Bodies such as a Quality Council or Portfolio Council are composed according to their function and may include academic staff, institutional representatives, learners, and external partners. Such bodies may be advisory only, or delegated limited decision-making authority. The specific composition and mandate are defined in the establishing resolution.

**§5.7 Exclusion of Conflicts of Interest.** Members of organs shall act in the interest of OUG as a whole. Where conflicts of interest arise due to institutional affiliation, financial interests, or dual roles, members are required to disclose such conflicts and, where appropriate, recuse themselves.

**§5.8 Summary Mapping (Normative Reference).** Senate: academic majority, learner participation, limited institutional representation. Rectorate: elected academic leadership, no group proportionality. Mission Hubs: academic-led, mission-oriented. Research Groups: open academic collaboration. Advisory Bodies: function-specific composition.

### Section 6 — Elections, Appointments, and Terms of Office

**§6.1 General Principles.** The appointment of members to OUG organs follows: Democratic legitimacy through election where strategic or academic authority is exercised; Academic responsibility as the primary criterion for eligibility; Delegated participation, reflecting the federated nature of OUG; Transparency and accountability in all appointment and election procedures; Limited tenure, preventing concentration of power and ensuring renewal. No office within OUG constitutes a permanent position or confers ownership over academic authority.

**§6.2.1 Academic Eligibility.** Eligible for election or appointment to academic organs are individuals who are members of the academic staff group as defined in §2.1, are formally delegated by a Full or Associate Member Institution, and meet the qualification requirements for the respective role. Direct employment by OUG is not a prerequisite for eligibility.

**§6.2.2 Learner Eligibility.** Learners as defined in §2.2 are eligible to elect learner representatives and to be elected to learner seats in designated organs. Eligibility requires active enrollment in an OUG programme at the time of election.

**§6.2.3 Alumni and External Eligibility.** Alumni and external partners may be eligible for appointment to advisory bodies, or election to designated representative roles, where explicitly defined in the mandate of the respective organ. They are not eligible for roles exercising examination or degree-awarding authority.

**§6.3 Election of the Senate (Community Circle).** Academic representatives are elected by and from the academic staff group. Learner representatives are elected by and from the learner group. Alumni representatives, where applicable, are elected or appointed according to rules defined by the Senate. Institutional representatives are nominated by Full Member Institutions. Elections are conducted for a fixed term and follow principles of equal vote within each group.

**§6.4 Election of the Rector / President.** The Rector / President is elected by the Senate. Eligibility requires academic qualification and experience, demonstrated capacity for federated leadership, and acceptance of the OUG leadership mandate. The Rector / President represents OUG externally and exercises executive academic authority.

**§6.5 Appointment of Vice-Rectors.** Appointed by the Rector / President, subject to confirmation by the Senate. Appointments reflect functional responsibilities (e.g. teaching and learning, research and innovation, governance and quality), diversity of academic perspectives, and balance across Mission Hubs.

**§6.6 Election of Deans (Mission Hub Stewards).** Each Mission Hub elects a Dean from among the academic staff affiliated with that hub. The election is conducted by the Mission Hub academic members. The Dean acts as academic coordinator, facilitator of collaboration, and representative of the Mission Hub in OUG organs.

**§6.7 Appointment of Committee Members.** Members of committees and councils are elected by the relevant organ, or appointed by the Rectorate, according to the mandate and function of the committee. Appointments aim to ensure relevant expertise, balanced representation, and avoidance of conflicts of interest.

**§6.8 Terms of Office.** Unless otherwise specified: Rector / President — 4 years. Vice-Rectors — 4 years. Senate Members — 2 years. Deans — 3 years. Committee Members — 2 years. Re-election or re-appointment is permitted once, unless explicitly restricted.

**§6.9 Removal and Early Termination.** Members of organs may be removed from office before the end of their term if they no longer meet eligibility requirements, they breach their duties, or they resign. Removal procedures are defined by the competent organ and must respect due process.

**§6.10 Transitional and Supplementary Provisions.** Further details on election procedures, nomination processes, and voting methods may be defined in implementing regulations, provided they do not contradict this statute.

### Section 7 — Continuity, Interpretation, and Conflict Resolution

**§7.1 Continuity of Governance.** The organs and procedures defined in this statute ensure the continuous functioning of OUG, independent of changes in individual office holders, changes in member institutions, or changes in the scale and composition of academic offerings. No organ or office holder may suspend or override the operation of this statute outside the procedures explicitly defined herein.

**§7.2 Interpretation of the Statute.** In cases of ambiguity or dispute concerning the interpretation of this statute: (1) priority shall be given to the academic mission of OUG, the principles defined in the Federation & Membership Framework, and the protection of academic standards; (2) the Senate (Community Circle) is the competent body for authoritative interpretation of this statute, unless otherwise specified by law. Interpretations shall be documented and made accessible to the OUG community.

**§7.3 Conflict Resolution.** Conflicts arising within or between OUG organs, or between OUG and member institutions, shall be addressed in a staged manner: (1) informal resolution at the level of the affected organ or Mission Hub; (2) mediation facilitated by the Rectorate; (3) formal resolution by the Senate. Escalation beyond OUG structures occurs only where legally required.

### Section 8 — Amendment of the Statute

**§8.1 Right to Propose Amendments.** Amendments may be proposed by the Senate, the Rectorate, or a defined number of representatives from Full Member Institutions. Proposals must be submitted in writing and include a rationale.

**§8.2 Amendment Procedure.** Amendments require deliberation within the Senate and approval by a qualified majority as defined by Senate rules of procedure. Amendments must remain consistent with the Federation & Membership Framework, applicable higher education law, and the fundamental principles of academic self-governance.

**§8.3 Protection of Academic Core Principles.** Amendments may not remove academic primacy in academic decision-making, eliminate learner participation entirely, or convert OUG organs into purely managerial or institutional control bodies. This clause ensures structural continuity and guards against erosion of the federated academic model.

### Section 9 — Entry into Force and Transitional Provisions

**§9.1 Entry into Force.** This statute enters into force upon adoption by the competent OUG organ and applies to all organs, members, and participants of OUG.

**§9.2 Transitional Arrangements.** Until elections and appointments under this statute are completed, existing interim bodies and office holders continue to exercise their functions, provided their actions remain consistent with this statute. Transitional arrangements shall be limited in duration and documented transparently.

**§9.3 Relationship to Other Regulations.** This statute is complemented by the Federation & Membership Framework, the Examination Regulations (Prüfungsordnung), and further statutes and regulations of OUG. In case of conflict, this statute governs matters of organs, participation, and elections.
