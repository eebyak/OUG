*Task Architecture · Rights · Duties · Onboarding · Time Commitment*

> **What this document is and how to use it.** This framework is a required annex to every OUG Partnership Agreement (Kooperationsvertrag). It defines what a partner institution must have, must do, and must become in order to participate in the OUG federation — and what the OUG commits to in return. It is not a service agreement. It is a membership framework: the minimum structural conditions for a genuine federated partnership, defined with as much precision as necessary and as little prescription as possible.
>
> The OUG federation is not a franchise. Partners are not delivery units executing OUG instructions. They are academic institutions with their own identity, their own staff, and their own traditions — who have chosen to make their modules available under the OUG credential architecture. This framework defines the interface, not the interior. What happens inside the partner institution is the partner's responsibility. What happens at the boundary is governed by this document.

## Section 1 — Named Roles

This framework uses English role names throughout. The corresponding German *Fachbegriff* (technical term) appears alongside each. Where a partner institution uses a different internal title for the same function, the mapping is recorded in Schedule A of the Partnership Agreement (Kooperationsvertrag).

| OUG role name | German Fachbegriff | Function in this framework |
|---|---|---|
| **Partnership Contact** | Kooperationsbeauftragte/r | Single named individual at the partner institution responsible for the OUG relationship. First point of contact for all joint governance matters. Does not need to be an academic. |
| **Programme Director** | Studiendekan/in | Senior academic responsible for the partner's academic quality and curriculum. Participates in joint programme development and annual partnership review. Not a member of OUG governance bodies. |
| **Module Lead** | Modulverantwortliche/r | Academic responsible for a specific module donated to the federation. Responsible for module delivery, assessment design, and cooperation with the OUG quality process. |
| **Programme Committee** | Prüfungsausschuss | Partner institution's internal body responsible for examination regulations and academic integrity. Counterpart to the OUG Quality Commission (Qualitätskommission) for module-level quality matters. |
| **Student Representative** | Studierendenvertretung | Partner institution's student body or designated student representative. Consulted during partnership review; not a party to the OUG governance process directly. |

## Section 2 — Partner Rights

A partner institution that has completed the onboarding process and holds active partnership status holds three rights within the OUG federation. These rights are substantive — not nominal.

**Right 1 — Credential Architecture Access.** The partner may offer modules under the OUG credential architecture. Learners who complete a partner module receive an OUG-issued Europass Digital Credential (Digitaler Europass-Nachweis) for that module. The credential is issued by OUG, not by the partner — this is the credential sovereignty that gives the OUG degree its integrity. The partner may communicate to prospective students that their modules lead to OUG-recognised credentials.

**Right 2 — Programme Development Participation.** The partner participates in joint programme development through the Programme Development Forum (Programmentwicklungsforum), a joint governance mechanism specified in Section 5. The partner's Programme Director (Studiendekan/in) contributes to decisions about the shape of the programmes in which their modules participate. This is a genuine contribution right, not a consultation. Partner input is documented and weighted in programme design decisions.

**Right 3 — Senate Academic Voice.** Each partner institution designates one Academic Voice (Akademische Stimme) — typically the Programme Director (Studiendekan/in) — who is invited to the annual OUG Senate Strategy Session (Strategische Sitzung des Senats). The Academic Voice may speak and submit written contributions. They do not hold voting rights in the OUG Senate (Senat), which is composed of OUG academic contributors and enrolled learners. They are guests with a structured voice, not members.

## Section 3 — Partner Duties

A partner institution holds four duties within the OUG federation. These duties are minimal by design — sufficient to maintain the integrity of the credential architecture and the quality of the learner experience, and no more. Each duty is specified with enough precision to be verifiable.

**Duty 1 — Module Delivery to Learning Outcome Standard.** The partner delivers each donated module to the learning outcome standard specified in the Module Agreement (Modulvereinbarung). The standard is defined in terms of competencies — what a learner who completes the module knows and can do — not in terms of content or pedagogy. The partner chooses how to teach. OUG verifies what is learned. If the Quality Commission (Qualitätskommission) finds through the Stichprobe (quality sampling) that learning outcomes are not being met, the partner is notified and a joint improvement process begins. Persistent failure triggers a module suspension process under Section 7 [sic — see Section 8, Module Suspension and Partnership Exit].

**Duty 2 — Quarterly Signal Report.** The Partnership Contact (Kooperationsbeauftragte/r) submits a structured signal report to the OUG Partnership Office (Kooperationsbüro) four times per year. The report uses the OUG standard format: three metrics (module completion rate against expected pace, assessment integrity incidents, student query volume above baseline) and one open text field for anything the metrics do not capture. The report takes approximately 30–45 minutes to complete. OUG provides the form and the submission platform. The report is not an audit — it is an early warning mechanism that protects both the partner and the learner.

**Duty 3 — Joint Governance Participation.** The partner participates in three joint governance mechanisms: the complaint escalation process (Beschwerdeeskalation) when a learner complaint cannot be resolved at module level; the Programme Development Forum (Programmentwicklungsforum) for annual programme review; and the module approval process (Modulzulassungsverfahren) when a new module is proposed or an existing module is significantly revised. Participation in each mechanism is defined in Section 5. The time commitment for each is specified in Section 6.

**Duty 4 — Stichprobe Cooperation.** When the OUG Quality Commission (Qualitätskommission) triggers an independent quality sampling (Stichprobe) of a partner module, the partner cooperates fully. Cooperation means: providing access to assessment records for the sampled cohort, making the Module Lead (Modulverantwortliche/r) available for a structured conversation of no more than two hours, and responding to any quality improvement recommendations within the timeframe specified by the Quality Commission. The Stichprobe is not an inspection — it is the verification mechanism that gives the OUG credential its integrity. Partners who cooperate in good faith have nothing to fear from it.

## Section 4 — Module Contribution

### Number of Modules

A partner institution contributes a minimum of two modules and a maximum defined by the Capacity Assessment (Kapazitätsprüfung) conducted during Phase 2 of the onboarding process. The minimum of two modules is required because a single-module partnership creates a fragility — for both the partner and for learners enrolled in programmes that depend on that module — that is disproportionate to the administrative overhead of the partnership. The maximum is not a fixed rule; it is an agreed number in the Module Agreement (Modulvereinbarung), reviewed annually.

### Module Rewriting — Mapping, Not Rewriting

Partners are not required to rewrite their existing modules to match an OUG template. They are required to map them. The distinction is important.

A module mapping (Modulzuordnung) shows which competencies in the OUG Competency Map (Kompetenzrahmen) the module develops, at what level, and through which assessments. If the mapping reveals that the module partially develops a competency, the partner documents this honestly — the credential will certify what the module actually delivers, not what a template assumes. If the mapping reveals a gap against the minimum learning outcome standard for the programme the module enters, the partner has three options: address the gap through module revision, adjust the module's position in the programme architecture, or accept that the module will not be admitted to that programme.

OUG provides the Competency Mapping Template (Vorlage für die Kompetenzuordnung) and a trained advisor who works with the Module Lead (Modulverantwortliche/r) during Phase 2 of onboarding to complete the mapping. The mapping is the partner's intellectual work; OUG provides the framework and the support.

### OOAPI v5 Format Requirement

Every module contributed to the OUG federation must be described in OOAPI v5 format before it is published on the OUG platform. OOAPI v5 is the open standard for educational programme data that enables the OUG platform to display, search, and credential module information in a standardised way.

Partners are not required to understand OOAPI v5. They are required to complete the OUG Module Submission Form (Moduleinreichungsformular), which collects the information that the OUG platform team uses to generate the OOAPI v5 record. The form takes approximately one to two hours per module to complete. The partner reviews and approves the generated OOAPI record before it is published. If the record is inaccurate, the partner notifies the Partnership Office (Kooperationsbüro) and a correction is made. The duty is the partner's; the tooling and technical translation are OUG's.

## Section 5 — Task Architecture

*Required Annex to Partnership Agreement*

The following tables specify every recurring task in the OUG/partner relationship, assigning each to OUG, to the partner, or to a joint governance mechanism. This is the operational core of the partnership framework. When a task arises and its ownership is unclear, this table is the first reference. When a new task arises that is not in this table, it is treated as **Unclassified** and submitted to the Partnership Office (Kooperationsbüro) for classification before it is assigned.

### Credential and Quality Layer — OUG owns

- Europass Digital Credential (Digitaler Europass-Nachweis) issuance
- Student academic record (Prüfungsakte) maintenance
- Assessment integrity verification — Stichprobe
- Learning outcome (Lernergebnis) specification — final approval
- Platform architecture and student data sovereignty
- Partnership compliance monitoring
- Prior learning recognition (Anrechnung) decisions
- Degree award (Abschlussvergabe) decision

### Learning Experience Layer — Partner owns

- Module delivery (Moduldurchführung) and teaching
- Assessment design (Prüfungsgestaltung) within module
- First-marking and assessment feedback
- Student academic advising within module
- Learning environment management
- Local student support services
- Internal quality assurance (interne Qualitätssicherung) of module delivery

### Joint Governance Layer — shared responsibility

| Joint Task | Who participates | Convener |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint escalation (Beschwerdeeskalation) above module level | OUG Academic Steward + Partner Programme Director | OUG |
| New module approval (Modulzulassung) | OUG Quality Commission (standards review) + Partner Module Lead (proposal) | OUG |
| Programme development (Programmentwicklung) — annual review | OUG Academic Steward + Partner Programme Directors of all contributing partners | OUG |
| Partnership Agreement (Kooperationsvertrag) review | OUG Partnership Office + Partner Partnership Contact | Joint — alternating |
| Stichprobe preparation and debrief | OUG Quality Commission + Partner Module Lead + Partner Programme Director | OUG |
| Module revision — significant change | OUG Academic Steward + Partner Module Lead | Joint |

> When a task arises that is not in this table, it is Unclassified. It is submitted to the Partnership Office (Kooperationsbüro) for classification before it is assigned to anyone. Proximity is not a classification. Availability is not a classification. The table governs.

## Section 6 — Time Commitment

*Transparent and Minimal*

The total annual time commitment for a partner institution at minimum partnership scale (two modules) is approximately 28 hours across all named roles. This is the number a partner institution should use when planning the internal resource implications of OUG membership. It is a minimum — additional modules add approximately 4–6 hours per module per year in Module Lead time.

| Role | Activity | Frequency | Estimated hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partnership Contact | Quarterly signal report | 4× per year | ~6 hours |
| Partnership Contact | Annual partnership review meeting | 1× per year | ~3 hours |
| Partnership Contact | Joint governance (escalations, ad hoc) | As needed — estimated | ~4 hours |
| Programme Director | Programme Development Forum | 1× per year | ~4 hours |
| Programme Director | Senate Strategy Session — as Academic Voice | 1× per year | ~3 hours |
| Module Lead (per module) | Module review and competency map update | 1× per year | ~2 hours |
| Module Lead | Stichprobe cooperation — when triggered | ~once every 3 years per module | ~2 hours |
| All roles | Onboarding — Phase 1 and 2 (one-time) | One-time investment | ~8 hours total |
| **Total annual partner time commitment (minimum)** | | | **~28 hours** |

*Note: the onboarding hours (8 hours) are a one-time investment, not an annual commitment — included here for planning purposes. The annual steady-state commitment after full onboarding is approximately 20 hours for a two-module partner.*

## Section 7 — Onboarding Process

*Three Phases to Full Partnership*

Every partner institution enters the OUG federation through a three-phase onboarding process. No module is published and no credential is issued until Phase 3 is successfully completed. The phases are sequential; no phase may be skipped.

### Phase 1 · 4–6 weeks · Eligibility Assessment

**What happens.** The OUG Partnership Office conducts a structured assessment of the partner institution's eligibility for federation membership. The assessment covers: institutional accreditation status, existing quality assurance infrastructure, capacity to designate the three named roles (Partnership Contact, Programme Director, Module Lead), and preliminary identification of modules for contribution.

**What the partner provides.** Evidence of institutional accreditation (Akkreditierungsnachweis), a completed Partnership Expression of Interest form (Interessensbekundungsformular), and a preliminary module list with brief descriptions.

**What OUG provides.** A named Partnership Advisor (Kooperationsberater/in) from the OUG Partnership Office, the Expression of Interest form, and a written Eligibility Assessment outcome within four weeks of receiving complete documentation.

**Outcome.** One of three: **Eligible** — proceed to Phase 2. **Conditionally Eligible** — specific conditions must be met before Phase 2 begins (conditions are named and a timeline is agreed). **Not Eligible** — written reasons provided; the partner may reapply when conditions are met.

### Phase 2 · 8–12 weeks · Joint Construction

**What happens.** The partner and OUG build the joint governance mechanisms together. This phase cannot be completed by either party alone — it requires genuine co-construction of the interface between the two institutions.

**Module mapping (Modulzuordnung).** The Module Lead completes the OUG Competency Mapping Template for each contributed module, supported by the OUG Partnership Advisor. Gaps against the learning outcome standard are identified and addressed.

**OOAPI v5 submission.** The partner completes the OUG Module Submission Form for each module. The OUG platform team generates the OOAPI v5 record and returns it for partner review and approval.

**Joint mechanism design.** The complaint escalation protocol (Beschwerdeeskalationsprotokoll) and the module-level quality communication process are specified for this partnership. These are not generic templates — they are agreements about how this partner and OUG will communicate when things go wrong.

**Role confirmation.** The three named roles (Partnership Contact, Programme Director, Module Lead) are formally confirmed in writing, with named individuals and their time commitments acknowledged by their institution.

**Outcome.** A signed Module Agreement for each contributed module, a completed OOAPI v5 record approved by the partner, a signed Partnership Agreement including this framework as Annex A, and a probationary start date.

### Phase 3 · 12 months · Probationary Year — leads to Full Partnership

**What happens.** The partner operates under the full partnership framework for one academic year, with enhanced signal reporting and one mid-year check-in. The probationary year is not a trial in which failure is the default assumption — it is a structured period in which both parties learn how the interface works in practice and make adjustments before the relationship is confirmed at full status.

**Enhanced signal reporting.** During the probationary year, the quarterly signal report includes one additional field: a free-text reflection on what is working and what is not in the partnership interface. This field is dropped at full partnership status unless the partner requests to retain it.

**Mid-year check-in.** At the six-month mark, the Partnership Contact and the OUG Partnership Advisor meet — in person or digitally — for a structured 90-minute check-in. The check-in reviews: signal report data to date, any issues that have arisen, and any adjustments needed to the Module Agreement or the joint governance mechanisms.

**Full partnership confirmation.** At the end of the probationary year, the OUG Partnership Office issues a Full Partnership Confirmation (Bestätigung der Vollmitgliedschaft) if: all four quarterly signal reports have been submitted, no unresolved quality issues remain, and both parties agree that the interface is functioning as designed. If issues remain, the probationary period may be extended by one semester with specific resolution conditions.

**What full partnership means.** Full partnership status triggers the partner's right to Academic Voice in the OUG Senate Strategy Session, participation in the Programme Development Forum with full contribution rights, and eligibility to propose additional modules for federation inclusion.

## Section 8 — Module Suspension and Partnership Exit

The OUG federation is a long-term relationship. Exit provisions exist not because exit is expected but because the absence of clear exit provisions creates perverse incentives — for both parties to remain in a relationship that is not working rather than to address problems directly.

### Module Suspension

A module may be suspended from the OUG federation if: the Stichprobe finds that learning outcomes are not being met and the partner does not complete a joint improvement process within the agreed timeline; the partner fails to submit three or more quarterly signal reports in a twelve-month period without prior notification; or a pattern of unresolved learner complaints indicates a systemic quality failure. Module suspension is not partnership termination. Suspended modules are removed from the OUG platform; other partner modules continue. The partner is notified in writing with specific conditions for reinstatement.

### Partnership Exit

Either party may exit the partnership with six months written notice. During the notice period, no new learner enrolments are accepted into the departing partner's modules. Learners already enrolled are guaranteed the right to complete their current module under OUG credential architecture. Learners mid-programme are assigned alternative module provision by the OUG Academic Steward. The partner retains all credentials already issued to its learners. Module intellectual property remains with the partner at all times.

### The Minimal Principle

Every provision in this framework was written against one question: is this the minimum required to maintain credential integrity and learner quality, or is it institutional overhead that serves the institution rather than the learner?

The answer to that question is visible in what this framework does *not* contain. It does not contain: requirements for partners to adopt OUG management systems, obligations to attend OUG events, reporting requirements beyond the quarterly signal report, content prescriptions for module design, or governance participation beyond the three specified joint mechanisms. These are absent by design, not by oversight.

> The OUG federation is not a compliance regime. It is a quality architecture. The partner's job is to teach well. OUG's job is to certify what has been learned. Everything in this framework serves one or both of those two functions. Nothing in this framework serves anything else.
