The OUG cannot be explained to every audience in the same order.

A prospective partner university first needs to understand what participation requires and what it makes possible. A ministry needs to see public purpose, legal feasibility, financial responsibility, and a credible path toward implementation. A reader encountering the OUG for the first time may need the institutional model explained before encountering its statutes and governance frameworks. Others need to understand why the architecture matters in human terms before they are ready to examine how it works.

The presentations in this directory are therefore not five competing descriptions of the OUG. They are audience-specific entrances into the same institutional design.

They should be read as communication instruments rather than as sources of institutional authority. Where a presentation simplifies, condenses, or interprets the OUG architecture, the underlying statutes, frameworks, financial plans, and governance documents remain authoritative.







## What's Here

* **[OUG Erklärung](OUG_Erklaerung.pptx)** — the general explanatory deck and the most suitable starting point for an audience unfamiliar with the project. It introduces the problem the OUG addresses, the logic of a public federated university, and the relationship between openness, academic responsibility, and institutional design.

* **[OUG Die Fehlende Schicht](OUG%20Die%20Fehlende%20Schicht.pptx)** — the strategic positioning deck. It presents the OUG as the missing institutional layer between existing universities, learners with increasingly non-linear educational biographies, and learning that already takes place across institutional boundaries. Best suited to discussions about the structural gap in the German higher-education system.

* **[OUG Hochschulpartner DHBW](OUG_Hochschulpartner_DHBW.pptx)** — the university-partner deck. It addresses the OUG from the perspective of a potential participating institution: why a university might join, what it can contribute, what it retains, and how teaching provision can be federated without surrendering institutional identity or academic standards. The deck is framed particularly for discussion within the DHBW context.

* **[OUG Menschlicher Fall](OUG_Menschlicher_Fall.pptx)** — the human case for the OUG. It begins with the lived consequences of the current system rather than with organisational structure. This deck is useful where the audience first needs to understand who is excluded, delayed, or made dependent on exceptional individual effort when higher education cannot accommodate modular, interrupted, cross-institutional, or lifelong learning.

* **[OUG Ministerium](OUG_Ministerium.pptx)** — the ministry-facing presentation. It translates the OUG into the questions public decision-makers must ask: why this institution serves a public purpose, how it relates to the existing higher-education system, whether it can be implemented lawfully and responsibly, what public support would enable, and which decisions would be required to move from design to founding.

## Choosing a Deck

Start with **OUG Erklärung** when the audience has little prior knowledge of the OUG.

Use **Die Fehlende Schicht** when the central question is why a new institutional layer is needed rather than another isolated programme or digital platform.

Use **OUG Hochschulpartner DHBW** when speaking with universities, faculties, programme leaders, or academic staff considering participation in the federation.

Use **OUG Menschlicher Fall** when the discussion should begin with learners, educators, and the human consequences of institutional fragmentation.

Use **OUG Ministerium** when the audience is concerned with public mandate, legal implementation, financing, system responsibility, and the founding pathway.

## Relationship to the Institutional Documents

The presentations draw on the broader OUG architecture but do not reproduce it in full.

Readers seeking the underlying communication logic should begin with the **[OUG Communication Architecture](../communication-architecture.md)**. It defines the different levels at which the institution can be explained and the questions, concerns, and forms of evidence associated with different audiences.

* **[Partner Membership Framework](../../partnership/partner-membership-framework.md)** — the operational interface between the OUG and participating universities;
* **[Federated Participation & Incentive Framework](../../partnership/federated-participation-incentive-framework.md)** — the participation types, incentives, and protections available to partners;

* **[Market Study](../market-study.md)** — the external positioning of the OUG in relation to existing German and European providers.

## Using the Presentations Responsibly

These decks are designed to support live explanation and discussion. Their brevity necessarily removes qualifications, definitions, and dependencies that are visible in the full institutional documents.

Before using a deck externally:

* confirm that its legal, financial, and implementation claims still match the current source documents;
* adapt the opening and closing slides to the specific audience and purpose;
* distinguish clearly between the current prospective design and an already established or accredited university;
* do not treat presentation wording as a substitute for formal institutional commitments;
* preserve the central architectural claim: the OUG coordinates academic pathways and degree responsibility through a public federation rather than replacing the universities that provide teaching.

GitHub may download the PowerPoint files rather than preview them in the browser. The original `.pptx` files are retained so that they can be presented, adapted, and versioned without reducing them to static images.
