## Six Audiences · Four Expression Levels · One Coherent Institutional Voice

> **How to use this document.** The Communication Architecture specifies what the OUG says to six distinct audiences, at four levels of detail, with awareness of what each audience most needs to hear and what they are most likely to be afraid of. It is a governance document — derived from the Role Statement, anchored to the founding principles, and reviewed whenever the strategic plan changes. It is not a marketing brief.
>
> Section I contains the four expression levels that every audience can draw from. Section II contains the audience-specific adaptations — what stays constant and what changes depending on who is in the room. Section III specifies what must never change regardless of audience.

## I. The Four Expression Levels

The OUG's core message must be expressible at four levels of detail without changing substance. Anyone who represents the OUG in any context should be able to move fluently between these levels depending on what the moment requires.

### Level 1 — 30 Seconds
*An elevator, a conference introduction, the first sentence of a cold email*

> The Open University of Germany is Germany's first public university designed from the ground up for the adult learner who works, cares, and studies on their own terms — at €25 per credit, with a portable credential for every module completed.

What this formulation does: it names the target learner immediately (working adult), the cost (€25/ECTS — one sixth of the private market), and the credential architecture (portable per module). It does not claim to be revolutionary, unique, or transformative — claims the audience cannot yet evaluate. It states a structural fact that any audience can assess.

### Level 2 — 2 Minutes
*A board meeting introduction · a ministerial briefing opening · a conference lightning talk*

Germany has a public duty to provide higher education on terms its citizens can actually access. That duty is currently unmet for approximately 15 million adults who hold no higher education qualification but express interest in further study. The primary barriers they name are time format, cost, geography, and entry requirements. The FernUniversität Hagen — Germany's only public distance university — was not designed to remove those barriers. IU Internationale Hochschule was. IU now has 140,000 students and charges up to €12,000 for a degree.

The Open University of Germany is the public institution that should have existed before IU did. Fully online, asynchronous-first, modular. €25 per ECTS — legally constituted as a public cost contribution, not a market price. A Europass Digital Credential issued for every completed module, so that partial completion has portable, verifiable value from the first module. Prior learning recognised as a primary entry pathway, not an exception.

We are not competing with Hagen and we are not competing with IU. We are reaching the learner neither has been designed to serve — and we are doing it as a public institution, with a governance architecture that protects the mission from both commercial pressure and institutional inertia.

That is the Open University of Germany.

### Level 3 — 5 Minutes
*A publisher meeting · a potential board member conversation · a funder pitch · a detailed partner introduction*

Let me start with a number: 140,000. That is how many students IU Internationale Hochschule — a private for-profit university — enrolled by 2026, making it Germany's largest university. IU grew from a small institution to this scale in roughly five years. The people who chose it were not making a statement about private education. They were choosing the only institution whose architecture was designed for their situation: flexible payment, formats that fit around employment, credentials that do not disappear if life intervenes before a degree is complete.

FernUniversität Hagen has an access mission and 50 years of distance learning experience. It has 78,000 students — roughly half of IU. The gap is not explained by quality. Hagen's curriculum is designed by five traditional faculty committees, its degrees require full completion to produce any portable value, its semester structure is fixed, and its tuition accumulates across a multi-year programme. These are not failures — they are structural choices that were not designed for the learner IU turned out to serve.

The OUG was designed specifically against those structural choices. We mapped the governance patterns that produce Hagen's limitations, and we built the counter-designs into the founding architecture. The curriculum is held by a central Design Authority — not by faculty committees — so quality is non-negotiable at the federation boundary. Every completed module produces a Europass Digital Credential — so partial completion has value from day one. The price is €25 per ECTS, constituted as a public cost contribution under Allgemeinwohl law — so the degree costs under €4,500 rather than €12,000. Prior learning recognition is a primary pathway with a 30-day turnaround target — not a petition process.

We operate a federated model. The OUG holds the credential architecture and the curriculum standard. Partner universities — European institutions with existing accreditation — contribute the teaching. This means we can scale access without scaling infrastructure costs, and it means our quality is continuously verified through the Stichprobe sampling system and a peer-based Developmental Audit that publishes its findings publicly every three years.

We are founded under German higher education law with a ministerial *Betrauungsakt* that constitutes the €25 as a public service price, not a commercial one. We are seeking state recognition as a *Fernhochschule* under LHG BW. We expect our first cohort in Year 1, break-even at 2,200 students in Year 4, and full sustainability from Year 5.

The people who built IU proved the demand is real. The OUG is the public answer to that proof.

### Level 4 — Full Narrative
*Accreditation submission · book proposal · formal ministry document · investor memorandum*

The full institutional narrative is the [Role Statement](../strategy/role-statement.md) combined with the constitutional argument ([Allgemeinwohl Verfassung](../governance/allgemeinwohl-verfassung.md)) and the governance pattern signature comparison (`OUG_Hagen_IU_PatternSignature_Alignment.docx` — not yet located in this repository, see the Role Statement's own note on this). These three documents together constitute the complete founding argument. For a formal submission, they are read in that order: Role Statement first (what we are and where we sit in the ecosystem), Allgemeinwohl document second (why the state is constitutionally obligated to fund this), pattern comparison third (why Hagen does not make this institution unnecessary).

For accreditation specifically, the [Quality Assurance & Academic Governance](../quality/quality-assurance-academic-governance.md) document and the Didactic Framework (`OUG_Didactic_Framework_BSc_CS.docx`) are the primary technical documents. The [Design Authority Constitution](../governance/design-authority-constitution.md) constitutes the body that holds curriculum standard authority. The [Developmental Audit Framework](../quality/developmental-audit-framework.md) specifies the quality accountability architecture beyond minimum accreditation compliance.

## II. Audience-Specific Adaptations

The four expression levels are the stable foundation. What changes by audience is emphasis — which dimension of the OUG's story leads, which concern is addressed first, and where the argument anticipates resistance.

### Publisher — Academic Press (Edward Elgar / MIT Press)
*Assessing whether the book is a genuine scholarly contribution or institutional self-promotion*

**Core message:** This book derives governance design principles from practitioner dialogues, builds a pattern catalogue for higher education governance, and tests the OUG against those patterns honestly — including naming the gaps. It is a methodology contribution first, an institutional account second.

**What they need to hear:** The pattern catalogue is the contribution. The OUG is the test case. The book would be worth reading if the OUG never opens — because the antipattern / design pattern pairs address every institution that faces governance dysfunction, not only this one.

**What they are afraid of:** That this is advocacy dressed as scholarship. Address directly: the stress tests name gaps alongside strengths. The Design Signature has three columns — built, deferred, committed. The institution is not presented as complete.

### Potential Board Member (Hochschulrat)
*Considering whether the institution is credible, the governance architecture is sound, and the personal commitment is worth the professional risk*

**Core message:** The OUG is constitutionally designed so that G1 (the Hochschulrat) holds genuine accountability — not advisory standing. The Hochschulrat approves the gate review at Year 2. It receives the Transformation Accountability Report annually. It owns the Developmental Audit publication decision.

**What they need to hear:** The governance architecture gives the board real authority with real accountability. The term structure (4 years) is bounded. The Developmental Audit is the accountability mechanism that makes the institution's performance publicly visible — which means board members can be held to account for what the institution produces.

**What they are afraid of:** That the governance is performative — that the Rector will run the institution and the board will rubber-stamp. Address: the G1/G2 constitutional separation, the gate review authority, and the Hochschulrat's specific power to commission unscheduled governance reviews.

### Funder — BMBF / EU / Foundation
*Assessing whether public money will produce measurable impact or fund institutional ambition*

**Core message:** The OUG's financial model breaks even at 2,200 students in Year 4 without ongoing public subsidy. The BMBF grant bridges the founding deficit. Every euro of BMBF funding produces a public university serving learners who currently pay a private company €80–120 per ECTS for what the state is constitutionally obligated to provide.

**What they need to hear:** The federated model means public funding leverages existing partner infrastructure rather than building new infrastructure from scratch. The Stichprobe and Developmental Audit are the accountability mechanisms. The Year 2 gate review with four named outcomes (including wind-down with learner protection) demonstrates that the founding team has designed for failure, not only for success.

**What they are afraid of:** That the OUG will consume public funding and fail to reach sustainability. Address: the break-even model, the phased founding framework with explicit gate criteria, and the Allgemeinwohl constitution that protects the mission from mission drift.

### Ministry (MWK BW) — "We Don't Need Another University"
*Protecting existing institutions from perceived competition, managing the political risk of a new institution failing publicly*

**Core message:** The OUG does not compete with any existing public institution. It reaches the 15 million adults with no higher education qualification who are not currently in any public institution — and who are currently paying a private company for what the state is constitutionally obligated to provide. IU's 140,000 students are the evidence of the constitutional gap. The OUG closes it.

**What they need to hear:** The three-partner pilot design means the state's exposure in Year 1–2 is bounded. If the federated quality model fails, the wind-down protocol transfers all learners to partner institutions with full credit recognition. The state's reputational risk is limited by the phased design. The constitutional risk of not acting — allowing a private company to dominate publicly obligated provision — is larger than the risk of founding the OUG.

**What they are afraid of:** That the OUG will embarrass the ministry if it fails publicly, or that it will damage Hagen. Address the first with the phased founding gate. Address the second directly: the OUG and Hagen serve different populations — the OUG serves learners Hagen's architecture was not designed for, and the evidence is that those learners chose IU rather than Hagen.

### Accreditation Board (EVALAG / AQAS / ACQUIN)
*Determining whether the OUG meets minimum requirements for degree-awarding status and whether the federated model is certifiable*

**Core message:** The OUG's quality architecture operates at four levels: continuous platform analytics, annual Stichprobe sampling (5% of modules across all partners), Europass competency credentials at module completion, and a three-year peer-based Developmental Audit against self-defined purpose. The Design Authority constitutes the body that holds curriculum standard authority. The Didactic Framework specifies the five-phase Didactic Unit structure. The Competency Codebook v10.0 maps 149 competencies to ESCO and Bloom L1–L4.

**What they need to hear:** The federated model addresses the "sufficient qualified staff" criterion through an equivalence argument: the combined teaching capacity of the partner network exceeds the minimum requirement for a degree-awarding institution, and is verified through partnership agreements and annual Stichprobe. The Design Authority's central curriculum standard is the mechanism that maintains quality coherence across a federated delivery model. The Developmental Audit is the OUG's self-accountability mechanism — it goes beyond what accreditation requires, not below it.

**What they are afraid of:** That the federated model is not certifiable under existing frameworks — that there is no precedent for a degree-awarding institution without its own primary teaching staff. Address directly with the legal analysis in the [Rechtliche Implementierungsvoraussetzungen](../governance/legal-implementation-requirements.md) and the precedent of the SRH Fernhochschule in Riedlingen for the BW jurisdiction.

### Potential Academic Partner
*Considering whether joining the federation serves their institution's interests and whether the OUG's standards are compatible with their culture*

**Core message:** Partnership means your teaching reaches learners who would not otherwise access higher education — at a price point you cannot offer alone, with a credential architecture that gives those learners portable value even for partial completion. The OUG holds the quality standard; you hold the teaching expertise and the delivery.

**What they need to hear:** The revenue share (40% of €25/ECTS to the partner) is a supplementary income stream for teaching that partners are already doing or want to do. The quality assessment process is rigorous but transparent — the five criteria are published, the Design Authority's mandate is bounded, and the Conditional Eligibility pathway provides a clear route to admission for modules that need modest revision.

**What they are afraid of:** That the OUG's quality standard will constrain academic freedom or that the Design Authority will interfere with teaching decisions. Address: the DA assesses design quality at admission and on a two-year cycle. It does not govern how a module is taught, only whether its design meets the competency specification and didactic architecture standards.

## III. What Must Not Change Across Audiences

Three things stay constant regardless of audience, format, or communication level. They are the tests for whether a communication is genuinely representing the OUG or drifting toward what the specific audience wants to hear.

**The price point** is €25 per ECTS constituted as a public cost contribution — not a temporary introductory rate, not a subsidised price, not a market position. It is the structural expression of the Allgemeinwohl mandate. Any communication that presents it as a competitive price rather than a constitutional one has misrepresented the institution.

**The target learner** is the adult who cannot access existing higher education on the terms it is offered — not the adult who prefers a cheaper or more flexible option. The OUG is not a convenience institution. It is a gap-filling institution. Any communication that presents it as a better version of what already exists has misrepresented the founding argument.

**The quality architecture** is more demanding than minimum accreditation compliance — not less. The Developmental Audit, the Stichprobe, and the Design Authority together constitute a quality system that goes beyond what the accreditation framework requires. Any communication that presents the OUG as a lighter-touch alternative to traditional quality standards has misrepresented the institution's founding commitment.
