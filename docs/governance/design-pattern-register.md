*Open University of Germany · Institutional Foundations · 2026*

> **Status:** *(to be set — see [Document Status](../index.md#document-status))* · **Version:** v3.0
> **What this document is.** A standalone register of all 42 design patterns from the book's governance pattern catalogue — each pattern paired with the antipattern (recurring institutional dysfunction) it is the structural inverse of — re-sorted by theme rather than by the dialogue source it came from. It is derived from, and should be read alongside, [*Designing a University from First Principles*](https://eebyak.github.io/OUG/), where each pattern's full context, mechanism, and worked implementation are set out in detail. This register gives only what is needed to recognise and apply each pattern: what it names, and what it requires.
> **Relationship to the [Principles Register](principles-register.md).** Principles are always present; patterns are one way — not the only way — of honouring them. A pattern's absence is not automatically a violated principle; a principle can be honoured by a single governance decision that never becomes a reusable pattern. This register and the Principles Register should be read as companions, not duplicates.
> **A note on scope.** The book states plainly that this catalogue is not complete: it covers 42 pairs across seven source perspectives, and a genuinely new eighth source (the Helix/Etzkowitz dialogue, five principles, EH-0 through EH-4) has not yet been derived into patterns at all. Nothing below should be read as an exhaustive account of institutional dysfunction — only of the dysfunction this catalogue has named so far.
> **How each entry is organised.** Antipattern name → Design pattern name, a short account of the failure and its structural inverse, then a citation giving the original pair number and source block for traceability back to the book.

## 1. Law as Design Space, Not Ceiling

*Three patterns addressing the same recurring confusion from three angles: institutions routinely misjudge where legal constraint actually ends, either overestimating it (treating tradition as law) or underestimating their own ambition once compliance is met (treating the legal minimum as the design goal).*

**The Permission Illusion → The Designed Floor.** Institutions frequently believe the law requires practices that are actually just accumulated convention, spending real political capital dismantling imagined constraints before any design work can begin. The counter-design classifies every governance rule as Statutory Requirement, Institutional Choice, or Open Design Space, reviewed on separate cycles, so tradition and law are never confused again. *(Pair 1 of 42 · HEI Observations block.)*

**The Compliance Ceiling → The Permission Floor.** The twin failure to the Permission Illusion: treating what the law requires as the limit of institutional ambition, rather than the floor above which real design begins. The counter-design keeps an active agenda of the legally-open space the institution has not yet used, not just a record of what it must do. *(Pair 12 of 42 · Learn8 block.)*

**The Constraint-First Institution → The Design-First Institution.** When institutional reform starts by asking what current funding and politics permit, the answer reflects the constraint more than the actual need — producing designs that are feasible but insufficient. The counter-design constitutionally separates a constraint-free design phase (what should this be, given real need) from an implementation phase (how do we approach it given real conditions), so ambition is set before it is filtered. *(Pair 25 of 42 · 6gov block.)*

## 2. Decision Authority, Accountability, and Jurisdiction

*Four patterns about a single recurring failure mode: governance structures whose formal description doesn't match their actual power, or whose boundaries were never written down — producing either paralysis (nobody can act) or diffusion (nobody is responsible once something goes wrong).*

**The Paper Tiger with Claws → The Transparent Authority.** A body formally labelled "advisory" that actually holds veto or consent power creates confusion about who is deciding versus who is consulting, and diffuses accountability when outcomes are poor. The counter-design forces every governance body into exactly one of three categories — Decision, Consent, or Advisory — with no overlap permitted. *(Pair 2 of 42 · HEI Observations block.)*

**The Participation Façade → The Quality Mechanism.** Consultation processes that produce no traceable influence on the decisions that follow them train participants to disengage, and the institution loses the distributed intelligence genuine participation would have provided. The counter-design requires a published Decision Account for every consulted decision: what was heard, what was decided, and how the two connect. *(Pair 22 of 42 · 7org block.)*

**The Participation Decoy → The Accountable Decision.** The complement to the Participation Façade: broad participation in shaping a decision with no one named as accountable for what it produces, so failures are attributed to "the process" rather than to any person or body. The counter-design's Decision Record names who participated, who decided, and — critically — who is accountable for implementation, reviewed on a schedule. *(Pair 30 of 42 · 6gov block.)*

**The Blurred Boundary → The Jurisdictional Map.** Where two roles could each plausibly claim authority over a decision, it either goes unmade (each assumes the other will act) or is made twice, incompatibly. The counter-design gives every role a written jurisdiction — what it decides alone, what it consults on, what it refers upward — produced before disputes arise rather than in response to them. *(Pair 34 of 42 · Person 1 block, retrospectively derived.)*

## 3. Role Design, Task Assignment, and Workload

*Three patterns addressing what happens when a role's actual scope is never written down: it expands to absorb whatever needs doing, strategic capacity gets consumed by operational urgency, and nobody can say afterward whether the role was ever reasonable to expect one person to hold.*

**Role Overflow → The Bounded Role.** A governance role defined by title and tradition rather than explicit scope expands indefinitely, until strategic leadership capacity is entirely consumed by operational accumulation. The counter-design specifies four explicit boundaries per role — strategic scope, operational scope, an exclusion list, and a minimum strategic-time allocation — reviewed against actual time use. *(Pair 3 of 42 · HEI Observations block.)*

**The Proximity Task → The Designed Task.** Recurring tasks with no designed owner default to whoever happens to be nearest, which is not the same as being correctly assigned, and quietly distorts whichever role absorbs them. The counter-design requires every recurring task to have an explicit owner before it is assigned, with an "Unclassified" holding category for anything genuinely new, routed to classification rather than absorbed informally. *(Pair 5 of 42 · HEI Observations block.)*

**The Overloaded Academic → The Unbundled Role.** Expecting one person to simultaneously design curriculum, create content, and deliver teaching means quality depends entirely on whether that individual happens to have good instructional-design instincts — a skill most academics were never trained in. The counter-design separates Curriculum Designer, Content Architect, and Learning Facilitator into distinct functional roles, so teaching quality is a matter of institutional design rather than individual accident. *(Pair 16 of 42 · Curr4 block.)*

## 4. Resources and Incentives Aligned to Purpose

*Two patterns naming the same structural insight from different angles: institutional incentive structures routinely reward behaviour that has nothing to do with the institution's actual purpose, simply because the incentive was inherited rather than designed.*

**The Year-End Flush → The Need-Based Allocation.** Annual budget cycles that punish underspending with reduced future allocation make full consumption, not strategic priority, the rational institutional response — a structural incentive no individual goodwill can overcome. The counter-design splits budget into a Base Allocation and a centrally-held Strategic Reserve, with need-alignment (not consumption rate) as the measure of good budget performance. *(Pair 4 of 42 · HEI Observations block.)*

**The Untranslated Incentive → The Converted Incentive.** External funding or policy incentives arriving without leadership capacity to convert them into genuine institutional direction produce reorganisation dressed up as transformation. The counter-design requires an Incentive Conversion Statement — three specific questions answered on the record — before any structural response to an external incentive is approved. *(Pair 29 of 42 · 6gov block.)*

## 5. Information Flow, Organisational Structure, and Governance Culture

*Three patterns about what happens when structural or cultural boundaries block information from reaching the people who need it — whether the boundary is between staff categories, between disciplinary units, or between rank and relevant expertise.*

**The Fault Line → The Crossing Point.** Different employment frameworks, governance rights, and career logics between academic and administrative staff create an information gap that no individual relationship reliably bridges — decisions get made without the operational intelligence that would have improved them. The counter-design maps every structural fault line explicitly and builds a designed Crossing Point for each: a formal mechanism, not a relationship, with a named convener and defined information flow. *(Pair 6 of 42 · HEI Observations block.)*

**The Disciplinary Island → The Cooperative Cluster.** Organisational units built around individual chair territories rather than shared tasks structurally prevent the interdisciplinary cooperation that institutional mission statements claim to value. The counter-design organises primary units around shared missions or problems, so cooperation is a structural default rather than something requiring individual goodwill to overcome disciplinary boundaries. *(Pair 31 of 42 · Person 1 block, retrospectively derived.)*

**The Status Hierarchy → The Collegial Order.** When formal rank determines whose voice carries weight regardless of relevance to the decision at hand, the institution systematically underuses the knowledge actually present in the room. The counter-design sources committee composition and discussion weight from relevant competence rather than seniority, and documents decisions by the arguments that shaped them rather than the rank of who made them. *(Pair 33 of 42 · Person 1 block, retrospectively derived.)*

## 6. Serving the Learner Who Actually Exists

*Six patterns, all naming versions of the same root failure: institutions design for an imagined student — young, full-time, campus-present, recently school-educated — and then treat every learner who doesn't match that image as an exception requiring accommodation rather than evidence of a design gap.*

**The Average Learner Fallacy → The Designed Profile.** Curriculum, pacing, and support structures are built around a single implicit student model, and departures from it are treated as individual accommodation requests rather than as evidence the design itself is incomplete. The counter-design maintains an explicit Learner Profile Map, testing every structural decision against the full range of profiles the institution actually serves, not just the historically dominant one. *(Pair 7 of 42 · Learn8 block.)*

**The Hidden Journey → The Visible Development.** When the degree is the only recognised output, everything learned before it is issued is institutionally invisible — a student who withdraws after three years of a four-year programme has nothing portable to show for it. The counter-design issues a standalone, portable credential at the level of each completed learning unit, so the full degree becomes the legible sum of its parts rather than an opaque bundle. *(Pair 9 of 42 · Learn8 block.)*

**The Passive Consumer → The Active Member.** Learners who cannot be campus-present or synchronously available lose the community and governance participation dimension of higher education entirely — a structural loss for asynchronous and remote learners, not an incidental one. The counter-design designs participation at two levels, community and governance, specifically for the profiles the institution serves, so flexibility does not produce isolation. *(Pair 10 of 42 · Learn8 block.)*

**The Standard Gate → The Designed Entry.** Institutions that formally accept alternative entry routes but only build visibility, legibility, and advising support for the standard pathway are open in theory and closed in practice for the population that most needs access. The counter-design gives every legally available entry pathway a named institutional owner and equivalent support infrastructure to the standard route. *(Pair 11 of 42 · Learn8 block.)*

**The Doorstep Fallacy → The Purposeful Entry.** Treating access expansion as self-evidently valuable, without first defining what is being accessed, means the institution scales whatever it already is — including its dysfunctions — to more people. The counter-design defines a substantive Purpose Statement before designing any access mechanism, and reports access metrics alongside learning-outcome metrics, never instead of them. *(Pair 13 of 42 · Curr4 block.)*

**The Isomorphic Federation → The Quality-Gated Network.** A federation of institutions whose curriculum admission is reviewed by member institutions reviewing each other converges toward mutual accommodation — the federation's quality becomes the average of its members, and the average is lower than the best any of them offered alone. The counter-design sets a non-negotiable quality standard enforced by an independent Design Authority, not by peer institutions grading each other. *(Pair 14 of 42 · Curr4 block.)*

## 7. Coherence Without Rigidity: Curriculum and Pathway Design

*Four patterns addressing the recurring tension between flexibility and orientation — modular, stackable learning that gives learners no way to see how their choices connect is not freedom, it is a catalogue; the counter-designs supply the map that makes genuine choice possible.*

**The Committee Curriculum → The Design Authority.** Curriculum decided by a committee of institutional representatives optimises for political acceptability among competing constituencies, not for pedagogical soundness — because the expertise the decision actually requires isn't in the room. The counter-design separates design authority (held by learning-science expertise) from governance authority (held by institutional accountability), explicit and enforced, not informal. *(Pair 15 of 42 · Curr4 block.)*

**The Loose Thread → The Coherent Path.** Modular and stackable study options without a structural mechanism for showing how choices connect leave learners choosing by availability or convenience rather than by any visible developmental logic — flexibility experienced as confusion, not freedom. The counter-design builds a Competency Map and a personal, growing Competency Profile for every learner, so the next choice is informed rather than arbitrary. *(Pair 8 of 42 · Learn8 block.)*

**The Content Delivery Machine → The Concept Architecture.** Curriculum organised around content transmission rather than capability development produces students who accumulate information they don't understand how to use — most dangerously in Year 1, where the whole subsequent trust relationship is established. The counter-design specifies conceptual learning outcomes before any content is selected, with Year 1 designed as the institution's single most important pedagogical commitment. *(Pair 17 of 42 · Curr4 block.)*

**The Accidental Programme → The Strategic Sequence.** Programmes assembled by accumulating whatever courses are available, before any learning strategy exists, cohere for no one — not the learner trying to see a developmental arc, not the institution trying to explain what makes the programme distinctive. The counter-design follows a strict design sequence — purpose and identity, then learning/teaching/assessment strategy, then curriculum — so no course is designed before the strategy it must serve exists. *(Pair 18 of 42 · Curr4 block.)*

## 8. Institutional Identity, Belonging, and Public Mandate

*Three patterns about an institution's relationship to its own distinctiveness — to the wider ecosystem it sits in, to the specific societal community it belongs to, and to the internal communities that must feel like home without becoming closed.*

**The Gold Standard Trap → The Designed Role.** When one institutional type — typically the research-intensive university — becomes the aspirational model every institution measures itself against regardless of actual mission, the whole ecosystem converges toward sameness even as institutions remain structurally different underneath. The counter-design requires every institution to hold an explicit Role Statement — what it does that others don't, for whom — and to be assessed against that role rather than a universal standard. *(Pair 19 of 42 · 7org block. The OUG's own Role Statement is a direct, working instance of this pattern.)*

**The Placeless Institution → The Embedded University.** An institution that understands its mandate purely in terms of academic production, with no specific named relationship to the city or region it inhabits, cannot say what would actually be lost if it closed — and therefore cannot make a constitutional, rather than merely competitive, claim on public resources. The counter-design requires the institution to answer, with specificity and constitutional grounding, what this city and society gain from it that no comparable institution provides. *(Pair 32 of 42 · Person 1 block, retrospectively derived.)*

**The Comfortable Silo → The Home That Challenges.** A unit with strong internal culture and no designed exposure to outside intellectual friction calcifies — insularity that feels, from inside, exactly like healthy community. The counter-design requires every unit to maintain a Permeability Architecture: designed interfaces with the wider intellectual environment, reviewed alongside the unit's academic plan, not left to the hope that strong culture stays open on its own. *(Pair 21 of 42 · 7org block. The OUG's own Mission Hub Permeability Architecture takes its name directly from this pattern.)*

## 9. Governance Adaptability and the Capacity to Change

*Two patterns about what happens when an institution's own governance structures, or its capacity for transformation specifically, are never revisited — accumulating the legitimacy of age rather than the ongoing justification of purpose.*

**The Frozen Structure → The Adaptive Institution.** Governance structures accumulate legitimacy through age until they are experienced as permanent fixtures rather than design choices — and the individual cost of disturbing any one of them is always more visible than the systemic cost of disturbing none of them. The counter-design maintains a Governance Review Calendar: every significant structure has a scheduled legitimacy review, confirmed, reformed, or retired on a defined cycle. *(Pair 20 of 42 · 7org block. The OUG's own Governance Review Calendar is a direct, named implementation of this pattern.)*

**The Orphaned Mandate → The Named Transformer.** When institutional transformation is everyone's implicit concern but nobody's explicit mandate, responsibility diffuses without ever accumulating enough force for structural change. The counter-design names a specific role or person as accountable for transformation, with real authority and a protected share of time, not an add-on to operational duties. *(Pair 28 of 42 · 6gov block.)*

## 10. Leadership as Accountable, Bounded Vocation

*Three patterns forming a complete leadership accountability architecture together: one addresses how leaders are selected and developed, one addresses whether anyone finds out afterward if the appointment is working, and one addresses whether the role itself has an exit that doesn't cost the leader their status.*

**The Accidental Leader → The Professionalised Vocation.** Filling governance roles by seniority, availability, or political negotiation — rather than by genuine leadership capacity and commitment — produces leaders who neither chose the role nor are equipped for it. The counter-design treats leadership as a distinct professional function with explicit selection criteria (covering both academic legitimacy and organisational capacity), development pathways, and career recognition. *(Pair 23 of 42 · 7org block. Directly implemented in the OUG's Leadership & Stewardship Profiles and its capability-measurement amendments.)*

**The Unreviewed Leader → The Evaluated Term.** Senior leadership held for years without systematic review means ordinary underperformance is tolerated indefinitely — no single incident is severe enough to trigger a formal process, even when the pattern is well known to everyone. The counter-design builds a mid-term and end-of-term review into every leadership appointment, with named goals set at appointment and four defined outcomes: renewal, conditional renewal, transition planning, or immediate transition. *(Pair 35 of 42 · Person 1 block, retrospectively derived. The book itself names this as sequential with Pair 23 — appointing well and reviewing afterward are both required; neither alone is sufficient.)*

**The Permanent Position → The Returnable Role.** When the only exit from a leadership role is retirement or forced removal, term limits — even when formally written down — never actually get used, because stepping down means losing status. The counter-design constitutionally limits terms and, just as importantly, structurally protects a genuine, status-preserving return pathway, so the end of a term is a normal governance event rather than a crisis to be managed. *(Pair 36 of 42 · Person 1 block, retrospectively derived.)*

## 11. Accountability Architecture: Audit, Outcome, and Ongoing Trajectory

*Three patterns addressing the difference between measuring whether an institution complied with a standard and measuring whether it is actually achieving — and continuing to move toward — its own stated purpose.*

**The Norm Audit → The Developmental Audit.** External quality review that measures conformity to input norms — staff numbers, square metres, contact hours — rewards compliance and is blind to both genuine excellence and genuine stagnation, since it can only see the presence or absence of the inputs it checks. The counter-design's three-part structure — Self-Assessment, expert peer panel, Development Report — asks whether the institution is achieving what it defined as its own purpose, not whether it matches an external template. *(Pair 24 of 42 · 7org block. The OUG's own Developmental Audit Framework is a direct implementation, sharing the same three-part structure.)*

**The Input Audit → The Outcome Measure.** Measuring module delivery and completion rates, rather than what learners can actually do afterward, rewards the appearance of teaching over its substance. The counter-design tracks two separate indicators — module delivery and demonstrated learning achievement, including longitudinal outcomes measured after completion — so delivery and learning are never conflated. *(Pair 27 of 42 · 6gov block. Directly named in the OUG's Amendment: Longitudinal Outcome Tracking, which shares this pattern's own gap code, GP-3.)*

**The Monitoring Trap → The Transformation Dashboard.** Performance dashboards that track current institutional health — enrolment, completion, satisfaction — answer "are we stable?" but never "are we becoming what we said we would become?", and the two questions require genuinely different instruments. The counter-design maintains two separate tracking systems rather than one blended one: health monitoring and transformation tracking, reviewed on different cycles by different governance layers. *(Pair 41 of 42 · Person 2 block, retrospectively derived. Directly implemented in the OUG's OKR Framework & Governance Dashboard, sharing this pattern's KPI-versus-OKR terminology.)*

## 12. Pedagogy and Community Without a Campus

*One pattern, standing alone because the failure it names — and its counter-design — operate at a genuinely different level than any other pair in this register: not a governance structure, but the deliberate design of the conditions a campus used to provide as an accident of physical proximity.*

**The Delivery Channel → The Presence Architecture.** An institution that moves teaching online without deliberately designing for cohort formation, intellectual community, and sustained engagement has simply removed physical co-location without replacing what it enabled — producing content delivery without the community dimension that makes an education more than an accumulation of modules. The counter-design specifies designed mechanisms — cohort formation, synchronous encounter with a real pedagogical purpose, beyond-session engagement, and now, where learners want it, local co-presence through cooperation spaces — each with a minimum standard and a review method. *(Pair 26 of 42 · 6gov block. The OUG's own Presence Architecture is a direct, named implementation, now extended with a fifth mechanism for local physical co-presence not present in the original pattern.)*

## 13. Strategy Formation and Governance Coherence

*Five patterns that the book itself describes as not independently useful — they form a single strategy-governance system, where implementing some without the others produces predictable gaps: a dashboard tracking a strategy nobody understands, or a well-prepared decision inside a system with no mechanism for correcting course.*

**The Decoupled Architecture → The Coherent System.** Governance structure and strategic intent can each be individually well-designed and still be misaligned with each other as a system — the most common reason reform efforts underdeliver, because they address one layer while leaving the others unchanged. The counter-design requires an explicit, periodically-verified account of whether the governance architecture and the strategy it is meant to carry actually cohere as one system. *(Pair 37 of 42 · Person 2 block, retrospectively derived. The book names the OUG's own Design Signature directly: "the record that verifies system coherence across the founding architecture.")*

**The Unsequenced Decision → The Prepared Decision.** Decisions made without adequate preparation — the information, the affected parties, the alternatives — produce outcomes that a properly staged process would have caught in advance. The counter-design stages every significant decision through defined preparation and coordination steps before ratification, so decisions are informed by design, not by whoever happened to raise the issue first. *(Pair 38 of 42 · Person 2 block, retrospectively derived. Directly implemented in the OUG's Governance & Decision Framework's staged G1–G4 decision architecture.)*

**The Extracted Strategy → The Owned Strategy.** Strategy developed by a small planning team and then delivered to the community that must implement it produces compliance, not the ownership that makes strategy survive contact with implementation. The counter-design builds strategy through a genuine, extended participatory process — harvesting, consolidation, iterative validation — that explicitly rejects survey-based "participation performance" in favour of real co-authorship. *(Pair 39 of 42 · Person 2 block, retrospectively derived. The OUG's own Strategy Formation Protocol matches this pattern's structure closely — same seven-phase logic, same explicit rejection of survey-based participation — though not its exact timeline: the pattern specifies up to two years, the Protocol eighteen months. Close, not identical; worth noting precisely rather than claiming an exact match.)*

**The Closed Loop → The Adaptive Cycle.** Institutions without a structural mechanism for detecting when strategy and reality have drifted apart keep executing a plan the world has already moved past. The counter-design builds a continuous feedback loop that treats a missed target as institutional learning to investigate, not individual failure to punish. *(Pair 40 of 42 · Person 2 block, retrospectively derived. The OUG's own OKR Framework's Bug Protocol uses the same "contribution, not failure" framing for missed targets.)*

**The One-Way Institution → The Three-Channel System.** Information that only flows downward — strategy to operations — leaves operational intelligence with nowhere to go, so implementation problems that were visible on the ground never reach the people who could correct the strategy. The counter-design builds three explicit channels — downward, upward, and lateral — so operational knowledge reaches governance and strategic direction reaches operations, in both directions, by design rather than by accident. *(Pair 42 of 42, the final pair in the catalogue · Person 2 block, retrospectively derived. The OUG's own Committee Information Architecture is named directly, by this exact pattern name, in the book's own comparative case study — with a concrete worked example: "practitioner intelligence from New Study has no designed channel to DHBW governance. The OUG's Three-Channel System routes it to governance as a design feature.")*

## Status of This Register

Forty-two patterns, thirteen thematic sections, re-sorted from the book's seven source blocks (Chapter 14 of the source manuscript) into groupings organised by what each pattern actually addresses rather than which dialogue produced it. Every entry below is grounded in the full primary-source text of its individual pattern page, read directly rather than summarised from an index — a deliberate choice, given how much this session's earlier work on the Principles Register turned up by insisting on primary text over secondary summary. Several entries already carry a direct note where this session's own work confirmed a named, working OUG implementation — those notes are preserved here because they were found during the reading that built this register, not retrofitted afterward. One correction worth flagging explicitly: Pair 36's own citation in the book points to a four-year Rector term in a document this repository has since corrected — the actual, current term is six to eight years under Grundordnung § 4 Absatz 1. The pattern's structural point (a genuine, status-protected return pathway) stands regardless; the specific number it once cited does not. One small imprecision corrected during the final read: Pair 39's own timeline (up to two years) is close to, but not identical with, the OUG Strategy Formation Protocol's eighteen months — noted precisely rather than smoothed into an exact match.

This register does not itself assess whether each pattern is currently built, partial, or absent in the OUG's actual governance architecture. That is a separate exercise — the same one already completed for the Principles Register in the Design Signature — and should follow this register as its own document, not be folded into it.
