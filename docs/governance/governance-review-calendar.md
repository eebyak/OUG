*Scheduled Legitimacy Reviews for Every Significant Governance Structure*

> **Why this document exists.** Governance structures accumulate legitimacy through age and familiarity. A committee that has existed for ten years is experienced as a natural feature of the institution rather than a design decision that can be revisited. The cost of changing any element is visible and immediate. The cost of not changing is invisible and deferred. Left unmanaged, this asymmetry produces institutional calcification — the Frozen Structure antipattern (AP-20).
>
> The Governance Review Calendar prevents this by making structural revision a normal, scheduled operation rather than a crisis. Every significant governance structure has a review date. At that date, the structure must justify its continued existence against its current purpose. Structures that pass are confirmed. Structures that fail are reformed or retired. New structures are created with explicit review triggers.

## I. The Review Process

Each governance structure review has three steps. First, the structure's current owners produce a one-page Legitimacy Statement: what is this structure for, what evidence shows it is still serving that purpose, and what would the institution lose if it did not exist? Second, a reviewer designated by the Hochschulrat (not the structure's own members) reads the Legitimacy Statement and the structure's record from the past cycle and produces a one-page Review Finding: confirmed, reformed (with specific required changes), or retired (with a transition plan). Third, the Hochschulrat endorses the Finding at its next scheduled meeting. The entire process takes no more than four weeks.

The review is not a performance evaluation of the people who serve in the structure. It is a legitimacy assessment of the structure itself. A structure may be served by excellent people and still be no longer fit for its purpose. Equally, a structure that is struggling may need better support rather than retirement. The review asks the structural question, not the personal one.

## II. The Calendar

The table below specifies every significant OUG governance structure, its review frequency, its next scheduled review date (anchored to the Year 1 operational start), and the body responsible for the review finding.

| Structure | Layer | Frequency | Legitimacy Criterion | Reviewer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hochschulrat (G1)** | G1 | Every 4 years | Is G1's composition still representative of the institutional ecosystem the OUG serves? Are its accountability functions being exercised? | External independent reviewer appointed by MWK |
| **Senate (G3)** | G3 | Every 3 years | Is the Senate's composition producing genuine academic voice? Are student participation mechanisms functioning? | G1 designates reviewer |
| **Community Circle** | G3 | Every 2 years *(initial cycle; may align to the Senate's 3-year cycle once established)* | Is the quarterly deliberation producing genuine strategic and academic input distinct from the Senate's formal votes? Is advisory participation by Mission Hub Stewards and affiliated academics substantive rather than token — is anything actually said in the Community Circle that changes what the Senate later decides? | Senate Chair reviews; G1 noting |
| **Quality Commission** | G3 | Every 3 years | Is the Stichprobe producing actionable quality intelligence? Is the Commission's independence from G2 being maintained? | G1 designates reviewer |
| **Mission Hub Steering Groups** | G3/4 | Every 2 years | Is the Hub still organised around a societal challenge with strategic relevance? Is it functioning as home and challenge? | Senate Chair reviews |
| **Mission Hub Permeability Architecture** | G3 | Annual *(the Permeability Review itself is annual by design; this row tracks the legitimacy of the mechanism, not the individual Hub reviews it produces)* | Are the four permeability mechanisms producing genuine cross-Hub encounter, or only its form (seminars attended without engagement, projects registered without substance)? Is any Hub showing two consecutive quarters without an active cross-Hub project? | Senate Chair reviews; escalates to Senate on concern |
| **Mission Hub Advisory Panels** | G3 | Every 2 years *(the annual Communiqué is the operational cycle; this tracks the mechanism's legitimacy, not each Hub's individual communiqué)* | Is the Duty to Voice actually being exercised — are Communiqués substantive and candid, or has any Panel become a silent circle? Are Steward responses to raised concerns actually being published within 60 days? | Senate Chair reviews |
| **Programme Development Forum** | Joint | Every 2 years | Is partner input genuinely influencing programme design? Is the Forum producing Decision Accounts? | Partnership Office Lead reviews |
| **External Cooperation Councils** | Joint | Annual | Is input from partner HEIs, Dual Partners/employers, public/regional stakeholders, and professional/societal bodies genuinely reaching the Community Circle, or does it stop at collection? Is the four-category distinction (Governance Architecture § 4.3) still meaningful in practice, or have distinct stakeholder types collapsed into one undifferentiated advisory pool? | Partnership Office Lead reviews; Senate Chair noting |
| **Senate Committees** | G3 | Every 2 years | Is each committee's mandate distinct and non-duplicative? Is it producing outputs that feed governance decisions? | Senate Chair reviews |
| **Partnership Office** | G4 | Every 3 years | Is the Partnership Office functioning as intelligence function (not only compliance)? Is the three-layer crossing architecture operational? | G2 reviews with G3 input |
| **Bug Protocol** | G4/OKR | Annual | Is the Protocol producing structural responses (not individual blame)? Is it being used or avoided? | Rector reviews; G1 noting |
| **Governance Mentor Pool** | Cross-layer | Every 2 years | Are mentors providing genuine development support? Is the pool composition still relevant to current role-holder needs? | G2/G3 joint review |

> **Note:** the "Bug Protocol" row references a mechanism that hasn't been located as a standalone document anywhere in this repository — it's tracked in [known-gaps.md](../known-gaps.md) under Governance as one of the items chapter 17 expected to find but couldn't confirm. This Calendar entry is further evidence the mechanism is real and referenced consistently, even though the document itself remains unfiled or unwritten.
>
> **Note on the Community Circle row:** this structure was established in [Grundordnung](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/grundordnung.md) § 5 Absatz 14 and detailed in the [Governance & Decision Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-architecture.md) §§ 4.3 and 5.2, as a deliberate response to Senate meetings functioning as approval formalities rather than genuine strategic deliberation. Because it is new, its first review is scheduled at 2 years rather than matched immediately to the Senate's 3-year cycle — the shorter initial interval is intentional, so that if the Community Circle degrades into the same signature-moment pattern it was created to avoid, that failure is caught early rather than in three years' time.

## III. Unscheduled Reviews

Any governance structure may be submitted for an unscheduled review when: a significant external change occurs (new legislation, new accreditation requirements, significant change in the institution's learner population or partner network); three or more governance participants formally request a review in writing to the Hochschulrat; or the Rector or Senate Chair identifies a structural concern that cannot wait for the scheduled review date.

Unscheduled reviews follow the same three-step process as scheduled reviews. The Hochschulrat may commission an unscheduled review on its own initiative at any time. An unscheduled review does not reset the scheduled review clock — the next scheduled review proceeds as planned unless the Hochschulrat explicitly changes the schedule.
