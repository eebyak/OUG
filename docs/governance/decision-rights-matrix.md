> **Position in the document architecture:** this matrix consolidates the nine Decision Domains defined in prose in the [Governance & Decision Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-architecture.md) § 3 into a single operational table. It does not create new authority — every entry below is derived from, and should be read subordinate to, the prose definitions in that document and the statutory provisions of the [Grundordnung](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/grundordnung.md). Where this table and the prose sections appear to diverge, the prose sections and the Grundordnung prevail, and this table should be corrected.

## How to Read This Table

- **Final authority** — the single body that formally decides (Governance Architecture § 3's "decision authority").
- **Agenda / co-creation** — the participatory bodies with a right to place items on the agenda or develop options, per Governance Architecture § 4.
- **Mandatory consultation** — bodies that must be consulted before the decision is finalised, though without veto power (Governance Architecture § 4.2).
- **Execution** — the body responsible for implementation once decided.
- **Escalation route** — which route in the [Conflict Escalation Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/conflict-escalation-framework.md) applies if this domain's decisions are disputed.
- **Governing instrument** — the statute or framework section the entry is grounded in.

**Note on D-G.** This is the one domain where "final authority" is genuinely split rather than singular, and the table format should not obscure that: G2 sets *strategic allocation priorities*, while *budget preparation, adoption, execution, and control* remain governed by statute and the Kanzler/in's role. See Governance Architecture § 3.7 and § 2.5 for the full explanation — this is a case where reading only the table would be misleading, which is exactly the risk a table format introduces and the prose sections are written to prevent.

## Constitutional Risk Flags

Following the flagging convention in the Strategic Constitution's Anlage 2 (Strategic Decision Protocol, Step 5), any proposal that would reassign a domain's final authority away from the body listed above — without going through the [Amendment Procedure](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/amendment-procedure.md) — should be treated as a constitutional risk and escalated via Route 5 (Constitutional Escalation) regardless of how the proposal is otherwise framed.
