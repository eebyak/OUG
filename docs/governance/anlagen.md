## Anlage 1: Strategisches Rahmenwerk

*First Principles and Derived Institutional Capabilities*

Per Grundordnung § 16 Absatz 2, this Anlage concretises the Präambel and the institutional guiding principles and derived capabilities laid down in § 1, § 8, and § 9 — the constitutional statement of *why* the institution is shaped the way it is.

Six components were specified for this Anlage: First Principles, Derived Institutional Capabilities, Design Principles, a constitutional interpretation clause, the relationship to future strategy documents, and an amendment procedure.

**First Principles, Derived Institutional Capabilities, and Design Principles — deferred to the book, by decision.** These live in *Designing a University from First Principles* — the dialogue-derived principles, the founding commitments, and the pattern catalogue were deliberately scoped there rather than into this document repository (see Chapter 17). Rather than half-extract that material into a repository stub, this Anlage simply defers to the book as the authoritative source for these three components. If a citable, repository-native version is ever needed for a specific formal process (e.g. an actual accreditation submission), that's a distinct future task — not something to improvise here.

**Explicitly out of scope for this Anlage:** the Design Signature / 42-pattern maturity scoring (the material scoring the OUG's governance architecture against the antipattern catalogue, and testing it against the New Study / DHBW case). That material belongs to the book's own argument about institutional maturity, not to a governance statute annex — bringing it into this directory would misplace it. Noted here so it isn't quietly proposed again later.

**Constitutional interpretation clause.** No separate clause is drafted here — [Grundordnung § 16 Absatz 3](grundordnung.md#-16-schlussbestimmungen) already states the interpretive rule governing this Anlage and Anlage 2: the Grundordnung's own wording controls wherever it speaks conclusively; the Anlagen govern everything else as binding concretisation and interpretive aid.

**Relationship to future strategy documents.** This Anlage sits above the Strategy & Finance domain's own documents. A future strategy document (Strategic Constitution, Role Statement, Strategy Formation Protocol, OKR Framework) sets direction within a planning cycle; this Anlage's First Principles and Design Principles are the fixed points that direction is set *against* — changing on constitutional-amendment timescales, not strategic-cycle timescales. A strategy document that conflicts with this Anlage yields, not the reverse. Mirrors the G1–G4 layering in [Governance & Decision Framework § 2](governance-architecture.md), where Constitutional Stewardship (G1) sits above Strategic Steering (G2) on the same logic.

**Amendment procedure.** Adopts the procedure specified in the **[Amendment Procedure](amendment-procedure.md)** document — proposal, Rektorat preparation and routing, Hochschulrat constitutional-alignment review, Senate consultation where academic domains are affected, joint Senat/Hochschulrat adoption, documentation and versioning. **This corrects the previous version of this page**, which cited a Federated Legal Model § 21 procedure involving a "Constitutional Council" — that procedure and that body no longer exist; the Federated Legal Model now incorporates the same dedicated Amendment Procedure document by reference (Federated Legal Model § 18) rather than maintaining its own separate process. Not a lighter, separate procedure — this Anlage's constitutional status under § 16 means it changes the same way the rest of the constitutional documents do.

## Anlage 2: Strategic Decision Protocol

Per Grundordnung § 16 Absatz 2, this Anlage concretises *"die Grundsätze der Entscheidungszuordnung, Entscheidungsvorbereitung und Eskalation für die Organe und Strukturen dieser Grundordnung"* — the constitutional *how*. Built as a binding index: each required component gets the governing rule in a sentence or two, and a link to the document where it's fully specified, per Grundordnung § 16 Absatz 3.

### 1. The G1–G4 Governance Layers

Constitutional (G1), Strategic (G2), Academic & Quality (G3), and Operational (G4) — exclusive competencies, no silent overlap.
**Specified in:** [Governance & Decision Framework § 2](governance-architecture.md) (the four layers, defined functionally) and § 2.5 (the Institutional Mapping — which body occupies which layer, grounded directly in Grundordnung §§ 3–8). Cross-referenced in [Governance Gap Closure v2](governance-gap-closure-v2.md) and [Committee Information Architecture](committee-information-architecture.md). *(Corrected: previously cited "Federated Legal Model § 13," which no longer exists in that document — the G1–G4 layer definitions were consolidated into Governance & Decision Framework rather than duplicated across two documents.)*

### 2. Decision Classes

Four classes (A–D) — local/reversible through normative/institution-wide — scaling governance effort to impact and risk.
**Specified in:** [Portfolio Governance § 3, Change Classification](portfolio-governance.md) — ✅ now converted; this was the open gap in the previous draft of this Anlage, resolved.

### 3. Escalation Principles

Findings and disputes move upward through defined tiers, each with a named responsible body, timeline, and required response.
**Specified in:** the **[Conflict Escalation Framework](conflict-escalation-framework.md)** — six distinct routes (horizontal coordination, competence-boundary review, academic escalation, administrative/legal escalation, constitutional escalation, and individual legal remedies), each with its own first step and resolving body, rather than a single undifferentiated ladder. Also the [Inter-Layer Feedback Protocol](interlayer-feedback-protocol.md) for how implementation findings specifically travel upward (signal classification, Constitutional Concern Protocol). *(Corrected: previously cited "Federated Legal Model § 14 (three-tier Mission Hub → Rectorate → Constitutional Council structure)" — that three-tier ladder and the Constitutional Council were both retired; conflicts now route through the six-route framework above, incorporated by reference in Federated Legal Model § 17.)*

### 4. Subsidiarity

Decisions are made at the lowest governance layer competent to make them.
**Specified in:** [Portfolio Governance § 1.3, Design Principles](portfolio-governance.md) — named explicitly as a Portfolio Governance design principle. Also present implicitly in the Mission Hub structure ([Grundordnung § 8](grundordnung.md#-8-mission-hubs-fakultäten)) and the Task Architecture's OUG/Partner/Joint split ([Governance Gap Closure v2](governance-gap-closure-v2.md)). Whether a portfolio-scoped statement is sufficient for this Anlage's institution-wide purpose, or whether subsidiarity deserves a broader constitutional statement, is worth a decision — see [conversion-notes.md](conversion-notes.md).

### 5. Constitutional Override Rules

Financial, executive, or strategic authority may not override academic standards or constitutional commitments.
**Specified in:** [Federated Legal Model § 4(3)](federated-legal-model.md) (the corrected dual-domain-authority principle — a body may hold functions across layers, but each individual decision has one domain and one authority) and [§ 5(3)](federated-legal-model.md) (no external body, partner, or executive office may override a G3 academic decision except through the Conflict Escalation Framework). Operationalised via [Governance Gap Closure v2 (HP-5, The Protected Strategy)](governance-gap-closure-v2.md) and the [Decision-Rights Matrix](decision-rights-matrix.md), which assigns exactly one final authority per domain. *(Corrected: previously cited "Federated Legal Model § 13(6), Non-Overlap Clause and Part VIII, § 18–19" — none of that content exists at those locations in the restructured document; this principle now lives in Part II, § 4–5.)*

### 6. Academic Independence

Academic and quality decisions sit in G3, structurally insulated from financial and executive override.
**Specified in:** [Federated Legal Model § 5, Academic Sovereignty and G3 Authority](federated-legal-model.md), and Governance & Decision Framework's own D-D/D-E/D-F domain assignments (all G3, per the [Decision-Rights Matrix](decision-rights-matrix.md)). Reinforced in [Governance Gap Closure v2](governance-gap-closure-v2.md) and [Committee Information Architecture](committee-information-architecture.md). *(Corrected: previously cited "Federated Legal Model § 18 and § 2–3" — § 18 is now the Amendment incorporation clause, not academic-independence content; the substantive material moved to § 5 during restructuring.)*

### 7. Feedback Protocol

Implementation findings travel upward in structured form; each layer has a defined obligation to respond.
**Specified in:** [Inter-Layer Feedback Protocol](interlayer-feedback-protocol.md) in full.

### 8. Portfolio Governance Interaction

Decision classes (Component 2) are Portfolio Governance's classification system; this Protocol routes each class to the correct governance response.
**Specified in:** [Portfolio Governance](portfolio-governance.md), particularly § 4, Routing and Escalation Logic — ✅ now converted, this component is fully resolved.
