*Open University of Germany · Institutional Foundations · 2026*

> **Status:** *(to be set — see [Document Status](../index.md#document-status))* · **Version:** v3.0
> **What this document is, and how it differs from its two companions.** The [Design Pattern Register](design-pattern-register.md) catalogues all 42 patterns. The [Pattern Application Log](pattern-application-log.md) asks the deliberate-choice question: which patterns were adopted, and which were left thin on purpose. This document asks a third, different question: **for the patterns that were adopted, how well are they actually implemented — and what would it look like if the institution started sliding back toward the antipattern each one exists to prevent?** That second half is new to this document specifically. A pattern can be Built in the sense of fully specified and still be vulnerable to a slow, undramatic slide back into exactly the dysfunction it was designed to structurally prevent — patterns don't self-enforce, they just make the failure mode visible sooner if someone is watching for it.
> **Organising scheme.** This document follows the taxonomy from the OUG Pattern Architecture diagram directly, rather than inventing a fourth structure: the Architectural tier; four Structural Pattern Families (Authority Architecture, Membership & Access, Roles & Leadership, Participation & Collegiality); five Dynamic Pattern Families (Decision Flow, Learning & Adaptation, Strategy & Transformation, Learner & Curriculum Flow, Institutional Permeability); and the five Cross-Cutting Spines the diagram identifies as influencing every other layer.
> **How to read a maturity marker.** ● Built — structurally specified and, where noted, load-bearing in actual statute or governance text. ◑ Partial — adopted in principle, not yet fully operationalised. Every entry carries an **Antipattern-Slip Warning**: the specific early signal that would indicate drift back toward the pattern's paired dysfunction, not a generic reminder to "stay vigilant."

## 0. Founding Principles — What the Whole System Answers To

Not patterns themselves, but the three commitments every pattern below ultimately serves: Learning Is the Goal, The Learner Directs the Learning Relationship, What Is Available to Learn Follows Learner Need. See the [Principles Register](principles-register.md), Section 0, for the full statement. Every antipattern-slip warning below is, at bottom, a warning that one of these three is quietly being deprioritised.

---

## 1. Architectural Patterns — What Kind of Institution This Is

**DP-25 The Design-First Institution.** ● Built, and practiced. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* the first sign of sliding back toward The Constraint-First Institution is not a bad decision — it's a *phrase*: "we can't do that because..." offered before the design question has actually been asked. Watch for design conversations that start with the budget or the org chart rather than with learner need.

**DP-32 The Embedded University.** ● Built, extended this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Placeless Institution returns quietly when the External Cooperation Councils or Mission Hub Advisory Panels stop producing anything that changes a decision — when their Communiqués become a formality logged and filed rather than genuinely read. The Governance Review Calendar's own criterion for the Advisory Panels already names this exact risk.

**DP-19 The Designed Role.** ● Built. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Gold Standard Trap creeps back when OUG's own language starts borrowing prestige metrics from research-intensive universities — publication counts, rankings — rather than the outcome measures the Role Statement actually commits to.

**DP-31 The Cooperative Cluster.** ● Built, foundational. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Disciplinary Island returns the moment a Mission Hub's actual staffing and course content start clustering around one discipline's home department in practice, even while the Hub's name still references a societal challenge on paper. The naming discipline in the Mission Hub Challenge Framework guards the label; it doesn't by itself guard the substance.

**DP-37 The Coherent System.** ● Built — this document's own family is the evidence. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Decoupled Architecture returns when a governance document is changed and its implications for strategy or implementation aren't checked in the same cycle — exactly the failure this session caught and fixed twice already (the `anlagen.md` regression, and the stale "four mechanisms" Presence Architecture reference). Recurrence of that specific failure pattern, not a new one, is the warning sign to watch for here.

**DP-1 / DP-12 The Designed Floor / The Permission Floor.** ● Built, closed this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Permission Illusion and its twin, the Compliance Ceiling, return the moment a claimed legal constraint goes unverified against primary statutory text — precisely what almost happened with the Kooperationsvereinbarung voting right before this session checked the LHG directly. The [Governance Compliance Map](governance-compliance-map.md) now exists as an actual artifact, seeded with three founding entries rather than starting empty, and the Amendment Procedure's Step 1a makes verification a gate a proposal cannot pass without, not a discretionary good habit. This is the pattern this document's own Section 5 named as the highest-leverage remaining gap; it no longer is one.

## 2. Structural Pattern Families

### 2.1 Authority Architecture

**DP-34 The Jurisdictional Map.** ● Built, extensively. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Blurred Boundary returns when a new governance mechanism is added without a Decision-Rights Matrix row — exactly the kind of gap this session found and fixed for the Kontaktstudium representation and the Mission Hub lifecycle additions. Any future addition that skips this step is the warning sign.

**DP-2 The Transparent Authority.** ● Built. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Paper Tiger with Claws returns when a body's *practiced* weight in a decision quietly diverges from its formal Decision/Consent/Advisory classification — for example, if the Community Circle's advisory recommendations started being treated as binding without the Senate ever formally adopting them.

**DP-16 The Unbundled Role.** ● Built. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Overloaded Academic returns if the boundary the Federated Legal Model draws — delivery responsibility versus academic authority — gets treated as a formality rather than enforced when a partner's role in practice starts drifting into curriculum design territory.

**DP-15 The Design Authority.** ● Built (specified) / ◑ Partial (operational). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Committee Curriculum returns the moment curriculum admission decisions start being made by consensus among Mission Hubs rather than by the (not-yet-appointed) Design Authority against its standard — a real risk precisely because the body doesn't exist yet to prevent informal committee decision-making from filling the vacuum.

### 2.2 Membership & Access

**DP-14 The Quality-Gated Network.** ◑ Partial. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Isomorphic Federation returns if partner course admission, in the absence of an appointed Design Authority, starts being decided by mutual accommodation among partner institutions instead — the exact failure mode this pattern exists to prevent, and the one this institution is currently most exposed to given DP-15's operational gap.

**DP-11 The Designed Entry.** — Not yet adopted as a document. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* not applicable until built; noted here as the pattern to watch once the prior-learning entry process is specified, since The Standard Gate is the default state, not a regression from something better.

**DP-7 The Designed Profile.** — Not yet adopted as a document. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* same status as DP-11 — this is a founding gap, not yet a regression risk.

### 2.3 Roles & Leadership

**DP-3 The Bounded Role.** ◑ Partial. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* Role Overflow returns whenever a governance role absorbs a task with no designed home rather than routing it through the Unclassified-task protocol — watch specifically for tasks accumulating on the Kanzler/in's or a Steward of Missions' desk without a documented assignment decision.

**DP-23 The Professionalised Vocation.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Accidental Leader returns if a future governance role is filled by seniority or availability rather than the PRECEPTA competency criteria — watch appointment processes specifically for a return to "who is willing" over "who is qualified."

**DP-35 The Evaluated Term.** ● Built, closed this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Unreviewed Leader returns not through the review being skipped outright — Grundordnung § 4 Absatz 4 makes that visible and documented — but through the review becoming *formal without being substantive*: a goal picture so vague that any outcome satisfies it, or a mid-term evaluation that always concludes "uneingeschränkte Fortsetzung" regardless of evidence. The statute's own safeguard (an absent or purely formal review is itself subject to Hochschulrat scrutiny) is aimed precisely at this softer failure mode, not just the harder one of skipping it.

**DP-36 The Returnable Role.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Permanent Position returns if the return pathway after a leadership term is nominally available but practically costs status or income — watch whether anyone ever actually uses it, not just whether it's written down.

### 2.4 Participation & Collegiality

**DP-6 The Crossing Point.** ● Built, the most heavily used pattern this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Fault Line returns, boundary by boundary, if any one of the four Crossing Points this session built — Community Circle, External Cooperation Councils, Mission Hub Advisory Panels, Programme Development Forum — stops producing a documented Decision Account and becomes a meeting that happens without changing anything downstream.

**DP-33 The Collegial Order.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Status Hierarchy returns if committee composition or discussion weight starts tracking academic rank again rather than relevant expertise — the Code of Collegial Conduct names this risk directly.

## 3. Dynamic Pattern Families

### 3.1 Decision Flow

**DP-38 The Prepared Decision.** ● Built, foundational. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Unsequenced Decision returns if a significant decision skips its G1–G4 staged sequence under time pressure — the exact scenario the Conflict Escalation Framework's Route differentiation exists to prevent from becoming the default excuse.

**DP-22 The Quality Mechanism.** ● Built. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Participation Façade returns if a Programme Development Forum Decision Account starts being produced as a formality — consultation logged, but the stated rationale never actually engaging with what was heard.

**DP-30 The Accountable Decision.** ● Built (in substance, looser in form — see Pattern Application Log). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Participation Decoy returns if the Decision-Rights Matrix's single-accountable-authority-per-domain principle is honoured on paper but a poor outcome is nonetheless attributed to "the process" rather than the named authority.

**DP-5 The Designed Task.** ● Built. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Proximity Task returns the moment an Unclassified task is quietly absorbed by whoever is nearest instead of being routed to the Partnership Office within the ten-working-day window the Task Architecture specifies — this is a countable, auditable signal, not a vague cultural one.

**DP-42 The Three-Channel System.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The One-Way Institution returns if the Committee Information Architecture's designed lateral overlaps stop being populated — committees that used to share members quietly stop, and nobody notices because no single meeting looks wrong in isolation.

### 3.2 Learning & Adaptation

**DP-20 The Adaptive Institution.** ● Built, extensively built this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Frozen Structure returns if a Governance Review Calendar review becomes a rubber-stamp confirmation every cycle — the Third Edition Design Signature already names this exact risk for the Calendar's own first cycle, not yet run.

**DP-40 The Adaptive Cycle.** ● Built, actively used this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Closed Loop returns if a missed Key Result stops triggering the Bug Protocol's Flag→Diagnose→Classify sequence and instead just gets quietly revised downward at the next OKR cycle without the diagnostic step ever happening.

**DP-41 The Transformation Dashboard.** ● Built, actively used this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Monitoring Trap returns if OKR review sessions start being run with the same tone and cadence as ordinary KPI health checks — the two are supposed to be qualitatively different governance conversations, and the pattern's own text warns this distinction erodes easily if not actively protected.

**DP-24 The Developmental Audit.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Norm Audit returns if the first actual Developmental Audit, due at the end of Year 1, ends up structured around input compliance (staff counts, contact hours) rather than the Self-Assessment/peer-panel/Development-Report structure it specifies.

**DP-27 The Outcome Measure.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Input Audit returns if the Longitudinal Outcome Tracking amendment's post-completion indicators quietly stop being collected once the pressure of founding-phase reporting eases.

### 3.3 Strategy & Transformation

**DP-39 The Owned Strategy.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Extracted Strategy returns if a future strategy cycle compresses the participatory harvesting phase under time pressure — the Register already flags that the OUG's own eighteen-month timeline is shorter than the pattern's own "up to two years," which makes this compression risk slightly more live here than the pattern's general case.

**DP-28 The Named Transformer.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Orphaned Mandate returns if the Rector's Transformation Mandate becomes an additional duty absorbed into ordinary operational leadership rather than a protected, separately reported function.

**DP-29 The Converted Incentive.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Untranslated Incentive returns if a future external funding opportunity is pursued with a structural proposal drafted before the Incentive Conversion Statement's three questions are actually answered — sequence matters here, not just whether the Statement eventually gets written.

**DP-4 The Need-Based Allocation.** ◑ Partial. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Year-End Flush is the default failure mode this pattern hasn't yet been built out enough to fully prevent — watch fourth-quarter spending patterns specifically, once the institution has an actual annual budget cycle to observe.

### 3.4 Learner & Curriculum Flow

**DP-8 The Coherent Path.** ● Built (pre-existing, curriculum domain). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Loose Thread returns if the Competency Map stops being updated as new modules are added, so the map no longer actually reflects what a learner can navigate by.

**DP-9 The Visible Development.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Hidden Journey returns if module-level credential issuance ever becomes something a learner has to request rather than something that happens automatically on verified completion.

**DP-10 The Active Member.** ◑ Partial. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Passive Consumer returns specifically at the asynchronous-participation gap this pattern is already marked Partial for — a learner engaging at 11pm with no designed channel into Senate agenda-setting is, structurally, already this antipattern's early form.

**DP-13 The Purposeful Entry.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Doorstep Fallacy returns if enrolment growth is ever reported without learning-outcome data alongside it in the same document.

**DP-17 The Concept Architecture.** ● Built (pre-existing, curriculum domain). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Content Delivery Machine returns if Year 1 module design ever gets specified by content coverage before conceptual learning outcomes are defined — a sequencing failure, not a content-quality one.

**DP-18 The Strategic Sequence.** ● Built (pre-existing). *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Accidental Programme returns if a new programme's course design begins before its learning/teaching/assessment strategies are finalised — again a sequencing tell, not a quality one.

### 3.5 Institutional Permeability

**DP-21 The Home That Challenges.** ● Built, extensively built this session. *Antipattern-Slip Warning:* The Comfortable Silo returns if a Mission Hub's Permeability Statement starts reporting the same cross-Hub seminar and project activity every cycle without any indication that outside perspectives actually changed anything internal — form without substance, the exact distinction Section III of the Permeability Architecture itself already warns about.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Spines

*The five patterns the diagram identifies as influencing every other layer — not because they're more important than the rest, but because their failure doesn't stay contained to one tier. A slip in any of these five is worth treating as a whole-system signal, not a local one.*

**DP-37 The Coherent System** — ensures whole-system coherence and alignment. Already detailed in Section 1 above; repeated here because its slip warning applies system-wide, not only architecturally.

**DP-6 The Crossing Point** — connects actors across every boundary in the institution. Already detailed in Section 2.4 above.

**DP-20 The Adaptive Institution** — keeps every structure revisable and open to change. Already detailed in Section 3.2 above.

**DP-40 The Adaptive Cycle** — turns evidence into improvement and change, system-wide. Already detailed in Section 3.2 above.

**DP-1 / DP-12 The Designed Floor / Permission Floor** — provides the legal and permission boundary for every choice made anywhere in the institution. Already detailed in Section 1 above; the one spine pattern that moved from Partial to Built within this document's own lifetime, once the Governance Compliance Map was actually built rather than only described.

**Why these five specifically, and what it means that they recur.** Each one appears in this document once already, under its home tier — the diagram's insight is that they should be read a second time, together, as a single cross-cutting question: *is the institution still coherent, still connected, still revisable, still learning from its own evidence, and still honest about the difference between what the law requires and what it chose?* A "yes" to all five individually does not guarantee a "yes" to that combined question — this is precisely why the diagram draws them as a separate spine rather than leaving them scattered across their home tiers with no signal that they deserve joint attention.

---

## 5. Status of This Signature

Of the patterns assessed here: the large majority sit at Built, several honestly at Partial, three genuinely not yet adopted as documents at all (DP-7's Learner Profile Map, DP-11's operational entry process, and — following this document's own recommendation — none remain from the original Cross-Cutting Spine gap, since the Governance Compliance Map closed DP-1/DP-12). This document does not round Partial up to Built, and does not treat "pre-existing, untouched this session" as equivalent to "verified this session" — several Built markers above rest on documents this session referenced but did not re-open and check line by line, which is a different and weaker form of confidence than the patterns this session actually rebuilt or extended.

The Antipattern-Slip Warnings are the actual point of this document, more than the maturity markers. A Built marker describes a snapshot. A slip warning describes a trajectory — the specific, checkable thing that would tell you the snapshot is going stale before the next full review catches it.
