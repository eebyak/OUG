This page records documents, inconsistencies, or unresolved decisions that affect the Governance domain.

Some items require existing constitutional and operating documents to be reconciled. Others concern implementing regulations that are referenced by the governance architecture but are not yet available in the repository.

**How to use this list:** for each item, determine whether it can be resolved by correcting an existing document, clarifying the relationship between legal and constitutional roles, or adding the required implementing instrument.

## Open

### Constitutional Architecture

- [ ] **Completion of Anlage 1** — [Anlage 1](anlagen.md) is described as a binding part of the Grundordnung but currently defers the First Principles, Derived Institutional Capabilities, and Design Principles to the OUG book. These constitutional principles should be stated or incorporated by reference in a repository-native, formally citable form before the Anlagen are used as binding statutory material. *Note: `anlagen.md` itself now states this is "resolved by decision" (defer to the book) rather than left open — but that decision doesn't fully satisfy this item's original ask for a repository-native, formally citable form. Left open here as a genuine tension between the two framings, not silently closed.*

### Mission Hubs and Academic Governance

- [ ] **Mission Hub operating framework** — Mission Hubs are recognised across the Grundordnung, Governance & Decision Framework, Portfolio Governance, quality architecture, and leadership documents. A consolidated operating framework has not yet been identified covering formation, dissolution, internal bodies, meetings, elections, delegated authority, reporting, programme responsibility, cross-Hub collaboration, and review. *(The Permeability Architecture and Advisory Panels documents now cover cross-Hub collaboration and external review specifically — see Resolved, below — but a consolidated operating framework covering the rest is still missing.)*

### Implementing Governance Regulations

- [ ] **Election and procedural regulations** — the Grundordnung refers to a Wahlordnung, Gremienordnung, Geschäftsordnung des Rektorats, Geschäftsordnung des Hochschulrats, internal administrative regulations, and an organisational statute for the student body. These instruments have not yet been identified in the repository and will be required to implement the constitutional bodies and election procedures in practice.

### Governance Assessments

- [ ] **Governance Principles Assessment update** — the [Governance Principles Assessment](governance-principles-assessment.md) predates several mechanisms now represented in the repository, including participatory strategy formation, structured inter-layer feedback, committee information architecture, the OKR framework, and leadership development. Its findings should be checked before the assessment is used as a current account of governance completeness. Partially addressed inline for P6, P8, P10, and P12 (see [conversion-notes.md](conversion-notes.md)); the remainder of the twelve principles have not been re-checked against this session's corrections specifically.

## Resolved

- [x] **G1 authority and institutional body** — previously: the Federated Legal Model assigned G1 authority to a separate Constitutional Council, while the Governance & Decision Framework presented the Hochschulrat as only a component of Constitutional Stewardship. **Resolved:** the Federated Legal Model no longer names a Constitutional Council anywhere; G1 is the Hochschulrat, stated explicitly in [Governance & Decision Framework § 2.5](governance-architecture.md) (the Institutional Mapping, grounded directly in Grundordnung §§ 3–8) and consistently referenced from there across the Conflict Escalation Framework, Amendment Procedure, Decision-Rights Matrix, and Federated Legal Model.

- [x] **Affiliated academic membership and electoral rights** — previously: the Federated Legal Model gave affiliated professors rights to vote in Senate elections and stand for academic office, without grounding in the Grundordnung's actual membership categories. **Resolved, with one flagged sub-question:** [Federated Legal Model §§ 7–8](federated-legal-model.md) now distinguishes three engagement models (Lehrauftrag, Freistellungsvereinbarung, OUG employment) with different governance consequences, grounded in LHG § 9 Absatz 1 and Grundordnung § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3 (which already grants full Wahlrecht to academics engaged "im Rahmen von Kooperationsvereinbarungen" — a pre-existing statutory provision this repository had not previously connected to the federation's affiliated academics). **One sub-question remains genuinely open and is stated as such in Federated Legal Model § 8(6):** whether this Wahlrecht places a Freistellungsvereinbarung academic inside the specific voting bloc LHG § 10 Absatz 1 Nummer 1 defines for mandatory Senate seat apportionment (which requires *hauptberuflich* status), or operates through a separate mechanism. Flagged for qualified legal review, not asserted as settled.

- [x] **Mission Hub composition and leadership eligibility** — previously: the Grundordnung restricts Mission Hub statutory membership and Steward of Missions eligibility to OUG-employed staff, while the Federated Legal Model assumed partner-employed professors would hold these roles. **Resolved by clarification, not by changing the Grundordnung's membership rules:** LHG § 22 Absatz 4 Satz 3 places a hard floor here — a co-opted (kooptiert) academic cannot become Dean/Steward of Missions "as such," regardless of how the Grundordnung is drafted. Affiliated academics were never meant to hold Mission Hub statutory membership; they participate through the Community Circle (Grundordnung § 5 Absatz 14, advisory or — for Freistellungsvereinbarung academics — full voting) and the Programme Development Forum instead. This is now stated explicitly in [Governance & Decision Framework § 5.4](governance-architecture.md).

- [x] **Portfolio Governance authority** — previously: Grundordnung § 9 was read as allowing academic changes to be governed through a Rectorate implementing order merely presented to the Senate for information, understating the Senate's statutory authority, while Portfolio Governance assigned decision authority to Mission Hubs, Rectorate, and Senate by change class. **Resolved:** Grundordnung § 9 was rewritten to require actual Mission Hub Befassung and, where the Senate's § 5 Absatz 1 competence is opened, its Beschlussfassung — not mere information — and to state explicitly that Portfolio Governance "regelt insoweit ausschließlich das Verfahren... [und] begründet keine von den §§ 5, 6 und 8 abweichenden Zuständigkeiten." On inspection, [Portfolio Governance](portfolio-governance.md)'s own Class C/D routing already deferred to "delegated authority" and "statutory responsibilities" rather than claiming independent authority — the documents turned out to be more consistent than this entry originally suggested. Portfolio Governance § 9's separate document-lettering scheme (Document A/B/C/D/E/G) was also corrected to name actual documents.

- [x] **Status reconciliation across governance assessments (HP-7)** — already resolved prior to this pass; recorded here for completeness. See [conversion-notes.md](conversion-notes.md): three documents (`governance-gap-closure-v2.md`, the partnership Task Architecture, and `partnership/amendment-lehrpersonalmodell.md`) independently confirm HP-7 (The Designed Task) as closed via the OUG/partner Designed Split; only `design-signature-founding.md` still lists it as an open committed gap, almost certainly a stale entry rather than a live disagreement.
