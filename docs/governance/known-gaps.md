This page records documents, inconsistencies, or unresolved decisions that affect the Governance domain.

Some items require existing constitutional and operating documents to be reconciled. Others concern implementing regulations that are referenced by the governance architecture but are not yet available in the repository.

**How to use this list:** for each item, determine whether it can be resolved by correcting an existing document, clarifying the relationship between legal and constitutional roles, or adding the required implementing instrument.

## Constitutional Architecture

* [ ] **Completion of Anlage 1** — [Anlage 1](anlagen.md) is described as a binding part of the Grundordnung but currently defers the First Principles, Derived Institutional Capabilities, and Design Principles to the OUG book. These constitutional principles should be stated or incorporated by reference in a repository-native, formally citable form before the Anlagen are used as binding statutory material.

* [ ] **G1 authority and institutional body** — the [Federated Legal Model](federated-legal-model.md) assigns G1 authority to a separate Constitutional Council. The [Governance & Decision Framework](governance-architecture.md) instead presents the Hochschulrat as a component of Constitutional Stewardship. The governance architecture must determine whether G1 is exercised by the Hochschulrat, a separate Constitutional Council, or a defined combination of bodies, and update escalation, amendment, oversight, and accountability provisions accordingly.

* [ ] **Affiliated academic membership and electoral rights** — the Federated Legal Model gives affiliated professors rights to participate in Mission Hubs, vote in Senate elections, and stand for academic office. The [Grundordnung](grundordnung.md) bases active and passive voting rights primarily on statutory membership categories and distinguishes these from persons who are merely associated with the university. The legal status, group allocation, electoral rights, and term of affiliation of partner-employed academics must be reconciled with the Grundordnung and the intended federated model.

## Mission Hubs and Academic Governance

* [ ] **Mission Hub composition and leadership eligibility** — the Grundordnung defines Mission Hub membership around OUG-employed staff and enrolled students and requires the Steward of Missions and deputy to be elected from among professors employed full-time at the OUG. The Federated Legal Model is built around partner-employed professors holding secondary OUG affiliation. The membership and leadership rules must be adjusted so that Mission Hubs can operate within the intended federated staffing model.

* [ ] **Mission Hub operating framework** — Mission Hubs are recognised across the Grundordnung, Governance & Decision Framework, Portfolio Governance, quality architecture, and leadership documents. A consolidated operating framework has not yet been identified covering formation, dissolution, internal bodies, meetings, elections, delegated authority, reporting, programme responsibility, cross-Hub collaboration, and review.

* [ ] **Portfolio Governance authority** — §9 of the Grundordnung provides that the detailed Portfolio Governance rules are issued by the Rectorate and presented to the Senate for information. The [Portfolio Governance Framework](portfolio-governance.md) assigns formal decision authority to Mission Hubs, the Rectorate, and the Senate depending on the class of change. The Grundordnung and the framework should be reconciled so that academic changes cannot be governed through an implementing order whose adoption route understates the Senate’s statutory authority.

## Implementing Governance Regulations

* [ ] **Election and procedural regulations** — the Grundordnung refers to a Wahlordnung, Gremienordnung, Geschäftsordnung des Rektorats, Geschäftsordnung des Hochschulrats, internal administrative regulations, and an organisational statute for the student body. These instruments have not yet been identified in the repository and will be required to implement the constitutional bodies and election procedures in practice.

## Governance Assessments

* [ ] **Status reconciliation across governance assessments** — the [Design Signature Founding](design-signature-founding.md) continues to describe HP-7, the formal Task Architecture, as an open committed gap. The [Governance Gap Closure v2](governance-gap-closure-v2.md) presents the designed OUG/partner task split as closing HP-7, while the partnership framework now contains a fuller Task Architecture. The status should be updated consistently across the assessment, gap-closure, partnership, and leadership documents.

* [ ] **Governance Principles Assessment update** — the [Governance Principles Assessment](governance-principles-assessment.md) predates several mechanisms now represented in the repository, including participatory strategy formation, structured inter-layer feedback, committee information architecture, the OKR framework, and leadership development. Its findings should be checked before the assessment is used as a current account of governance completeness.
