# PART I

## FOUNDATIONAL LEGAL IDENTITY OF THE OPEN UNIVERSITY OF GERMANY (OUG)

## DESIGN PRINCIPLES FOR SECTION I

1. OUG is a university in its own right.

2. OUG holds exclusive academic sovereignty over its degrees.

3. Employment and academic authority are legally separable.

4. Federation does not dilute degree authority.

5. Academic freedom is protected across the federation.

6. OUG governance layers are constitutionally binding.

7. No partner institution holds residual authority over OUG degrees.

## §1 Purpose and Scope

(1) The Open University of Germany (OUG) is a degree-awarding higher
education institution established as a federated academic entity
operating within the applicable Higher Education Law.

(2) OUG is constituted as an academically sovereign institution whose
purpose is:

- to design, govern, and award higher education degrees,

- to ensure academic integrity and quality assurance,

- to enable federated academic collaboration among partner institutions,

- to promote innovation in digital and modular higher education.

(3) OUG operates through a federated model in which partner higher
education institutions contribute teaching and disciplinary expertise.
Such contributions do not transfer degree-awarding authority unless
explicitly provided under joint-degree provisions defined in this
document.

(4) OUG distinguishes legally and operationally between:

- a) employment authority over academic staff,
- b) academic authority over curriculum and assessment,
- c) degree-awarding authority, and
- d) operational administration.

These domains are constitutionally allocated and may not be merged or
overridden except through formal constitutional amendment procedures.

## §2 Legal Status and Academic Sovereignty

(1) OUG is recognized as a higher education institution with full
authority to award academic degrees in its accredited programs.

(2) OUG holds exclusive responsibility for:

- academic standards,

- examination regulations,

- assessment integrity,

- quality assurance,

- issuance of degrees and diplomas,

- maintenance of academic records and registry.

(3) The authority to award degrees resides solely with OUG and is
exercised in the name of the institution.

(4) Degrees are conferred under the official seal of OUG and signed by
the President and Chancellor in their constitutional capacity as
institutional representatives.

(5) No partner institution retains residual authority over degrees
awarded by OUG except where joint or dual degree arrangements are
formally established in accordance with §6 of this document.

## §3 Academic Freedom and Institutional Safeguards

(1) OUG guarantees academic freedom in teaching, research, and
academic expression in accordance with the highest applicable standards
among participating partner institutions and national law.

(2) Participation in the OUG federation shall not diminish the
academic freedom protections afforded to affiliated professors under
their primary appointment.

(3) Academic standards and curriculum decisions shall be governed
exclusively through the Academic and Quality Governance Layer (G3), as
defined in the Governance & Decision Framework

(4) Executive bodies may not alter academic standards or examination
rules except through procedures established by the Senate.

# PART II

# ACADEMIC AUTHORITY STRUCTURE

DESIGN PRINCIPLES – ACADEMIC RESPONSIBILITY

1. Academic authority must follow degree authority.

2. Examination board appointment must follow degree authority.

3. Curriculum authority must follow Senate authority.

4. Partner HEIs retain employment authority.

5. Research autonomy remains with primary institution.

6. No shared veto in academic standards.

7. Consultation mechanisms allowed, but decision rights must be singular.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES – AFFILIATED PROFESSORS

1. Primary employment remains at Home HEI.

2. Secondary academic affiliation at OUG is statutory.

3. Affiliation requires formal contract.

4. Affiliation grants governance rights.

5. Affiliation includes duties (QA, standards compliance).

6. Academic freedom must be guaranteed.

7. Conflicts of interest must be disclosed.

8. Eligibility for examination boards must be clearly defined.

9. Termination must follow due process.

10. Affiliation duration must be fixed and renewable.

## §3 Academic Responsibility Allocation

### (1) Principle of Academic Sovereignty

The Open University of Germany (OUG) exercises exclusive academic
authority over all programs leading to degrees awarded in its name.

Academic authority shall be exercised through the Academic and Quality
Governance Layer (G3), as defined in the Governance & Decision Framework
.

No external body, partner institution, or executive office may override
decisions taken within this domain except through constitutional
amendment procedures.

### (2) Domains of Academic Authority

The OUG Senate, acting as the supreme academic body, holds exclusive
decision-making authority over:

- a) curriculum architecture and program design;
- b) learning outcomes and competency frameworks;
- c) assessment formats and grading systems;
- d) examination regulations and procedural rules;
- e) quality assurance standards and review mechanisms;
- f) degree requirements and academic thresholds;
- g) approval, modification, suspension, or termination of academic
programs.

These domains may involve consultation with partner institutions, but
final decision authority remains with the OUG Senate.

### (3) Examination Authority

(1) Examination boards for all OUG programs shall be appointed by the
OUG Senate or by a body explicitly mandated by it.

(2) Examination boards operate under OUG examination regulations and
are accountable exclusively to OUG academic governance.

(3) Appeals against examination decisions shall be adjudicated through
procedures established by OUG.

(4) Partner institutions may nominate eligible affiliated professors
for examination board service, but appointment authority remains with
OUG.

### (4) Quality Assurance Authority

(1) OUG establishes and maintains a central quality assurance
framework applicable to all federated programs.

(2) All courses contributing to OUG degrees must comply with OUG
academic standards, assessment regulations, and quality review
procedures.

(3) OUG retains audit rights over all courses and assessments
contributing to its degrees.

(4) Failure to comply with OUG academic standards may result in
suspension of course recognition for degree purposes.

### (5) Separation of Academic and Employment Authority

(1) Employment authority over affiliated professors remains
exclusively with their primary (home) higher education institution.

(2) Academic decision-making authority within OUG programs resides
exclusively with OUG governance bodies.

(3) Employment status at a partner institution does not confer
automatic academic decision authority within OUG.

(4) Academic authority within OUG does not imply employment authority,
contractual control, or disciplinary supervision over affiliated
professors.

### (6) Research Autonomy

Participation in OUG programs does not limit or transfer research
autonomy of affiliated professors within their primary institution.

OUG does not claim ownership over research output except where
separately agreed in specific collaborative research contracts.

## §4 Legal Status of Affiliated Professors

### (1) Dual Academic Affiliation Structure

(1) Professors contributing to OUG programs shall hold:

- a) a primary academic appointment at a recognized higher education
institution ("Home HEI"), and
- b) a secondary academic affiliation with OUG.

(2) Secondary affiliation with OUG is a formal academic status
established through written agreement.

(3) Secondary affiliation does not constitute an employment
relationship with OUG unless explicitly stated.

### (2) Academic Membership Contract

(1) Secondary affiliation shall be governed by an Academic Membership
Contract specifying:

- a) rights and duties;
- b) duration of affiliation;
- c) participation in governance;
- d) compliance with OUG academic standards;
- e) confidentiality and data protection obligations;
- f) conflict-of-interest disclosure requirements.

(2) The Academic Membership Contract shall align with OUG statutes and
examination regulations.

### (3) Rights of Affiliated Professors

Affiliated professors shall have the right to:

- a) participate in Mission Hubs;
- b) vote in OUG Senate elections, subject to representation rules;
- c) stand for election to academic bodies, where eligible;
- d) propose curriculum initiatives;
- e) serve on examination boards if appointed;
- f) access institutional academic resources necessary for program
delivery.

Voting rights shall be regulated to ensure balanced representation and
prevent dominance by any single partner institution.

### (4) Duties of Affiliated Professors

Affiliated professors shall:

- a) comply with OUG curriculum standards;
- b) adhere to OUG examination regulations;
- c) participate in quality assurance processes;
- d) maintain academic integrity in teaching and assessment;
- e) disclose potential conflicts of interest;
- f) participate in faculty development programs where required.

### (5) Academic Freedom

(1) Affiliated professors retain academic freedom protections
guaranteed under their primary appointment and applicable law.

(2) Participation in OUG programs shall not diminish academic freedom
in teaching, research, or academic expression.

(3) Academic freedom within OUG programs is exercised within the
framework of adopted curriculum and examination regulations.

### (6) Eligibility for Examination Board Service

(1) Affiliated professors may be nominated for examination board
service.

(2) Eligibility requires:

- a) appropriate academic qualification;
- b) active affiliation status;
- c) compliance with conflict-of-interest rules;
- d) completion of required OUG assessment training.

(3) Final appointment authority rests with OUG.

### (7) Duration and Renewal of Affiliation

(1) Secondary academic affiliation shall be granted for a defined
term.

(2) Affiliation may be renewed upon review of academic contribution
and compliance with OUG standards.

(3) Loss of primary appointment at a Home HEI shall trigger review of
OUG affiliation status.

### (8) Termination of Affiliation

(1) Affiliation may be terminated:

- a) upon expiry of term;
- b) by resignation;
- c) upon loss of eligibility;
- d) for material violation of OUG academic standards;
- e) for conflict-of-interest breaches;
- f) for sustained non-compliance with quality assurance obligations.

(2) Termination shall follow due process procedures defined in OUG
regulations.

(3) Termination of affiliation does not affect degrees already
awarded.

### (9) Conflict-of-Interest Safeguards

Affiliated professors shall disclose:

- institutional conflicts,

- financial interests,

- governance conflicts involving partner institutions.

OUG may restrict participation in specific decisions where conflict
exists.

## §5 Degree Authority

### (1) Exclusive Degree-Awarding Authority

(1) The Open University of Germany (OUG) holds exclusive authority to
confer academic degrees in all programs accredited under its
responsibility.

(2) Degrees awarded under OUG programs are conferred solely in the
name of OUG, except where a formally established joint or dual degree
arrangement applies pursuant to §6.

(3) The authority to confer degrees includes:

- a) determination of eligibility for graduation;
- b) verification of fulfillment of academic requirements;
- c) issuance of diplomas and transcripts;
- d) maintenance of official academic records;
- e) revocation of degrees in accordance with due process.

### (2) Signature Authority

(1) Degrees conferred by OUG shall bear:

- a) the official name of the Open University of Germany;
- b) the official institutional seal;
- c) the signature of the President;
- d) the signature of the Chancellor.

(2) The President and Chancellor sign degrees in their constitutional
capacity as representatives of the institution.

(3) No additional signatures from partner institutions shall appear on
OUG-only degrees.

### (3) Central Academic Registry

(1) OUG shall maintain a central and legally controlled academic
registry for all degrees awarded in its name.

(2) The registry shall include:

- a) final grades and examination outcomes;
- b) degree classifications;
- c) dates of award;
- d) archival examination records;
- e) documentation required for verification and authentication.

(3) OUG retains exclusive responsibility for:

- a) verification of degree authenticity;
- b) correction of academic records;
- c) certification copies;
- d) digital authentication systems;
- e) compliance with data protection laws.

(4) Partner institutions may not maintain parallel official registries
for OUG-awarded degrees.

### (4) Revocation Authority

(1) OUG retains authority to revoke degrees awarded in its name where:

- a) fraud is proven;
- b) academic misconduct materially affected eligibility;
- c) statutory conditions for revocation are met.

(2) Revocation shall follow due process procedures established in OUG
examination regulations.

## §6 Joint and Dual Degree Arrangements

### (1) Principle

(1) Joint or dual degree arrangements may be established only where
academic contributions from a partner institution meet the objective
threshold defined in this section.

(2) Joint or dual degree arrangements shall not be negotiated on a
discretionary or case-by-case political basis.

### (2) Objective Threshold Calculation

(1) A joint or dual degree arrangement may be established when more
than fifty percent (50%) of the total required ECTS credits of a program
are delivered and assessed under the academic responsibility of a single
partner institution.

(2) The calculation shall:

- a) be based on total ECTS credits required for the degree;
- b) include coursework and thesis components;
- c) exclude recognized prior learning or transferred credits not
delivered within the program;
- d) be calculated per cohort and per accredited program.

(3) The threshold calculation shall be documented and auditable.

(4) Rounding procedures shall be defined in the Examination Regulation
Annex.

### (3) Formal Agreement Requirement

(1) A joint or dual degree may only be established through a formal
written agreement between OUG and the partner institution.

(2) The agreement shall define:

- a) degree title;
- b) signature order and representation;
- c) applicable examination regulations;
- d) quality assurance coordination;
- e) registry coordination;
- f) revocation procedures;
- g) dispute resolution mechanism.

(3) No joint or dual degree may be issued without prior approval of
the OUG Senate.

### (4) Governance of Joint Degrees

(1) Even in joint or dual degree arrangements, OUG retains academic
authority over:

- a) curriculum standards;
- b) examination regulations;
- c) quality assurance procedures applicable to OUG-accredited programs.

(2) Liability for academic integrity shall be allocated according to
the formal agreement.

### (5) Registry and Archival Coordination

(1) OUG shall maintain a complete academic record of all students
enrolled in joint or dual degree programs.

(2) Where dual diplomas are issued, registry coordination procedures
shall ensure consistency of academic records.

(3) OUG shall retain authority to authenticate degrees bearing its
name.

## §7 Diploma Supplement and Transcript Governance

### (1) Central Issuance

(1) All diplomas, transcripts, and diploma supplements for OUG degrees
shall be issued exclusively by OUG.

(2) Partner institutions may not issue separate official transcripts
for OUG-only degrees.

### (2) Standardized Format

(1) Diplomas and diploma supplements shall follow a standardized
institutional format aligned with the European Higher Education Area
(EHEA) framework.

(2) The diploma supplement shall transparently describe:

- a) the federated structure of the program;
- b) participating partner institutions;
- c) distribution of ECTS credits;
- d) applicable examination regulations;
- e) quality assurance framework.

### (3) Description of Federated Contributions

(1) The diploma supplement shall identify courses delivered by partner
institutions without diluting OUG's degree-awarding authority.

(2) The structure shall clarify that OUG retains academic sovereignty
unless otherwise specified under joint degree arrangements.

### (4) Digital Authentication

(1) OUG shall implement secure digital verification systems for:

- a) degree authentication;
- b) transcript validation;
- c) registry access control.

(2) Digital verification systems shall comply with applicable data
protection and cybersecurity regulations.

## §8 Appointment and Governance of Examination Boards

### (1) Principle of Academic Appointment

(1) Examination boards for all programs leading to OUG degrees shall
be appointed by the OUG Senate or by a standing academic body explicitly
mandated by it.

(2) Appointment authority resides exclusively within the Academic and
Quality Governance Layer (G3).

(3) No executive officer, including the President or Chancellor, may
unilaterally appoint or remove examination board members.

### (2) Nomination Procedure

(1) Members of examination boards shall be nominated by the relevant
Mission Hubs.

(2) Nominations shall reflect academic expertise, disciplinary
competence, and representation across participating partner
institutions.

(3) Final appointment requires confirmation by the OUG Senate or its
delegated academic committee.

### (3) Eligibility Criteria

To serve on an examination board, a candidate must:

- a) hold active affiliation status with OUG;
- b) possess appropriate academic qualifications;
- c) demonstrate relevant disciplinary expertise;
- d) complete required assessment and moderation training;
- e) declare absence of conflicts of interest;
- f) comply with OUG examination regulations.

### (4) Term of Office

(1) Examination board members shall be appointed for a fixed term.

(2) Terms may be renewed subject to Senate approval.

(3) Terms shall be staggered where feasible to ensure institutional
continuity.

### (5) Removal Mechanism

(1) A member may be removed prior to expiration of term only for:

- a) material violation of examination regulations;
- b) conflict-of-interest breach;
- c) sustained failure to fulfill duties;
- d) loss of eligibility status.

(2) Removal requires due process and confirmation by the OUG Senate or
its delegated academic body.

### (6) Accountability

(1) Examination boards are accountable exclusively to OUG academic
governance bodies.

(2) Examination boards shall apply OUG examination regulations
uniformly across all federated programs.

## §9 Central Examination Regulation Annex

### (1) Binding Nature

(1) OUG shall establish a Central Examination Regulation Annex
applicable to all programs leading to OUG degrees.

(2) The Central Examination Regulation Annex is binding on all
affiliated professors and partner institutions contributing to OUG
programs.

(3) Local institutional examination rules shall not override OUG
examination regulations for OUG-accredited programs.

### (2) Assessment Types

The Annex shall define permissible assessment types, including but not
limited to:

- a) written examinations;
- b) oral examinations;
- c) project work;
- d) portfolios;
- e) theses;
- f) laboratory assessments;
- g) presentations;
- h) other forms approved by the Senate.

Assessment formats must align with defined learning outcomes.

### (3) Grading System

(1) OUG shall apply a unified grading scale across all programs.

(2) Grade boundaries, classifications, and conversion procedures shall
be defined centrally.

(3) Any international grade conversion shall follow transparent and
documented standards.

### (4) Moderation Procedures

The Annex shall define:

- a) double marking requirements where applicable;
- b) external moderation procedures;
- c) grade confirmation processes;
- d) documentation requirements;
- e) archiving procedures.

### (5) Retake Policy

(1) The Annex shall define:

- a) maximum number of retake attempts;
- b) conditions for progression;
- c) timelines for reassessment;
- d) consequences of failure.

(2) Retake rules shall apply uniformly across all federated programs.

### (6) Academic Misconduct Procedures

(1) The Annex shall define academic misconduct, including but not
limited to:

- a) plagiarism;
- b) unauthorized collaboration;
- c) fabrication or falsification;
- d) cheating during examinations;
- e) misrepresentation of authorship.

(2) Procedures shall define:

- a) investigation process;
- b) hearing rights;
- c) sanction levels;
- d) appeal rights;
- e) documentation standards.

(3) Sanctions shall be proportionate and centrally recorded.

### (7) Documentation and Record Retention

(1) Examination records shall be stored centrally within the OUG
registry.

(2) Retention periods shall comply with applicable law.

(3) Digital security and data protection standards shall be
maintained.

## §10 Appeals and Academic Disputes

### (1) Principle of Independent Review

(1) Appeals against examination decisions shall be heard by an
independent Appeals Body established by OUG.

(2) Members of the Appeals Body shall not have participated in the
original examination decision.

### (2) Jurisdiction

The Appeals Body shall have jurisdiction over:

- a) procedural irregularities;
- b) alleged grading errors;
- c) misconduct findings;
- d) sanctions imposed;
- e) other academic disputes defined by regulation.

### (3) Procedural Timeline

(1) Appeals must be submitted within a defined period following
notification of results.

(2) The Appeals Body shall issue a reasoned written decision within a
defined timeframe.

(3) Deadlines and procedural steps shall be specified in the
Examination Regulation Annex.

### (4) Final Authority

(1) Decisions of the Appeals Body constitute the final academic
decision within OUG.

(2) Executive bodies may not override appeal decisions.

(3) Constitutional disputes concerning interpretation of statutes may
be escalated to the Constitutional Stewardship Layer (G1), but not to
alter individual grading outcomes.

### (5) Judicial Review

(1) OUG decisions remain subject to judicial review under applicable
law.

(2) Internal appeal procedures must be exhausted prior to external
legal challenge, unless otherwise required by law.

## §11 Central Academic Standards Office

### (1) Establishment and Mandate

(1) The Open University of Germany (OUG) establishes a Central
Academic Standards Office (CASO).

(2) CASO is responsible for safeguarding the academic integrity,
equivalence, and continuous quality of all programs leading to OUG
degrees.

(3) CASO operates under the Academic and Quality Governance Layer (G3)
and reports directly to the OUG Senate.

(4) CASO shall act independently in its academic evaluation and audit
functions.

### (2) Core Responsibilities

CASO shall:

- a) monitor compliance with OUG curriculum and examination regulations;
- b) conduct periodic program reviews;
- c) evaluate equivalence of academic standards across partner
institutions;
- d) review grading distributions and assessment outcomes;
- e) oversee moderation processes;
- f) monitor academic progression and completion rates;
- g) identify systemic risks to academic integrity;
- h) recommend corrective measures to the Senate;
- i) coordinate external quality evaluations where required.

### (3) Audit Authority

(1) CASO is authorized to conduct academic audits of:

- a) individual courses;
- b) examination procedures;
- c) grading practices;
- d) assessment documentation;
- e) partner institution compliance with OUG standards.

(2) Partner institutions and affiliated professors shall cooperate
fully with audit procedures.

(3) CASO may request documentation, anonymized assessment samples,
moderation reports, and relevant academic data.

### (4) Review Cycle

(1) All OUG programs shall undergo formal academic review within a
defined review cycle.

(2) The review cycle shall include:

- a) internal evaluation;
- b) external academic input where appropriate;
- c) Senate review of findings;
- d) formal adoption of recommendations.

### (5) Corrective Measures

Where academic standards are found deficient, CASO may recommend to the
Senate:

- a) mandatory corrective action plans;
- b) enhanced monitoring;
- c) suspension of course recognition;
- d) temporary suspension of partner participation;
- e) other proportionate measures.

Only the Senate may impose program suspension or termination.

### (6) Reporting

(1) CASO shall submit an annual Academic Standards Report to the
Senate.

(2) A summary of findings may be made publicly available to promote
transparency and institutional accountability.

### (7) Protection from Interference

(1) CASO's academic evaluations shall not be altered by executive
bodies.

(2) Strategic or financial considerations shall not override
documented academic quality findings.

## §12 Digital Infrastructure and Data Authority

### (1) Central Platform Requirement

(1) All programs leading to OUG degrees shall utilize the centrally
designated digital infrastructure of OUG for:

- a) assessment submission;
- b) grade recording;
- c) examination documentation;
- d) appeals processing;
- e) quality monitoring.

(2) Partner institutions may utilize additional local platforms for
teaching delivery, provided that assessment records and final grading
are recorded in OUG's central system.

### (2) Central Storage of Academic Records

(1) All examination records contributing to OUG degrees shall be
stored within the OUG central registry.

(2) Stored records shall include:

- a) submitted assessments;
- b) examiner reports;
- c) moderation documentation;
- d) grading decisions;
- e) appeals outcomes.

(3) Retention periods shall comply with applicable legal requirements.

### (3) Data Protection Compliance

(1) OUG shall process academic data in accordance with applicable data
protection legislation.

(2) CASO and authorized governance bodies shall access academic data
solely for defined academic purposes.

(3) Data processing shall adhere to principles of:

- a) lawfulness;
- b) transparency;
- c) data minimization;
- d) purpose limitation;
- e) storage limitation;
- f) security.

### (4) Access Control

(1) Access to academic records shall be governed by role-based access
control mechanisms.

(2) Examination board members, appeals bodies, CASO personnel, and
authorized administrative staff shall have access only to data necessary
for their functions.

(3) Access logs shall be maintained and auditable.

### (5) Security and Continuity

(1) OUG shall maintain cybersecurity standards appropriate to protect
academic data.

(2) Disaster recovery and business continuity plans shall ensure
preservation of academic records.

(3) Regular security assessments shall be conducted.

### (6) Data Sovereignty

(1) OUG retains full authority over academic data generated within its
programs.

(2) Partner institutions may not maintain independent official grading
records for OUG-awarded degrees.

(3) Data sharing between OUG and partner institutions shall be
governed by formal agreements.

## §13 Decision Domain Allocation

### (1) Principle of Layered Governance

(1) Governance within the Open University of Germany (OUG) is
structured into four distinct decision domains:

- a) G1 – Constitutional Authority
- b) G2 – Strategic Authority
- c) G3 – Academic & Quality Authority
- d) G4 – Operational Execution

(2) Each governance layer exercises exclusive competencies within its
domain.

(3) No governance body may exercise authority outside its designated
domain unless explicitly provided by statute.

### (2) G1 – Constitutional Authority

(1) The Constitutional Council (G1) safeguards the constitutional
integrity of OUG.

(2) G1 is responsible for:

- a) interpretation of the constitution;
- b) constitutional amendments;
- c) structural disputes between governance layers;
- d) safeguarding institutional mission and long-term integrity.

(3) G1 shall not intervene in individual academic grading decisions.

### (3) G2 – Strategic Authority

(1) The Strategic Authority (G2), including the Rectorate Coordination
Council, is responsible for:

- a) long-term institutional strategy;
- b) financial planning;
- c) partner development;
- d) international positioning;
- e) institutional resource allocation.

(2) G2 may not override academic decisions adopted under G3 authority.

### (4) G3 – Academic & Quality Authority

(1) The OUG Senate exercises exclusive authority over:

- a) curriculum design;
- b) academic standards;
- c) examination regulations;
- d) quality assurance;
- e) appointment of examination boards;
- f) approval and termination of academic programs.

(2) Academic decisions adopted under G3 may not be overridden by G2 or
G4 bodies.

### (5) G4 – Operational Execution

(1) G4 encompasses administrative implementation of decisions adopted
by G1, G2, and G3.

(2) G4 may not independently alter academic, strategic, or
constitutional decisions.

### (6) Non-Overlap Clause

(1) Decision domains shall not overlap.

(2) In cases of disputed competence, the matter shall be referred to
G1 for constitutional clarification.

(3) No governance body may assume competence by default or through
informal practice.

## §14 Conflict Resolution Mechanism

### (1) Principle of Structured Escalation

All institutional conflicts shall be addressed through a structured
three-tier escalation process.

Informal resolution outside this framework shall not constitute binding
institutional decisions.

### (2) Tier 1 – Mission Hub Mediation

(1) Academic and operational conflicts arising within a program or
disciplinary area shall first be addressed at the Mission Hub level.

(2) Mission Hubs shall:

- a) document the dispute;
- b) attempt mediation;
- c) propose resolution;
- d) submit written outcomes to relevant governance bodies.

(3) Student interests shall be protected during mediation.

### (3) Tier 2 – Rectorate Coordination Council

(1) If Tier 1 mediation fails, the dispute shall escalate to the
Rectorate Coordination Council (G2).

(2) The Council shall:

- a) assess institutional impact;
- b) coordinate between partners;
- c) seek negotiated resolution consistent with constitutional principles.

(3) G2 may not override academic standards established under G3
authority.

### (4) Tier 3 – Constitutional Council (G1)

(1) Structural or persistent disputes shall be referred to the
Constitutional Council.

(2) G1 shall:

- a) interpret constitutional provisions;
- b) determine final allocation of authority;
- c) issue binding institutional decisions.

(3) Decisions of G1 are binding upon all governance bodies and partner
institutions.

### (5) Documentation and Transparency

(1) All conflict resolution stages shall be documented.

(2) Procedural timelines shall be defined in implementing regulations.

### (6) Student Protection During Disputes

(1) Ongoing disputes shall not disadvantage enrolled students.

(2) Academic progression and assessment continuity must be preserved
during conflict resolution.

# APPENDIX - JOINT & DUAL DEGREE CALCULATION FORMULA

### §C1 Purpose

This appendix defines the objective calculation mechanism that
determines eligibility for a joint or dual degree arrangement under §6
of the Constitution.

The formula is binding and non-discretionary.

No political negotiation or case-by-case exception may override this
calculation.

### §C2 Definitions

For purposes of calculation:

- **Total Program ECTS (T)** = Total number of ECTS credits required for graduation in the accredited program.

- **Partner Delivered & Assessed ECTS (Pᵢ)** = Total ECTS credits delivered and assessed under the academic responsibility of Partner Institution i.

- **Assessment Responsibility** means:

    -   Examination design

    -   Grading authority

    -   Moderation participation

    -   Final grade confirmation

Only ECTS both delivered and assessed under partner responsibility are
included.

### §C3 Calculation Formula

For each partner institution i:

> **Partner Share (Sᵢ) = (Pᵢ / T) × 100**

Where:

- Pᵢ = Partner Delivered & Assessed ECTS

- T = Total Program ECTS

### §C4 Joint Degree Trigger Threshold

A joint or dual degree arrangement becomes eligible when:

> **Sᵢ > 50.000%**

The threshold requires strict numerical superiority.

Equal to 50.000% does not qualify.

No rounding upward is permitted.

### §C5 Inclusion Rules

The following shall be included in Pᵢ:

1. Coursework delivered and assessed by partner.

2. Thesis supervision and grading responsibility.

3. Capstone or final project assessment.

The following shall be excluded:

1. Recognized prior learning.

2. Credit transfer from external institutions not formally part of the OUG federation.

3. Exchange mobility credits assessed outside formal partner responsibility.

4. Non-assessed participation credits.

### §C6 Rounding Rules

1. Calculations shall be carried to three decimal places.

2. Rounding shall occur only at final result.

3. Rounding shall follow standard mathematical rules.

4. Rounding may not be used to artificially exceed threshold.

### §C7 Cohort-Specific Application

(1) The calculation shall be applied per accredited program structure.

(2) If structural variation exists between cohorts, calculation shall
be cohort-specific.

(3) Documentation shall be archived in the Central Registry.

### §C8 Documentation and Audit

(1) The calculation must be documented in writing prior to issuance of
the first degree for each program cycle.

(2) CASO shall verify the calculation annually.

(3) Calculation records shall be auditable.

### §C9 Formalization Requirement

Even where threshold eligibility is met:

1. A joint or dual degree may only be issued following a formal inter-institutional agreement.

2. Senate approval is mandatory.

3. Signature protocol must be defined in advance.

4. Registry coordination must be established.

Eligibility does not automatically mandate issuance.

### §C10 Stability Clause for Joint Degrees

Once a joint degree arrangement is formally established for a cohort:

1. It shall apply consistently to that cohort.

2. Subsequent structural changes may not retroactively alter eligibility.

3. Withdrawal of a partner shall not invalidate degrees for qualifying cohorts.

- a) Participate in Mission Hubs.
- b) Vote in Senate elections (subject to representation rules).
- c) Stand for academic office where eligible.
- d) Nomination for examination boards.
- e) Access academic resources required for OUG program delivery.

## §D5 Duties

Affiliated professors shall:

- a) Comply with OUG curriculum and examination regulations.
- b) Participate in moderation and QA procedures.
- c) Complete required assessment training.
- d) Disclose conflicts of interest.
- e) Respect digital infrastructure policies.
- f) Uphold academic integrity standards.

## §D6 Academic Freedom

(1) OUG guarantees academic freedom consistent with constitutional
provisions.
(2) Participation in OUG shall not diminish research autonomy at Home
HEI.
(3) Academic expression within adopted curriculum remains protected.

## §D7 Conflict of Interest

(1) The affiliated professor must disclose institutional or financial
conflicts.
(2) Recusal is required where material conflict exists.

## §D8 Termination

Affiliation may be terminated:

- a) Upon expiry of term.
- b) Upon resignation.
- c) For material breach of OUG standards.
- d) For conflict-of-interest violation.
- e) Loss of eligibility.

Termination follows due process under OUG statutes.

## §D9 Dispute Resolution

Disputes shall follow the escalation mechanism defined in Part VI of the
Constitution.

### 3. Core Academic Responsibility Matrix

**Legend:** **D** = Decides (final authority) · **P** = Proposes / prepares · **C** = Consulted · **I** = Informed · **S** = Signs legally binding document

#### 3.1 Curriculum & Module Governance

| Process | Senate (G3) | Mission Hub | Rectorate | Member HEI | Exam Board |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **New degree programme approval** | D | P | C | C | I |
| **Major curriculum change (Class C/D)** | D | P | C | C | I |
| **Minor curriculum change (Class A/B)** | I | D | I | C | I |
| **New module approval** | I | D | I | C | I |
| **Module-level didactic adjustment** | I | D | I | I | I |

**Explanation:** Academic authority resides within OUG (Mission Hub + Senate). Member HEIs contribute teaching but do not hold final curriculum authority for OUG degrees.

#### 3.2 Examinations & Academic Standards

| Process | Senate | Mission Hub | Exam Board (OUG) | Rectorate | Member HEI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Adoption of Prüfungsordnung** | D | C | C | C | I |
| **Appointment of examiners** | I | P | D | I | C |
| **Conduct of examinations** | I | D | Oversight | I | Operational support |
| **Examination appeals** | I | C | D | I | I |
| **Grade confirmation** | I | I | D | I | I |

**Key principle:** All examinations contributing to an OUG degree fall under an **OUG Examination Board**. Member HEIs may host or administer exams operationally, but academic responsibility lies with OUG.

#### 3.3 Recognition & Credit Transfer (RPL / Mobility)

| Process | Senate | Mission Hub | Recognition Committee | Rectorate | Member HEI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Recognition policy** | D | C | C | I | I |

### 4. Escalation Logic (If Conflict Occurs)

If conflict arises between:

- Member HEI and Mission Hub
- Examination Board and delivery site
- Senate and Rectorate
- Strategic vs academic authority

Escalation follows the path defined in the Governance Framework:

1. Mission Hub → Senate (G3)
2. Senate → Rectorate (if structural/strategic impact)
3. Constitutional layer (G1) only if principle conflict

Academic standards cannot be overridden by financial or political pressure.
