# Part I — Status, Purpose, and Legal Hierarchy

## § 1 Nature of This Instrument

(1) This Federated Legal Model is an **integrated legal architecture and allocation framework** for the OUG federation. It states, in one place, how employment authority, academic authority, degree-awarding authority, delivery responsibility, and administrative authority are distinguished and allocated across OUG and its partner institutions.

(2) It is **not itself a legally binding constitution**. Legal force attaches where its allocations are implemented through the Grundordnung, the Prüfungsordnung, the Federation & Membership Framework, and formal agreements with partner institutions and affiliated academics. Where this document uses "must" or "shall," it states the allocation that those instruments are expected to implement, not a directly enforceable rule in its own right, unless the relevant statute or agreement has already incorporated it.

(3) Where this document and any statute or formal agreement diverge, the statute or agreement governs, and this document should be corrected to match — not the reverse.

## § 2 Purpose and Scope

(1) The Open University of Germany (OUG) is a degree-awarding higher education institution operating as a federated academic entity within applicable German and Baden-Württemberg higher education law.

(2) This framework exists to answer one question with precision: **when teaching capacity is distributed across partner institutions, how does OUG remain the academically sovereign, degree-awarding university?** Its answer is the five-way separation defined in Part II, reconnected through formal affiliation, delegation, quality assurance, and registry control.

(3) This framework governs: the allocation of authority among OUG and its federation partners (Part II); the legal status of academically affiliated staff (Part III); federation-specific quality assurance (Part IV); partnership participation principles (Part V); and cross-cutting safeguards (Part VI). It does **not** restate examination procedure (governed by the Prüfungsordnung), detailed financial mechanics (governed by the Federation & Membership Framework), conflict escalation procedure (governed by the [Conflict Escalation Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/conflict-escalation-framework.md)), or amendment procedure (governed by the [Amendment Procedure](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/amendment-procedure.md)).

## § 3 Legal Hierarchy

Within the scope of this framework, the following precedence applies wherever instruments appear to conflict:

1. Mandatory law (German federal and Baden-Württemberg state law, including the LHG and data protection law).
2. OUG statutes governing an OUG award (Grundordnung, Prüfungsordnung).
3. This framework and the Governance & Decision Framework, read together.
4. Federation and provider agreements (Federation & Membership Framework, individual partnership and provider agreements).
5. Delegated local procedures and operational guidance.

Where a partner institution's own mandatory legal duties (employment law, local examination-hosting rules, data protection obligations attaching to its own status) are incompatible with a lower-numbered instrument in this list, the conflict must be resolved contractually or the affected arrangement must cease — this framework does not require a partner to breach its own binding legal duties.

# Part II — Allocation of Authority

## § 4 The Five-Way Separation

(1) OUG distinguishes, legally and operationally, between five domains of authority:

| Authority | Holder |
| --- | --- |
| Employment and personnel authority over teaching staff | Home HEI (the partner institution primarily employing the affiliated academic) |
| Teaching delivery responsibility | Partner HEI, within OUG-approved academic frameworks |
| Academic and quality authority (curriculum, assessment, standards) | OUG, exercised through G3 (Senat and delegated bodies — see the [Decision-Rights Matrix](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/decision-rights-matrix.md)) |
| Degree and registry authority | OUG exclusively (Part IV of the companion [Degree and Examination Authority](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/degree-examination-authority.md) document) |
| Administrative execution | OUG Rektorat/Kanzler and central administration, G4 |

(2) These domains may not be merged or overridden except through the [Amendment Procedure](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/amendment-procedure.md). Federation does not dilute degree authority: a partner's contribution of teaching capacity does not, by itself, transfer any part of degree-awarding authority.

(3) **A body or office may perform functions in more than one governance layer where law or statute so provides, but each individual decision must be assigned to one defined domain, authority, and accountability route.** This corrects an earlier, stricter formulation of this principle: the Kanzlerin/Kanzler is a Rektorat member (G2) by law while exercising a G4 execution function (Grundordnung § 7; Governance Architecture § 2.5); the Senat exercises both statutory academic authority and, quarterly, its Community Circle deliberative identity (Grundordnung § 5 Absatz 14). What may not happen is a single decision being claimed by more than one domain at once.

## § 5 Academic Sovereignty and G3 Authority

(1) OUG holds final institutional academic authority over curriculum architecture, learning outcomes, assessment formats, examination regulations, and quality assurance standards for all programmes leading to OUG degrees, exercised through the Senat (Grundordnung § 5 Absatz 1) as G3.

(2) This is **final institutional academic authority, subject to delegation** — not an assertion that every academic decision is made directly by the Senat in plenary. Mission Hubs, examination boards, and programme committees exercise delegated or assigned functions within Senate-approved frameworks (Grundordnung § 8 Absätze 7–8; Governance Architecture § 5.2). Partner institutions may be consulted on curriculum and assessment matters through the Programme Development Forum (see the [Governance Review Calendar](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-review-calendar.md)), but final decision authority remains with OUG.

(3) No external body, partner institution, or executive office may override a G3 decision except through the escalation routes defined in the [Conflict Escalation Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/conflict-escalation-framework.md).

## § 6 Research Autonomy

(1) Participation in OUG programmes does not limit or transfer the research autonomy of affiliated academics within their primary institution.

(2) OUG does not claim ownership over research output except where separately agreed in specific collaborative research contracts.

# Part III — Academic Affiliation

## § 7 Dual Academic Affiliation Structure

(1) An academic contributing to OUG programmes holds a primary appointment at a recognised higher education institution (the "Home HEI") and, separately, a secondary academic affiliation with OUG.

(2) The legal weight of this affiliation is **not uniform** — it depends on which of three engagement models applies, and this framework corrects an earlier version that treated affiliation as a single undifferentiated contractual status. The three models, and their legal consequences, are:

| Model | Legal character | LHG basis | Resulting status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Lehrauftrag** (standard, individual Nebentätigkeit) | Individual side-contract, not an institutional cooperation agreement | Does not meet § 9 Absatz 1 Satz 5 LHG's *"im Rahmen von Kooperationsvereinbarungen"* — that phrase requires an institutional-level agreement | Contractual affiliation only (§ 8 below) |
| **B — Freistellungsvereinbarung** (institutional, ministerially approved partial release) | A genuine Kooperationsvereinbarung between OUG and the Home HEI | **Matches § 9 Absatz 1 Satz 5 LHG directly** | Statutory Hochschule *Mitglied* status — see § 8(5) |
| **C — OUG-employed** (core functions) | Direct employment | Ordinary hauptberuflich membership | Full, uncontested member |

(3) The choice of engagement model, and the specific conditions under which each applies, are governed by the Federation & Membership Framework and its Lehrpersonalmodell provisions, not restated here.

## § 8 Rights and Participation

**This section corrects two things in the previous version of this framework: an over-broad claim (Senate voting rights for all affiliated academics) and, subsequently, an over-cautious one (advisory participation for all affiliated academics, full stop).** Neither was right. Participation rights track the engagement model in § 7(2):

**For all affiliated academics (Models A, B, and C):**

(1) Participation, with advisory voice, in the **Community Circle** — the Senate's quarterly deliberative convening (Grundordnung § 5 Absatz 14).

(2) Contribution to curriculum and programme design through the **Programme Development Forum**, producing documented Decision Accounts reviewed under the Governance Review Calendar.

(3) Nomination by their Mission Hub for examination board service, subject to eligibility criteria in the companion [Degree and Examination Authority](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/degree-examination-authority.md) document; final appointment authority rests with OUG (G3).

(4) Proposal of curriculum initiatives through their Mission Hub, and access to institutional academic resources necessary for programme delivery.

**Additionally, for Model B and Model C academics:**

(5) **Grundordnung § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3 already grants full aktives und passives Wahlrecht** to *"Hochschullehrerinnen und Hochschullehrer, die nach einer gemeinsamen Berufung mit einer Forschungseinrichtung außerhalb des Hochschulbereichs oder im Rahmen von Kooperationsvereinbarungen dienstliche Aufgaben an der Hochschule wahrnehmen"* — language taken directly from § 9 Absatz 1 Satz 5 LHG. A Model B academic falls squarely within this category. **This is not a new entitlement created by this framework; it is an existing statutory and Grundordnung provision this framework had previously failed to connect to the federation's actual affiliated academics.**

(6) **One point remains genuinely open and is not resolved by this framework:** whether Wahlrecht under § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3 places a Model B academic inside the specific voting bloc that § 10 Absatz 1 Satz 2 Nummer 1 LHG defines for mandatory Senate/Fakultätsrat seat apportionment — a bloc textually limited to those *"soweit sie hauptberuflich tätig sind und überwiegend Aufgaben einer Professur wahrnehmen"* — or whether it operates through a separate mechanism the Grundordnung would need to specify. This framework does not assert an answer. It should be resolved by qualified legal review before any Model B academic is told they hold a Senate vote, and the resolution, once reached, should be written into the Grundordnung directly rather than left implicit here.

(7) A co-opted member (Kooptation under § 22 Absatz 4 LHG, a distinct route from Model B) **cannot** be appointed Mission Hub Steward "as such" — this is a hard statutory floor (§ 22 Absatz 4 Satz 3 LHG), not a design choice this framework could waive even if it wanted to.

(8) **Statutory Senate membership beyond what is described above, electoral rights, and eligibility for statutory organs arise only where separately provided by the Grundordnung and applicable election regulations.** This framework does not create rights by contract beyond what § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3 already provides.

## § 9 Duties, Academic Freedom, Conflict of Interest, Duration, Termination

(1) Affiliated academics shall comply with OUG curriculum and examination standards, participate in quality assurance processes required of their role, maintain academic integrity, and disclose conflicts of interest.

(2) **OUG guarantees academic freedom in accordance with Article 5(3) of the German Basic Law, applicable higher-education law, its own statutes, and the protections attached to the individual's primary appointment.** Federation agreements may provide additional protections but may not diminish these mandatory guarantees. Participation in OUG programmes does not diminish the academic freedom protections an affiliated academic holds under their primary appointment, and does not limit their research autonomy at the Home HEI.

(3) Conflicts of interest — institutional, financial, or governance-related — must be disclosed; OUG may restrict participation in specific decisions where a material conflict exists.

(4) Affiliation is granted for a defined, renewable term and reviewed on loss of the underlying primary appointment. Termination follows due process defined in OUG regulations and does not affect degrees already awarded under the affiliate's prior contribution.

# Part IV — Quality Assurance

## § 10 Federation Quality Compliance

**This section corrects the previous version of this framework, which proposed a standalone Central Academic Standards Office (CASO).** That risked creating a second audit authority alongside the Quality Commission already established for OUG generally (see the [Governance Review Calendar](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-review-calendar.md)). Instead:

(1) The **Quality Commission** is the audit authority for academic standards across the federation, including federation-specific compliance monitoring: equivalence of academic standards across partner institutions, grading-distribution review, moderation oversight, and partner compliance with OUG standards.

(2) The Quality Commission's independence from G2 — already a scheduled legitimacy criterion under the Governance Review Calendar — extends to federation matters: strategic or financial considerations relating to a partner relationship shall not override a documented academic quality finding.

(3) Where the Quality Commission identifies deficient academic standards at a partner institution, it may recommend to the Senat corrective action plans, enhanced monitoring, suspension of course recognition, or temporary suspension of partner participation. Only the Senat may impose programme suspension, termination, or suspension of a partner's participation.

# Part V — Partnership Participation Principles

**This Part states principles only.** Detailed provisions on revenue pool composition, central retention percentages, annual reporting mechanics, and exit-penalty schedules belong in the Federation & Membership Framework and individual provider agreements — freezing that operational detail into this framework risks exactly the rigidity the Strategy Constitution's Section 7 (Deliberate Non-Decisions) warns against.

## § 11 Contribution-Transparency Principles

(1) Financial participation within the federation follows a transparent, formula-based model. Discretionary revenue allocation outside a published formula is not permitted, and no side agreements altering proportional distribution are permitted.

(2) **Financial contribution does not determine academic content, grading standards, or examination outcomes.** Academic governance (G3) remains independent of financial distribution considerations.

(3) **Governance representation weighted by financial or academic contribution may apply only to a specifically constituted federation or partner body — never to the Senat or any statutory academic organ.** The Grundordnung fixes the Senat's 21 voting seats by statutory member group (§ 5 Absätze 4–8); a contribution-weighted voting supplement cannot be legally overlaid onto those seats. Where a federation council or partner assembly is established, it may use a base-vote-plus-proportional-supplement model with a dominance cap, but its decisions do not extend to Senat matters.

(4) Under the allocation in § 4, **final authority over the distribution model and institutional retention rests with the Rektorat (G2)**, which proposes and adopts the strategic financial model within its statutory competence; the Kanzlerin/Kanzler (G4) validates legality and financial sustainability and executes; **the Senat's consent is required specifically and only where a financial decision would materially affect academic standards or programme viability**, per Governance Architecture § 3.7 — not as a general budget-approval power.

## § 12 Partner Admission, Participation, and Exit

Partner admission is not a single decision but a decomposed one, corresponding to the domains in § 4:

1. **Academic eligibility** — Senat (G3): does the prospective partner have the academic capacity and standards commitment to deliver OUG-quality teaching?
2. **Strategic and financial suitability** — Rektorat (G2): does the partnership fit OUG's portfolio and financial sustainability?
3. **Legal and administrative due diligence** — Kanzlerin/Kanzler (G4): data protection, contractual, and regulatory readiness.
4. **Formal admission** — the competent body under the Federation & Membership Framework and the Grundordnung, once 1–3 are satisfied.

Participation obligations, minimum participation periods, exit notice, teach-out obligations, and degree-continuity guarantees for enrolled students on partner exit are governed by the Federation & Membership Framework; the governing principle carried over from the previous version of this document — **withdrawal of a partner does not invalidate degrees already progressing toward award, and OUG retains authority to reassign academic delivery to another qualified partner where necessary** — is retained and restated here because it is a degree-sovereignty guarantee, not merely a financial or operational detail.

## § 13 Data Responsibility

(1) OUG is responsible for the authoritative academic record and determines the purposes and governance of the central registry.

(2) **Controller, joint-controller, and processor responsibilities are allocated by applicable data protection law and formal data-processing agreements** — this framework does not itself assign "full authority" over all academic data to OUG, since partner institutions necessarily process data in their own capacities as employers, teaching providers, or hosts.

(3) Partner institutions may maintain local operational records necessary for their own functions; such records are not the authoritative OUG academic record, and this distinction — not a blanket prohibition on any local record-keeping — is what protects registry integrity.

# Part VI — Safeguards

## § 14 Academic Freedom Guarantee

Restated from § 9(2): OUG guarantees academic freedom in accordance with Article 5(3) of the Basic Law, applicable law, its own statutes, and each affiliate's primary-appointment protections. No federation agreement, financial arrangement, or administrative procedure may reduce academic freedom below this standard. Digital infrastructure operated by OUG shall not be used to restrict lawful academic expression beyond legal compliance and academic integrity requirements. Students enjoy the same freedom of academic inquiry and expression, consistent with academic standards and respectful conduct.

## § 15 No Partner Residual Authority Over OUG Degrees

**No partner institution holds residual academic or degree-awarding authority over an OUG-only award merely by virtue of delivering teaching or employing participating academics.** This is narrower than an earlier, broader formulation of this principle: partners retain their own legitimate legal authority over employment, workplace discipline, local facilities, research, health and safety, and their own contractual and statutory responsibilities. This framework protects OUG's degree sovereignty; it does not, and could not, extinguish a partner's authority in domains that were never OUG's to begin with.

## § 16 Safeguards Against Power Concentration

(1) No single partner institution may hold majority voting control in any OUG statutory academic body, subject to § 11(3).

(2) Constitutional amendments and decisions affecting degree authority require the qualified majority defined in the relevant statute or, where none exists, follow the [Amendment Procedure](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/amendment-procedure.md).

(3) Governance body members disclose institutional and financial conflicts of interest and recuse themselves where a material conflict exists.

## § 17 Conflict Routing

Conflicts, competence disputes, academic escalations, administrative or legal escalations, constitutional questions, and individual remedies arising under this framework are routed in accordance with the [Conflict Escalation Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/conflict-escalation-framework.md). No escalation route under that framework alters statutory appeals or individual legal remedies. This framework does not maintain its own separate escalation ladder.

## § 18 Amendment

Amendments to this framework are governed by the [Amendment Procedure](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/amendment-procedure.md). Where implementation requires amendment of the Grundordnung, the Prüfungsordnung, or another formal statute, the adoption requirements of that instrument and applicable law additionally apply. This framework does not maintain its own separate amendment sequence.

# Part VII — Transitional Provisions

## § 19 Phased Implementation and Readiness Gates

(1) Implementation proceeds through a Founding Phase, commencing on formal adoption of this framework and continuing until operational readiness is certified.

(2) **Readiness certification is a multi-authority gate, not a single Senate certification:** the Senat certifies academic readiness; the Kanzlerin/Kanzler certifies administrative, data-protection, and registry readiness; the Rektorat confirms strategic and operational launch readiness; the Hochschulrat performs oversight where constitutional questions arise. This corrects an earlier version that made Senate certification sufficient on its own for domains it does not have the competence to certify.

(3) No partner institution may deliver courses contributing to OUG degrees without a signed Federation Membership Agreement. Transitional arrangements during the Founding Phase may not diminish academic standards or student rights, and no retrospective academic disadvantage may be imposed due to transitional adjustments.

## Status

This framework is internally authoritative in the sense defined in Governance Architecture § 0 (legally binding / internally authoritative / procedurally binding). It is reviewed under the [Governance Review Calendar](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-review-calendar.md) alongside the Partnership Office (every 3 years) given the overlap in scope.
