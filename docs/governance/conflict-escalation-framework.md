> **Position in the document architecture:** this framework supplies the escalation-route detail referenced but not defined in the [Governance & Decision Framework](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-architecture.md) §3.10 and §6.5. It does not create new authority; it routes existing authority (as mapped in Governance Architecture §2.5 and the [Grundordnung](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/grundordnung.md)) to the correct resolution mechanism.

## 0. Purpose

Governance Architecture §6.5 defines a single vertical ladder — clarification within the layer, escalation to the next layer, constitutional review — for **constitutional escalation** specifically. Not every conflict is constitutional. Treating every disagreement as a candidate for constitutional review either overloads the Hochschulrat (G1) with matters it has no comparative advantage in resolving, or trains people to avoid raising real conflicts because the only visible path feels disproportionate.

This document distinguishes six escalation routes, states which conflicts belong on each, and specifies the first step, the resolving body, and the further escalation point (if any) for each route.

## 1. The Six Routes

| Route | Applies when | First step | Resolved by | Further escalation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Horizontal Coordination** | Two peer bodies at the same governance layer disagree (e.g. two Mission Hubs over a shared module or student cohort) | Direct negotiation between the Stewards of Missions or committee chairs involved | The peer bodies themselves | Route 2, only if the disagreement turns out to be about competence rather than substance |
| **2. Competence-Boundary Review** | It is unclear which decision domain (Governance Architecture §3) or which layer a matter belongs to | Written request for competence clarification, addressed jointly to the bodies in dispute | Rektorat (G2) and Senat (G3) jointly, by reference to the domain table in Governance Architecture §3 and the Institutional Mapping in §2.5 | Hochschulrat (G1), only if G2 and Senat cannot agree on the domain assignment itself |
| **3. Academic Escalation** | A dispute internal to academic governance cannot be resolved at Mission Hub or committee level (e.g. a curriculum or assessment disagreement) | Mission Hub / Steward of Missions | Senat (Grundordnung § 5 Absatz 1) | Route 5 (constitutional), only if the Senate's decision is itself alleged to violate a constitutional principle |
| **4. Administrative / Legal Escalation** | A question concerns lawful execution, budget administration, personnel administration, or IT/data infrastructure (Grundordnung § 7) | Kanzlerin/Kanzler | Rektorat (G2), where the matter exceeds the Kanzler/in's delegated authority | Route 5, only where a resource decision is alleged to conflict with a constitutional precedence rule (Strategic Constitution § 5) |
| **5. Constitutional Escalation** | Constitutional principles (Strategic Constitution §§ 2–5) appear to be violated, or persistent conflict exists between governance layers that Routes 1–4 could not resolve | As defined in Governance Architecture § 6.5: (1) clarification within the relevant layer, (2) escalation to the next layer, (3) constitutional review | Hochschulrat (G1), as the final constitutional authority (Governance Architecture § 2.5, § 5.7) | None — this is the top of the ladder |
| **6. Individual Legal Remedies and Appeals** | An individual (student, staff member) disputes a specific decision affecting them — an examination result, a disciplinary matter, an employment decision | The formal legal channel specific to the matter (see § 2 below) | The competent authority under that legal channel — not a governance body | Ordinary legal appeal / Verwaltungsrechtsweg, as applicable |

## 2. Route 6 in Detail: This Framework Does Not Replace Legal Remedies

Routes 1–5 are internal governance mechanisms. They are not a substitute for, and do not delay or suspend, statutory legal remedies available to individuals. In particular:

- **Examination disputes** follow the appeal path defined in the Prüfungsordnung, not this framework.
- **Personnel and employment disputes** follow dienstrechtliche Rechtsbehelfe as applicable to the individual's employment status.
- **Equality and discrimination matters** may additionally involve the Gleichstellungsbeauftragte (Grundordnung § 10), whose formal rights of consultation and the formal remedies of other bodies (Antidiskriminierungsstellen, Personalvertretung, Schwerbehindertenvertretung) remain unaffected (Grundordnung § 10 Absatz 7).

Where a matter genuinely straddles both an individual legal remedy and a governance-level competence question (for example, a pattern of examination disputes that reveals a genuine ambiguity in Route 2), the individual remedy proceeds on its own track while the competence question is raised separately through Route 2. One does not wait for the other.

## 3. Worked Examples

**A Mission Hub disagreement over shared teaching capacity.** Two Mission Hubs each want priority access to the same visiting lecturer's hours next term. → **Route 1.** The Stewards of Missions negotiate directly; if they cannot agree, either may request a joint session convened by the Rektorat, but this does not yet involve the Hochschulrat.

**A dispute over whether a decision belongs to Portfolio Logic (D-C) or Curriculum Architecture (D-E).** A proposal to discontinue a specialisation track is framed by one side as portfolio scope (G2 authority) and by the other as curriculum design (G3 authority). → **Route 2.** Rektorat and Senat jointly determine the domain assignment by reference to Governance Architecture § 3.3 and § 3.5. If they cannot agree, the Hochschulrat resolves the boundary question — not the underlying decision itself, only which layer decides it.

**An operational question about whether a purchase is lawful under the current budget.** → **Route 4.** This goes to the Kanzlerin/Kanzler, not to G3 or G1, even if the purchase happens to support an academic programme — the question here is lawful execution, not academic standards.

**A student disputes an examination grade.** → **Route 6.** This follows the Prüfungsordnung's appeal process. It is never escalated through Routes 1–5, and no governance body substitutes its judgment for the examination board's on the substance of the grade.

## 4. Relationship to Governance Architecture

This document does not modify Governance Architecture § 3.10 or § 6.5; it supplies the detail those sections reference. Where this document and Governance Architecture appear to conflict, Governance Architecture's Section 3 domain assignments and Section 2.5 institutional mapping prevail — this framework only routes disputes to the bodies those sections already name.

## 5. Status

This framework is internally authoritative in the sense defined in Governance Architecture § 0. It should be read alongside Governance Architecture §§ 3.10 and 6.5, which it supplements rather than replaces.
