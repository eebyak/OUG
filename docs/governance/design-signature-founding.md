*Open University of Germany · Founding Document*
*Fourth Edition · Reorganised on the Principles Register · 2026*

> **Status:** *(to be set — see [Document Status](../index.md#document-status))* · **Version:** v3.0
> **What changed, and why this is a new document rather than a further edition of the old one.** The previous editions of this signature scored the institution against forty-two principles drawn from six of the book's eight dialogue sources. Compiling the [Principles Register](principles-register.md) directly from the book surfaced two entire sources — GP-1 through GP-8 and EH-0 through EH-4 — that were never scored at all, plus four principles (HP-1, HP-6, CP-1, CP-4) that existed in the old document but had no thematic home until the Register placed them. The organising structure has also changed completely: this document is sorted by the Register's nine meaning-based sections, not by which dialogue a principle came from. A principle keeps its original code only as a citation, exactly as the Register does. Where a principle was already scored in the prior signature, that assessment is carried forward, re-cited to its new section, and revisited only where this session's work has actually changed the evidence. Sixteen principles — the eight GP principles, the five EH principles, and the three founding principles — are scored here for the first time.
> **How to read a maturity marker.** Built — structurally anchored, though not necessarily yet tested in operation; this distinction is stated explicitly wherever it matters. Partial — present in intention or partially specified, not yet fully operationalised. Recommendations are given for every Partial entry, and for a small number of Built entries where the underlying mechanism is itself new enough that "built" still means "specified," not "proven."

## 0. The Three Founding Principles

**Learning Is the Goal.** Built. *Proof:* module-level credentialing so partial progress carries real value, competency-based curriculum protected by an academic authority structurally insulated from financial pressure, a performance framework that explicitly separates health-monitoring from transformation-tracking, and a developmental audit that asks whether the institution is achieving its own stated purpose independent of accreditation compliance. *Recommendation:* none — see Section 1 for the full evidentiary basis and its residual gaps.

**The Learner Directs the Learning Relationship.** Built. *Proof:* statutory student voting seats, a deliberative forum structurally separated from the formal vote, an external societal panel built around an explicit duty to voice concerns rather than wait to be asked, and a real, individually-earned voting tier for sustained academic contributors grounded in previously unused statutory room. *Recommendation:* none — see Section 2 for the full evidentiary basis and its residual gaps.

**What Is Available to Learn Is Determined by Learner Need.** Built. *Proof:* academic units chartered around documented global challenges rather than disciplines, an enforced rule against disciplinary naming, a statute-grounded lifecycle for opening, closing, and renaming those units tied to documented societal need, and a federation model that expands what is available without requiring every institution to duplicate every discipline. *Recommendation:* none — see Sections 3–4 for the full evidentiary basis and its residual gaps.

*Note common to all three: "Built" describes design completeness, not operating history. None of the three has yet been tested by a full cohort, a full annual cycle, or a genuine institutional crisis. This caveat is not repeated at every subordinate entry below, but it applies to nearly all of them.*

## 1. Purpose: Learning as the Institutional Goal, Not Its Byproduct

**University as a Socially Embedded Institution.** Built. *Proof:* the founding purpose — serving working adults, caregivers, and geographically excluded learners at accessible pricing — is a direct structural response to a named social condition, not a mission statement appended afterward. Societal-need input is now a required, recorded part of Mission Hub opening, closing, and renaming, fed by an annual public Communiqué and an explicit duty to raise concerns unprompted. *(P-12.)*

**Purpose Before Access.** Built. *Proof:* the pricing model and target population are defined before any access mechanism, in substantive terms — who is served, why existing provision fails them — with the access mechanism designed to fit that purpose rather than the reverse. *Recommendation:* the Purpose Statement remains distributed across several founding documents rather than consolidated into one reviewable statement; worth consolidating before external communications scale up. *(CP-1.)*

**Presence as Pedagogical Design.** Partial. *Proof:* the book's Presence Architecture concept is referenced across the curriculum domain, but this session did not touch curriculum or pedagogy documents directly, so the operational specification is unverified rather than confirmed absent. *Recommendation:* verify the Presence Architecture's actual specification against this principle directly, the same primary-source discipline applied to the LHG this session. *(GP-2.)*

**Learning Is Measured by What Endures.** Partial. *Proof:* the OKR Framework's transformation-tracking instrument is built and distinguishes durable capability outcomes from health-monitoring proxies; a longitudinal outcome-tracking mechanism is referenced in the quality assurance domain but was not verified this session. *Recommendation:* confirm the longitudinal tracking amendment is actually wired into the OKR dashboard's transformation panel rather than existing as a separate, unconnected instrument. *(GP-3.)*

**The Living Mandate.** Built. *Proof:* every structural feature — asynchronous-first delivery, modular credentialing, federation, accessible pricing — is a direct response to the lifelong, non-traditional learner existing frameworks were not built around. *Recommendation:* discipline of language, not design: resist substituting "student" for the specific learner profile in external communication. *(HP-2.)*

**Concept Over Content.** Built. *Proof:* curriculum is organised around demonstrated competency rather than content coverage, with a protected academic authority holding that standard independent of delivery partner. *Recommendation:* whether partner-delivered modules share this orientation in practice, rather than only in specification, depends on quality verification not yet operated. *(CP-5.)*

**Transformation-Oriented Performance.** Built. *Proof:* the OKR Framework explicitly rejects KPI-only management; the Bug Protocol treats underperformance as a structural signal rather than individual failure, with a defined Flag/Diagnose/Classify/Act/Learn sequence. *(P-7.)*

**Visible Development.** Built. *Proof:* module-level digital credentials mean partial completion carries real, portable, externally verifiable value from the first completed unit, not only at a degree endpoint. *Recommendation:* credential issuance is architecturally specified but operationally dependent on infrastructure integration not yet tested. *(LP-3.)*

## 2. Accountability Direction: Toward the Learner, Toward the Learner's Development

**Distributed and Staged Decision-Making.** Built. *Proof:* the G1–G4 layered authority model, now explicitly mapped to named statutory bodies, structurally prevents any single actor from taking a binding decision unilaterally in any domain. *(P-2.)*

**Continuous Feedback and Adaptive Execution.** Partial. *Proof:* quarterly OKR review cycles are specified; the platform-based continuous-feedback infrastructure is a planned milestone, not yet live. *Recommendation:* assign the Quality Commission's existing Stichprobe cycle as an explicit interim feedback source rather than leaving the gap unassigned until the platform milestone is reached. *(P-6.)*

**Checks and Balances.** Built. *Proof:* constitutional insulation of academic authority from financial and executive pressure, a six-route Conflict Escalation Framework replacing a single undifferentiated ladder, and a Decision-Rights Matrix assigning exactly one final authority per domain. *Recommendation:* the escalation framework is specified but has not yet been tested by a genuine, contested cross-layer dispute. *(P-10.)*

**Committees as Distributed Intelligence.** Built. *Proof:* governance bodies — the Quality Commission, Mission Hub Steering Groups, Senate committees — are specified by domain expertise and function rather than status-based composition. *(P-8.)*

**Participation, Authority, and Accountability Must Be Connected.** Built. *Proof:* this is, in substance, the entire architecture built this session for exactly this purpose — the Decision-Rights Matrix names who proposes, who is consulted, who decides, and who executes for every domain, closing the gap this principle names directly. This is one of the most thoroughly evidenced principles in the whole register. *(GP-7.)*

**Designed Participation.** Partial. *Proof:* statutory student voting seats exist; asynchronous participation formats — how a learner engaging at odd hours contributes to governance — are not yet specified. *Recommendation:* extend the Community Circle's two-tier pattern (a broader input channel feeding a smaller live decision) to give students an async-first channel into Senate agenda-setting. *(LP-4.)*

## 3. Access and the Learner's Starting Point

**Designed Diversity.** Partial. *Proof:* founding documents explicitly name target learner profiles — working adults, caregivers, geographically excluded, career reorienting — but no formal Learner Profile Map exists as a governance instrument informing programme design. *Recommendation:* build a small, explicitly non-exhaustive profile map — the same illustrative-not-exhaustive approach used for the Mission Hub Challenge Framework's charter areas — rather than attempting a complete taxonomy before first admissions. *(LP-1.)*

**Designed Access.** Partial. *Proof:* prior learning recognition and professional experience are accepted as entry criteria in principle; the operational process — named committee, published criteria, timeline, advising support — is not yet specified. *Recommendation:* use the founding Design Authority as the template: a small, real, named body sized for the founding phase, not a large representative committee that won't be ready in time. *(LP-5; cross-referenced again in Section 9 for its renewal dimension.)*

**Quality-Gated Federation.** Partial. *Proof:* the three-outcome eligibility assessment (Eligible / Conditionally Eligible / Not Eligible) replaces institution-to-institution negotiation as the entry gate. *Recommendation:* the Design Authority that enforces this gate is named but not yet constituted as a functioning body — this is an appointment gap, not a design gap; the founding composition and expansion pathway already exist in full. *(CP-2.)*

## 4. Guided Flexibility: Coherence Without Rigidity, Freedom Without Fragmentation

**Strategic Clarity and Feasibility.** Partial. *Proof:* the OKR Framework requires explicit feasibility assessment; the founding-phase OKR set has not yet been finalised against operational reality. *Recommendation:* run the Bug Protocol's own Flag/Diagnose/Classify structure proactively against every founding OKR now, rather than waiting to apply it only after a Key Result is missed. *(P-5.)*

**Structure as Home and Challenge.** Built. *Proof:* Mission Hubs are organised around societal challenges with an enforced rule against disciplinary naming; internal cross-Hub interfaces (research seminars, joint projects, rotating governance membership) prevent the replacement structure from becoming a new silo under a new name. *Recommendation:* unoperated — no Hub has completed a full annual cycle of either interface set yet. *(SP-3.)*

**Adaptive Governance.** Built. *Proof:* a Governance Review Calendar specifies review frequency and legitimacy criteria for every significant governance structure, following a defined three-step review process rather than an ad hoc one. *Recommendation:* no review cycle has actually run yet; the honest test is whether reviews produce genuine findings or rubber-stamp confirmations. *(SP-2.)*

**Year 1 as Designed Trust.** Partial. *Proof:* the programme philosophy emphasises immediate relevance and visible competency from the first module; Year 1 is not yet specified as a distinct governed phase with its own success criteria and review frequency. *Recommendation:* give Year 1 a review checkpoint at each module boundary rather than folding it into the standard annual cycle — apply the same "new mechanism gets a shorter interval" logic already used for the Mission Hub Advisory Panels. *(CP-6.)*

**Strategy Before Structure.** Built. *Proof:* the Strategy Formation Protocol precedes curriculum decisions; no programme is designed before the strategic layer defines purpose and population. *(CP-7.)*

**The Guiding Thread.** Built. *Proof:* stackable, module-level credentials are explicitly connected through a competency architecture intended to show a learner how each piece contributes to a coherent whole, rather than presenting modularity without connective logic. *Recommendation:* the platform function that visualises this thread automatically is a planned milestone, not yet live. *(LP-2.)*

**Flexibility Already Exists — The Challenge Is Enabling Institutions to Use It.** Built. *Proof:* this principle is the single most concretely evidenced in the entire register. Direct reading of the LHG's actual text — not institutional convention, not a secondary summary — surfaced a genuine, previously unused voting pathway for federation academics (§ 9 Absatz 1 Satz 5, § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3) that prior drafts had defaulted away from without checking whether the statute foreclosed it. The same discipline caught two inaccurate secondary-source claims about comparator universities' actual governing texts. *Recommendation:* formalise primary-source verification as a standing requirement before any accreditation submission, not just a practice exercised once. *(GP-8.)*

## 5. Role Clarity, Mandate, and Bounded Authority

**Layered System Alignment.** Built. *Proof:* the G1–G4 layer boundaries are now explicitly mapped to named statutory bodies rather than left as an architectural abstraction — G1 is the Hochschulrat, G2 the Rektorat, G3 the Senat and delegated academic bodies, G4 the Kanzler/in and administration. *(P-1.)*

**Role Clarity and Mandate Transparency.** Partial. *Proof:* Leadership and Stewardship Profiles define competency requirements and term limits; specific provisions (exclusion lists, proactive-capacity thresholds, scope-review triggers) exist in a separate document and are not yet folded into the Profiles themselves. *Recommendation:* pure editorial integration, not new design — fold the existing provisions directly into the Profiles in a single pass and mark the source document superseded on this point. *(P-9.)*

**The Aligned Authority.** Built. *Proof:* every governance body's authority classification is now consolidated into a single instrument — the Decision-Rights Matrix — rather than distributed unevenly across prose sections. *Recommendation:* partner-institution authority alignment, as distinct from OUG-internal alignment, is not yet reflected in the partnership agreement template. *(HP-3.)*

**The Bounded Role.** Partial. *Proof:* strategic scope, governance layer, and term limits are specified for governance roles; the same exclusion-list and proactive-capacity provisions named above under Role Clarity are still pending integration. *Recommendation:* identical fix, same editorial pass. *(HP-4.)*

**Expert Design Authority.** Partial. *Proof:* the Quality Commission and Design Authority hold distinct, named quality functions; the explicit boundary between design decisions (expertise-based) and governance ratification decisions (representation-based) is not yet stated with worked examples. *Recommendation:* apply the Federated Legal Model's own corrective formula for dual-domain authority — a role may touch more than one kind of decision, but each individual decision has exactly one owner — to this boundary specifically. *(CP-3.)*

**The Unbundled Teacher.** Built. *Proof:* the Federated Legal Model's five-way separation explicitly distinguishes delivery responsibility from academic authority — a partner may deliver teaching under an OUG-approved framework without thereby holding curriculum or examination authority. *(CP-4.)*

**Responsibility for Transformation Must Be Explicitly Assigned.** Built. *Proof:* a named Transformation Reserve protects a minimum share of the Rector's time for transformation work specifically, reviewed separately from operational objectives, with an annual public accountability report. *(GP-5.)*

**Encode the Innovation, Not the Innovator.** Partial. *Proof:* this session's own practice is itself evidence — the Kooperationsvereinbarung finding, the Community Circle design, and the Mission Hub Challenge Framework were all written directly into statute and standing governance documents rather than left as tacit knowledge held by whoever happened to discover them. *Recommendation:* the practice is sound but untested by an actual leadership transition; no formal succession-proofing exercise has yet been run against any of this session's new mechanisms. *(EH-1.)*

## 6. Governance Architecture: Protected Independence and Legitimate Participation

**Collegial Governance.** Partial. *Proof:* genuine student voting rights exist in the Senate; the mechanisms that protect collegial process from being bypassed under an "efficiency" claim are not yet specified. *Recommendation:* apply the Conflict Escalation Framework's own route-differentiation logic — name the specific categories of genuinely time-critical decision that may shorten consultation, rather than leaving one vague exception that pressure will stretch. *(P-3.)*

**Participatory Strategy Formation.** Built. *Proof:* the Strategy Formation Protocol specifies an eighteen-month participatory cycle with an explicit prohibition on top-down predetermination. *Recommendation:* unoperated — no strategy cycle has yet been completed to test whether the designed process produces genuine ownership in practice. *(P-4.)*

**Governance as Designed Counterbalancing.** Built. *Proof:* the OKR Framework's own boundary — that financial/executive layers may note concerns about an academic Key Result but may not direct the academic response — is now written directly into statute: a Mission Hub's academic findings require Senate, not Rektorat, interpretation before they can ground closure. *(SP-4.)*

**The Protected Strategy.** Partial. *Proof:* the prohibition on conflating operational coordination with strategic governance is stated; protected agenda rules for designated strategic forums exist only in a supporting document, not as each forum's own standing order. *Recommendation:* follow the precedent the Community Circle set — a norm that lives only in a supporting document stays optional under pressure; write it into each forum's own rules of procedure. *(HP-5.)*

**The Structured Crossing.** Partial. *Proof:* the Programme Development Forum and Advisory Panels are real, designed crossing points; the Partnership Office's role as a continuous intelligence function is not yet operationally specified with named metrics and cadence. *Recommendation:* model it directly on the Advisory Panels' Communiqué mechanism — named metrics, fixed cadence, a documented and reviewable output. *(HP-8.)*

**The Designed Task.** Built. *Proof:* a full Task Architecture exists as a formal governance document specifying the OUG/partner/joint task split, including an explicit ten-working-day classification protocol for tasks that arrive uncovered. *Recommendation:* whether tasks arriving by proximity are actually caught and routed, rather than quietly absorbed by whoever is nearest, is the real test during first partner onboarding. *(HP-7.)*

## 7. Leadership as a Professionalised, Bounded Vocation

**Professionalisation of Leadership.** Built. *Proof:* the Leadership Development and Induction Framework specifies competency-based development for governance roles, explicitly treating leadership as learnable rather than a status conferred by academic seniority. *(P-11.)*

**Exogenous Incentives Can Shift Governance Logic — But Only If Leadership Converts Them.** Built. *Proof:* a named Incentive Conversion process requires the Rector to answer three specific questions before any structural response to an external incentive is approved, reviewed by the Hochschulrat, with a companion Conversion Outcome note filed once the incentive ends. *(GP-6.)*

**The Designed Incentive.** Built. *Proof:* the institution's structural design removes the primary conditions that produce a year-end spending flush — no campus, no block-grant absorption pressure in the current model, ECTS-based compensation aligned with delivery. *Recommendation:* when public block-grant funding eventually arrives, carryforward provisions need to be designed into that specific agreement from the start, not retrofitted afterward. *(HP-6.)*

**Leadership as Legitimate Vocation with Structural Accountability.** Built. *Proof:* term limits, defined return pathways, and a protected-capacity requirement (a fixed share of leadership time shielded from operational demand) are constitutionally specified for governance roles. *(SP-7.)*

**The Heart and Mind of Leadership.** Partial. *Proof:* Leadership and Stewardship Profiles require both academic legitimacy and organisational design capacity for appointment; how both dimensions are actually measured, rather than merely completed as training, is not yet specified. *Recommendation:* apply the OKR Framework's own Key Result design discipline — measurable, time-bound, a checkable result rather than a completed activity — to leadership capability assessment specifically. *(SP-6.)*

## 8. Law and Structure as Enabling Conditions, Not Just Constraints

**Design Before Constraint.** Built. *Proof:* the founding architecture is repeatedly built by asking what a first-principles design requires before asking what current constraint permits — most visibly demonstrated this session in the Kooperationsvereinbarung research, which started from what the institution needed and only then checked what the statute allowed, rather than starting from assumed restriction. *(GP-1.)*

**The Legible Law.** Partial. *Proof:* several founding documents distinguish statutory requirement from institutional choice in places; no standing instrument reviews this distinction on a defined cycle across the whole institution. *Recommendation:* add this as a new row in the Governance Review Calendar — the mechanism for exactly this kind of "does this classification still hold" question already exists and just needs to be pointed at one more artifact. *(HP-1.)*

**Law as Enabling Structure.** Partial. *Proof:* the founding premise rests on the most permissive good-faith reading of the LHG, actively exercised rather than only claimed — the Kooperationsvereinbarung discovery is the clearest evidence available anywhere in this register. *Recommendation:* the founding interpretation must be actively defended in every future accreditation and regulatory engagement, not asserted once and assumed to hold. *(LP-6.)*

**Audit Over Accreditation — Maturity Against Self-Defined Goals.** Built. *Proof:* a Developmental Audit Framework specifies a genuine peer-based review of institutional purpose, distinct from and prior to accreditation compliance review, with a first audit scheduled at the end of Year 1. *Recommendation:* this is the one entry where "Built" most clearly means "specified," not "practiced" — no audit has run, since the institution has not yet reached the end of a first year. *(SP-8.)*

## 9. Governance Capacity for Innovation, Renewal, and Institutional Learning

**Governance Is the Condition for Sustained Pedagogical Innovation.** Partial. *Proof:* named mechanisms for recognising and propagating excellent pedagogical practice are referenced in the founding design; this session did not verify their operational specification directly. *Recommendation:* confirm the Stichprobe and Design Authority actually perform this propagation function in practice, not only in description. *(GP-4.)*

**The Learning Organisation.** Partial. *Proof:* the Bug Protocol treats underperformance as a system signal rather than individual failure; explicit, named protected experimental space per unit is not yet formalised. *Recommendation:* Portfolio Governance's Class A/B change categories are already, in substance, a standing sandbox — formalise "one protected experimental space per Mission Hub" as an explicit allocation within that existing category rather than inventing a separate mechanism. *(SP-5.)*

**Designed Access — renewal dimension.** *(Cross-referenced from Section 3; see LP-5 above.)* An institution that only ever admits learners who already fit its existing structures cannot discover what those structures are failing to serve — worth reading alongside the access assessment, not scored separately here.

**Expressible Vision.** Partial. *Proof:* the institutional vision is clear and distinctive at the founding-team level; no Communication Architecture tested against real external audiences at multiple levels of compression yet exists. *Recommendation:* build and test the shortest version first — a 30-second statement, against a handful of real conversations with people outside the founding team — before investing in a full four-level architecture that has never been said aloud to a stranger. *(CP-8.)*

**The Diverse Ecosystem Principle.** Built. *Proof:* the founding premise articulates a distinctive institutional role — federated, fully online, modular, publicly funded — that no existing German institution occupies, encoded in structural features rather than only in mission language. *(SP-1.)*

**Diagnose at the Correct Level.** Partial. *Proof:* the Conflict Escalation Framework's six-route differentiation is itself a form of level-correct diagnosis for disputes specifically; no general diagnostic register exists for governance problems outside that one domain. *Recommendation:* extend the same logic that already distinguishes horizontal, competence-boundary, academic, administrative, and constitutional disputes into a lighter, general-purpose diagnostic habit for non-dispute governance questions. *(EH-0.)*

**Design Bureaucracy as a Medium for Experimentation.** Built. *Proof:* Portfolio Governance's four-class change system explicitly distinguishes local, reversible change (no formal ratification required) from institution-wide normative change, matching this principle's requirement precisely. *(EH-2.)*

**Design the Interfaces First.** Built. *Proof:* the Federated Legal Model's five-way authority separation, the Mission Hub Advisory Panels, the Programme Development Forum, and the External Cooperation Councils were all built as designed crossing points between institutional actors before the units on either side of those crossings were fully specified — interface design genuinely preceded unit design in this session's own sequence of work. *(EH-3.)*

**Build the Ecosystem That Produces Innovators.** Partial. *Proof:* Research Groups are specified as voluntary, cross-institutional collaborative units within Mission Hubs; the broader ecosystem conditions (norms, support structures, community formation) this principle asks for were not verified in depth this session. *Recommendation:* check Research Groups' actual operating conditions against this principle specifically, rather than assuming the structural specification is sufficient on its own. *(EH-4.)*

Zero named gaps, consistent with the Third Edition's finding — but the count of Partial entries has risen from eighteen to twenty-five, not because the institution regressed, but because this document is more honest about scope than its predecessor: it is scoring fifty-eight principles instead of forty-two, and sixteen of them had never been scored before. A wider register with a higher Partial count is a more trustworthy instrument than a narrower one with a lower count achieved by not looking as hard.

> This document, like its predecessors, describes design completeness more often than operating history. Almost nothing in it has been tested by a full academic year, a genuine governance dispute, or a leadership transition. The next honest edition of this signature will be the first one that can report on some of that — and it should be expected to move some of today's "Built" markers back toward "Partial," not only forward, if the mechanisms don't hold under real conditions. That possibility is not a risk to manage away. It is what makes this instrument worth trusting.
