*Founding Years · Third Edition*

> **What a Design Signature is — and what it is not**
>
> A Design Signature is not a mission statement. Mission statements describe intentions. A Design Signature describes choices — what an institution has built, what it has deliberately not yet built, and what it commits to becoming. The distinction matters because intention without structure is aspiration, and aspiration without accountability is performance.
>
> This is the third edition. It adds no new principles — its forty-two remain those derived from the six original dialogue sources — but corrects the signature against a governance-repository reconciliation pass, fixes two internal inconsistencies present since the Second Edition, and adds a Recommendation to every entry still marked Partial: a concrete "how," grounded in a mechanism the institution has already built somewhere else, rather than a generic call to "specify this eventually."
>
> We are not everything we intend to be. We are honest about the distance between where we stand and where we are going. That honesty is itself a design principle.

## II. The Signature by Source


### Expert Dialogues — Person 1 and Person 2

#### P1 — Layered System Alignment
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The G1–G4 four-layer governance architecture is constitutionally specified with explicit domain boundaries and the constitutional insulation of G3 from G2 financial pressure. The Portfolio Governance Framework routes decisions through appropriate layers by institutional reach. *(Third Edition)* The layer boundaries are now explicitly mapped to named statutory bodies — Governance & Decision Framework § 2.5 states G1 is the Hochschulrat, G2 the Rektorat, G3 the Senat and delegated academic bodies, G4 the Kanzler/in and administration.

**What we have deferred.** A governance health review mechanism — periodic check of whether layer boundaries are being respected in practice — has not yet been specified.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before the first governance cycle: specify a bi-annual layer boundary audit as a standing G1 agenda item, with a named reviewer and a defined consequence when boundary violations are found.

#### P2 — Distributed & Staged Decision-Making
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The prepared decision model is explicit: committees prepare, coordination aligns, executive synthesises, G3 decides on academic matters, G1 validates where required. No single person can take a binding decision unilaterally in any domain.

**What we have deferred.** A lightweight fast-track procedure for G4-level decisions where the staged process is too slow for operational pace has not been specified.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first operational phase: specify a time-bounded fast-track escalation protocol for G4 decisions, with automatic G3 notification and a defined review window.

#### P3 — Collegial Governance
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Academic Community Charter commits to collegial governance with genuine student voting rights in the Senate. G3 independence structurally prevents top-down domination of academic decisions. *(Third Edition correction)* "Academic contributor voting rights" is no longer accurate as a blanket claim: federation academics engaged through a standard Lehrauftrag hold advisory standing only; only those engaged through a Freistellungsvereinbarung hold the statutory Wahlrecht a genuine voting right requires (Grundordnung § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3; Federated Legal Model § 8).

**What we have deferred.** The mechanisms that sustain collegial culture against institutional pressure — specifically what happens when efficiency arguments are used to bypass collegial process — are not yet specified.

**Recommendation.** Model this on the Conflict Escalation Framework's own logic: it doesn't have one generic "urgent, so skip the process" exception, it names six distinct route types so urgency in one domain can't be used to justify bypassing a different domain's process. Apply the same discipline here — name the specific categories of genuinely time-critical decision that may shorten consultation, explicitly, rather than leaving a single vague "efficiency" exception that pressure will inevitably stretch to cover more than it should.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first Senate convening: specify a collegial governance protection protocol — the conditions under which an efficiency claim can legitimately shorten consultation, and those under which it cannot.

#### P4 — Participatory Strategy Formation
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The Strategy Formation Protocol specifies an 18-month cycle, a harvesting threshold, and explicit prohibition of top-down predetermination. Mission Hub contributors participate from the earliest phases.

**What we have deferred.** The first strategy cycle has not yet been completed. Participatory design in documents differs from participatory practice in operation.

**What we commit to becoming.** Document the first strategy cycle — its actual participation rate, degree to which practitioner input shaped the final strategy, and any gaps between designed process and lived experience — as institutional learning for cycle two.

#### P5 — Strategic Clarity and Feasibility
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The OKR Framework specifies a manageable set of institutional goals with explicit feasibility assessment requirements. The Bug Protocol assigns accountability for underperformance. The Strategic Reserve provides resource flexibility.

**What we have deferred.** The OKR set for the founding phase has not yet been finalised against operational reality.

**Recommendation.** Use the Bug Protocol's own Flag → Diagnose → Classify structure (OKR Framework & Governance Dashboard § IV) as the audit template itself, run proactively rather than only after a Key Result is missed: for every founding-phase OKR, classify now whether it is currently measurable, whether it is actually being measured, and whether a missed result would trigger the Act stage or just sit on the dashboard. This turns "finalise the OKR set" into the same disciplined three-step check the institution already uses for failure, applied pre-emptively.

**What we commit to becoming.** At end of founding year: conduct a strategic clarity audit — are the OKRs measurable, are they being measured, and are the results being used for adaptive decisions rather than performance theatre?

#### P6 — Continuous Feedback & Adaptive Execution
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The OKR Framework specifies quarterly review cycles. The platform's learning analytics infrastructure (Milestone 7) is designed to generate continuous feedback. The three-layer Partnership Crossing Point provides operational intelligence between Stichprobe cycles.

**What we have deferred.** In the founding phase, before the platform reaches Milestone 7, the continuous feedback mechanism is partial.

**Recommendation.** Don't build a parallel manual mechanism — assign this explicitly to the Quality Commission's existing Stichprobe cycle as an interim data source. The Stichprobe already produces exactly the kind of continuous, structured intelligence this principle wants; the actual gap is administrative (nobody is yet assigned to route Stichprobe findings into OKR review) rather than structural. Naming that routing explicitly is a smaller task than it currently appears to be.

**What we commit to becoming.** Specify a founding-phase feedback proxy: what manual intelligence-gathering will substitute for platform analytics before Milestone 7 is live? Name the mechanism, the frequency, and the person responsible.

#### P7 — Transformation-Oriented Performance
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The OKR Framework explicitly rejects KPI-only management and specifies progression-oriented objectives. The Bug Protocol treats underperformance as a system signal requiring design response, not individual blame. G1's annual strategic review asks whether the institution has progressed, not merely maintained.

**What we have deferred.** The cultural practice of using OKRs for genuine learning rather than performance reporting is not yet established — the institution has not yet operated.

**What we commit to becoming.** In year two: publish an honest internal account of the first OKR cycle — what was achieved, what was not, and what the institution learned.

#### P8 — Committees as Distributed Intelligence
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The governance architecture specifies functional committees with domain expertise requirements — not status-based composition. The Quality Commission, Mission Hub Steering Groups, and Senate committees are defined by what they know, not who they are.

**What we have deferred.** The committees have been designed but not yet populated.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first committee appointments: publish the competency criteria for each committee alongside the appointment process. Make the criteria — not the names — the public record.

#### P9 — Role Clarity and Mandate Transparency
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Leadership and Stewardship Profiles define roles with explicit competency requirements and governance layer assignments. Term limits and return pathways are specified.

**What we have deferred.** Exclusion Lists, Proactive Capacity thresholds, and scope review triggers are specified in the Gap Closure document but not yet integrated into the Profiles themselves.

**Recommendation.** This is a pure integration task, not a design task — the content already exists. Fold Gap Closure's provisions directly into each Leadership and Stewardship Profile in a single editorial pass, then mark those sections of the Gap Closure document superseded so there is one authoritative source rather than two documents that can drift apart — the same DRY correction this session applied when Governance Architecture's Section 3.10/6.5 stopped restating the old escalation ladder and pointed at the Conflict Escalation Framework instead.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first roles are filled: update all Leadership and Stewardship Profiles to include Exclusion Lists, Proactive Capacity thresholds, and scope review triggers. The Profile is the governance document; the Gap Closure decisions belong inside it.

#### P10 — Checks and Balances
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** G1 appointment authority, G3 constitutional insulation, Senate veto on academic standards, Quality Commission independence from G2, and the Partner Compliance architecture together form a multi-layer system in which no single actor controls outcomes across all domains. *(Third Edition)* The conflict resolution protocol named below as deferred now exists in specified form — the Conflict Escalation Framework distinguishes six distinct routes, and Grundordnung § 9 states explicitly that Portfolio Governance regulates procedure only.

**What we have deferred.** The protocol is now specified; whether it holds under a genuine, contested G2/G3 dispute is still unproven.

**What we commit to becoming.** After the first G2/G3 boundary dispute: document the resolution process and publish the institutional learning as a governance precedent.

#### P11 — Professionalisation of Leadership
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The Leadership Development and Induction Framework specifies competency-based development for all governance roles. Leadership is explicitly defined as a learnable, bounded, temporal role — not a status or a reward for academic excellence.

**What we have deferred.** The development programme has been designed but not yet delivered.

**What we commit to becoming.** After first leadership induction cycle: assess whether the programme produced measurable change in governance practice, not just completion of training hours. Adjust based on the assessment.

#### P12 — University as Socially Embedded Institution
*Source: Person 1 & 2* · **● Built** *(↑ from Partial)*

**What we have built.** The OUG's founding purpose — serving working adults, caregivers, and geographically excluded learners at €25 per ECTS — is a direct institutional response to a specific social condition. *(Third Edition)* The mechanism previously found missing — how the institution revises itself when the social conditions that justified it change — is now built, in a different and arguably stronger shape than originally envisioned: societal-need input is a required, recorded part of opening, closing, and renaming any Mission Hub (Grundordnung § 8 Absätze 9–12), fed by the Mission Hub Advisory Panels' annual public Communiqué and its explicit Duty to Voice.

**What we have deferred.** An institution-wide Mission Alignment Review at G1 level, as originally envisioned, was not built in that specific form — the Hub-level mechanism substitutes for it rather than adding to it.

**What we commit to becoming.** Observe at least one full annual cycle of Advisory Panel Communiqués across all Mission Hubs before deciding whether an institution-wide review is still needed in addition to the Hub-level mechanism.


### HEI Observation Principles — HP-1 through HP-8

#### HP-1 — The Legible Law
*Source: HEI Observations* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** Several OUG documents distinguish between statutory requirement and institutional choice. The Readers' Guide for Accreditation explicitly addresses which OUG features require legislative support. The Gap Closure document includes a preliminary Governance Compliance Map.

**What we have deferred.** The Governance Compliance Map is not yet a standing governance instrument with a defined review cycle.

**Recommendation.** Add the Compliance Map as a new row in the Governance Review Calendar, the same way the Community Circle, Mission Hub Advisory Panels, and Bug Protocol were added this session — the Calendar already exists and already handles exactly this "does this artifact still reflect reality" question for other instruments. This is a small addition to an existing table, not a new mechanism requiring design.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first staff cohort: integrate the Governance Compliance Map into the annual governance calendar as a standing review item assigned to the Rector.

#### HP-2 — The Living Mandate
*Source: HEI Observations* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The OUG was designed for the person the 1973 ruling did not imagine. Every structural feature — fully online, modular, asynchronous-first, €25 per ECTS, Europass credentials per module — is a direct response to the lifelong learner the existing legal framework still does not recognise as its primary subject.

**What we have deferred.** Nothing material is deferred here. The Living Mandate is the founding premise.

**What we commit to becoming.** In every external communication and accreditation submission: name the target learner explicitly. Resist the language drift that substitutes 'student' for 'working adult caregiver navigating education in fragments across a career'.

#### HP-3 — The Aligned Authority
*Source: HEI Observations* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** Every governance body has a single, consistent authority classification, specified in the Governance & Decision Framework. *(Third Edition)* This is now consolidated into a single operational instrument, the Decision-Rights Matrix — final authority, agenda/co-creation, mandatory consultation, execution, and escalation route for all nine decision domains in one table.

**What we have deferred.** Authority alignment has been designed for OUG-internal governance. Authority structures of partner institutions and how they interact with the OUG map have not yet been specified in the partnership agreement template.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first partner onboarding: add minimum authority alignment requirements to the partnership agreement template.

#### HP-4 — The Bounded Role
*Source: HEI Observations* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Leadership and Stewardship Profiles define strategic scope, governance layer, competency requirements, and term limits for all governance roles.

**What we have deferred.** Exclusion Lists, Proactive Capacity thresholds, and scope review triggers have been designed in the Gap Closure document but not yet integrated into the Profiles.

**Recommendation.** Identical fix to P9 above, and worth doing in the same editorial pass rather than twice — same source document, same destination, same integration task.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first roles are filled: integrate all three features into all Profiles. Role accumulation begins on day one.

#### HP-5 — The Protected Strategy
*Source: HEI Observations* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Strategy Formation Protocol prohibits conflating operational coordination with strategic governance. Four strategic forums are designated with protected agenda rules.

**What we have deferred.** The protected agenda rules exist in the Gap Closure document but not yet as standing orders of the designated forums.

**Recommendation.** Follow the precedent Grundordnung § 5 Absatz 14 set for the Community Circle: a governance norm that only lives in a supporting document stays optional under pressure; once it's written into the relevant body's own Geschäftsordnung as a standing order, it isn't. Have each of the four designated strategic forums formally adopt its protected agenda rules the same way, rather than leaving them as a Gap Closure recommendation the forum could quietly decline to follow.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first strategic forum convenes: adopt protected agenda rules as standing orders for all four designated forums.

#### HP-6 — The Designed Incentive
*Source: HEI Observations* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The OUG's structural design removes the primary conditions that produce the Year-End Flush. No campus, no block grant absorption pressure in the current model, ECTS-based contributor payment aligned with delivery.

**What we have deferred.** When public block grant funding arrives, the structural incentive to consume before year-end will operate unless carryforward provisions are in place from the start.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first HFV funding agreement: design carryforward provisions and Strategic Reserve mechanics into the agreement.

#### HP-7 — The Designed Task
*Source: HEI Observations* · **● Built** *(↑ from Gap — committed)*

**What we have built.** The full Task Architecture now exists as a governance document — Partner Membership Framework § 5, formally designated "OUG Task Architecture v1.0" by Amendment B of the Lehrpersonalmodell Amendment. It specifies the OUG/Partner/Joint task split in full, including an explicit protocol for tasks that arise uncovered ("Unclassified," routed to the Partnership Office within ten working days).

**What we have deferred.** Nothing material against this principle's original ask. The Critical Gap named in the first stress test is closed.

**What we commit to becoming.** Monitor the Unclassified-task pathway during the first year of partner onboarding — the real test is whether tasks arriving by proximity are actually caught and routed, not whether the document exists.

#### HP-8 — The Structured Crossing
*Source: HEI Observations* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** Students have Senate voting rights. The Stichprobe is a designed annual crossing. The three-layer crossing architecture is specified in the Gap Closure document.

**What we have deferred.** The Partnership Office's role as a continuous intelligence Crossing Point has not yet been operationally specified as a standing procedure.

**Recommendation.** Model this directly on the Mission Hub Advisory Panels' Communiqué mechanism: named metrics, a fixed cadence, and a recorded — not necessarily fully public, but at minimum documented and reviewable — output, rather than an informal "intelligence function." The Advisory Panels prove this pattern actually produces something checkable; apply the same shape to the Partnership Office rather than inventing a different one.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first operational year: specify the Partnership Office's intelligence function with named metrics, defined frequencies, and a documented escalation path to the Quality Commission.


### Person Learn8 — The Learner Perspective

#### LP-1 — The Designed Profile
*Source: Person Learn8* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The OUG's founding documents explicitly name target learner profiles: working adults, caregivers, geographically excluded, career reorienting. The programme design and pricing reflect these profiles as primary subjects.

**What we have deferred.** A formal Learner Profile Map as a governance instrument that serves as a design input to programme review does not yet exist.

**Recommendation.** Build it the way the Mission Hub Challenge Framework built its illustrative charter-area table: explicitly non-exhaustive, a small set of concrete profiles rather than an attempt at a complete taxonomy. A Learner Profile Map that tries to be exhaustive before first admissions will stall; one that names four or five real profiles clearly, marked as illustrative and growable, can actually ship in time to be useful.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first programme review: produce a Learner Profile Map and integrate it as a required input to all programme design and review decisions.

#### LP-2 — The Coherent Path
*Source: Person Learn8* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The platform architecture is designed around a growing Competency Profile. The stackable credential design (Europass per module) is the structural expression of the red thread. ESCO alignment provides external competency vocabulary.

**What we have deferred.** The platform's Competency Profile function is a planned milestone, not yet live.

**What we commit to becoming.** Specify a founding-phase orientation proxy: what structured advising process substitutes for the automated Competency Profile before it is live?

#### LP-3 — The Visible Development
*Source: Person Learn8* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** Europass Digital Credentials are issued per completed module. Partial completion has credential value from day one.

**What we have deferred.** The credential issuance process is architecturally designed but operationally dependent on Europass infrastructure integration.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first module completions: complete and test the Europass credential issuance integration. The first learner who completes the first module must receive their credential automatically.

#### LP-4 — The Active Member
*Source: Person Learn8* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** Students hold Senate voting rights. The Academic Community Charter specifies asynchronous and digital participation formats. Mission Hub communities are organised around shared intellectual purpose.

**What we have deferred.** Asynchronous governance participation formats — how a learner who studies at 11pm can contribute to institutional decisions — are not yet fully specified.

**Recommendation.** Reuse the two-tier pattern the Community Circle established: a broader, lower-friction input channel that feeds a smaller live decision-making session, rather than trying to make every live meeting itself asynchronous-compatible. Give students an async-first input channel — written, on their own time — that feeds directly into Senate agenda-setting, the same relationship External Cooperation Councils now have to the Community Circle, rather than treating "attend the live session" as the only participation format worth designing for.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first Senate session: specify asynchronous participation protocols for all governance bodies that include student members.

#### LP-5 — The Designed Entry
*Source: Person Learn8* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The OUG accepts prior learning recognition and professional experience as entry criteria alongside traditional qualifications. The financial model is designed for learners who could not afford private alternatives.

**What we have deferred.** The prior learning recognition process — the committee, the criteria, the timeline, the advising support — has not yet been operationally specified.

**Recommendation.** Use the founding Design Authority as the template (Design Authority Constitution § II): a small, real, named body sized for the founding phase specifically, with an explicit expansion pathway rather than an attempt to stand up a large representative committee that won't actually be ready in time. A prior-learning-recognition body doesn't need to be big to be legitimate at founding; it needs to be real and named.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first admissions cycle: specify the prior learning recognition process as a complete operational procedure with a named committee, published criteria, defined timelines, and dedicated advising support.

#### LP-6 — The Permission Floor
*Source: Person Learn8* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The OUG's founding premise is built on the most permissive good-faith interpretation of the LHG. The Governance Compliance Map classifies rules as Statutory Requirement, Institutional Choice, or Open Design Space. *(Third Edition)* This principle was actively exercised, not only claimed, during this session's reconciliation — direct reading of the LHG's actual text (§ 9 Absatz 1 Satz 5, § 12 Absatz 1 Nummer 3) surfaced a genuine, previously unused voting-rights pathway for federation academics that prior drafts had defaulted away from without checking whether the statute actually foreclosed it.

**What we have deferred.** The Compliance Map is a founding document. In operation, pressure to interpret the law conservatively will come from multiple directions. The founding interpretation must be actively defended.

**Recommendation.** Formalise the practice that just worked: primary-source verification — reading the actual statute text, not a summary of it — as a standing requirement before any accreditation submission or regulatory position is finalised. This session also caught two inaccurate secondary-source claims (about Göttingen's and Vienna's actual faculty-establishment procedures) precisely by insisting on primary text over summary. That discipline, made standing rather than incidental, is the entire recommendation.

**What we commit to becoming.** In every accreditation submission and regulatory engagement: state explicitly which OUG features are institutional choices within the legal space. Make the distinction a public institutional position.


### Person Curr4 — Curriculum as Governance

#### CP-1 — Purpose Before Access
*Source: Person Curr4* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The OUG's purpose is defined in substantive terms — who it serves, what they gain, why the existing system does not serve them — before any access mechanism is specified. The €25/ECTS price point is the structural expression of this sequence.

**What we have deferred.** The Purpose Statement and Value Proposition are distributed across founding documents rather than consolidated in a single reviewable document.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first external communications campaign: consolidate the Purpose Statement and Value Proposition into a single governance document reviewed every three years alongside the strategic plan.

#### CP-2 — Quality-Gated Federation
*Source: Person Curr4* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Partner Membership Framework specifies a non-negotiable curriculum quality standard assessed during Phase 2 onboarding. The three-outcome eligibility assessment (Eligible / Conditionally Eligible / Not Eligible) is the selection mechanism that replaces negotiation.

**What we have deferred.** The Design Authority that enforces the standard is named but not yet constituted as a functioning body with named members, explicit expertise criteria, and a documented assessment procedure.

**Recommendation.** This one doesn't need new design work — the Design Authority Constitution already specifies the founding composition table, quorum rules, and a Year 3/4 expansion pathway in full. The gap is appointment, not design. Recommend executing the appointment process already written rather than treating this as still requiring specification.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first partner onboarding: constitute the Design Authority as a functioning body. The quality gate exists on paper; the mechanism that operates it does not yet.

#### CP-3 — Expert Design Authority
*Source: Person Curr4* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Quality Commission holds independent quality oversight. The Partner Membership Framework names the Design Authority as the body that assesses partner course quality. The Academic Steward role holds curriculum authority within Mission Hubs.

**What we have deferred.** The formal boundary between curriculum design decisions and governance ratification decisions has not been specified explicitly. It exists implicitly but can be captured by committee processes under pressure.

**Recommendation.** Apply the same corrective formula the Federated Legal Model now uses for its dual-domain-authority principle (§ 4(3)): a body or role may touch more than one kind of decision, but each individual decision has exactly one owner and one domain. State explicitly, with worked examples, which specific decisions the Design Authority owns outright versus which it merely informs before a governance body ratifies — the same clarity that stopped the Kanzler/in's dual G2/G4 position from being ambiguous should be applied here.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first curriculum governance cycle: document the explicit boundary between design decisions (made by expertise) and governance decisions (made by representation), with examples of decisions that fall on each side.

#### CP-4 — The Unbundled Teacher
*Source: Person Curr4* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The Competence Codebook v10.0 (149 entries across 23 domains) is the designed curriculum framework any module must map to. The OOAPI v5 format requirement standardises module metadata. The Partner Membership Framework explicitly assigns module delivery to the partner and outcome specification to the OUG.

**What we have deferred.** The Curriculum Designer role is not yet a formally constituted role with a defined appointment process.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before the first cohort enrols: constitute the Curriculum Designer role with named responsibilities, relationship to the Design Authority, and a defined review cycle.

#### CP-5 — Concept Over Content
*Source: Person Curr4* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The curriculum is built around capability development rather than content transmission. The Competence Codebook specifies 149 atomic competencies at Bloom levels L1–L4. Platform Milestone 2 converts modules from content descriptions to competency maps.

**What we have deferred.** Whether partner institutions share the concept-over-content orientation in practice is not guaranteed by design — only by quality verification, and Platform Milestone 7 is not yet live.

**What we commit to becoming.** Until Platform Milestone 7 is live: specify a manual proxy for detecting content-delivery practice in partner modules, as part of the Stichprobe sampling protocol.

#### CP-6 — Year 1 as Designed Trust
*Source: Person Curr4* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The programme philosophy emphasises immediate real-world relevance, competency visibility from the first module, and Europass credentials from day one. The Didactic Framework specifies Year 1 as 'Learning to See' with a Bloom average of 2.1.

**What we have deferred.** Year 1 is not yet specified as a distinct governed phase with its own success criteria, review frequency, and named accountability.

**Recommendation.** Apply the same logic the Governance Review Calendar used for the brand-new Mission Hub Advisory Panels: a deliberately *shorter* review interval than the standard, specifically because the mechanism is new and unproven, so a real problem surfaces in months rather than at year-end. Give Year 1 a review checkpoint at each module boundary rather than folding it into the standard annual curriculum review — the newest, least-proven phase of the whole programme is exactly the wrong place to apply the same cadence used for stable, established years.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first cohort enrols: specify Year 1 success criteria beyond ECTS completion, assign a more frequent review cycle than other curriculum years, and name the person accountable for the trust-establishment function.

#### CP-7 — Strategy Before Structure
*Source: Person Curr4* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The Strategy Formation Protocol precedes all curriculum decisions. Learning strategy, teaching strategy, and assessment strategy exist in substance across founding documents. No course is designed before the strategy layer.

**What we have deferred.** The three strategies exist in substance but are distributed across multiple documents rather than consolidated into three explicitly titled governing documents.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first accreditation submission: consolidate learning strategy, teaching strategy, and assessment strategy into three named public documents — each short, specific, and reviewable on a defined cycle.

#### CP-8 — Expressible Vision
*Source: Person Curr4* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The OUG's vision is clear and distinctive at the level of the founding team. The founding documents demonstrate that the institution can articulate what makes it different.

**What we have deferred.** A Communication Architecture — canonical expressions of the vision at four levels of detail (30 seconds, 2 minutes, 5 minutes, full narrative), tested with actual audiences — does not yet exist as an institutional document.

**Recommendation.** No close internal precedent for this one, worth saying plainly rather than forcing an analogy that doesn't fit. The honest recommendation: build and test the shortest version first — the 30-second statement, against a handful of real conversations with people outside the founding team — before investing in all four levels at once. A polished four-level Communication Architecture that has never been said out loud to a stranger carries real risk of being wrong in ways only a live conversation would reveal.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first partnership conversations: develop and test the Communication Architecture at all four levels with representative audiences. Maintain it as a governance document updated when the curriculum design changes.


### Person 7org — System Perspective

#### SP-1 — The Diverse Ecosystem Principle
*Source: Person 7org* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The OUG's founding premise is the articulation of a distinctive role no existing institution fills. This role is encoded in structural features — €25/ECTS, asynchronous-first, modular, fully online — not only in mission language.

**What we have deferred.** A formal Role Statement positioned explicitly against the European higher education ecosystem — reviewable on a defined cycle — does not yet exist as a standalone governance document.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first institutional review: extend the Design Signature into a formal Role Statement. Review it every three years alongside the strategic plan.

#### SP-2 — Adaptive Governance
*Source: Person 7org* · **● Built** *(↑ from Partial)*

**What we have built.** The G1–G4 architecture was designed with adaptability as an explicit principle. Constitutional insulation of G3 prevents financial calcification. *(Third Edition)* The gap this principle named is now closed: the Governance Review Calendar specifies review frequency and legitimacy criteria for every significant governance structure, following a defined three-step process (Legitimacy Statement, independent Review Finding, Hochschulrat endorsement).

**What we have deferred.** The Calendar exists and specifies review cycles; none has yet actually run, since the institution has not yet operated.

**What we commit to becoming.** After the first full Review Calendar cycle completes: publish an honest account of which reviews produced genuine findings and which produced rubber-stamp confirmations.

#### SP-3 — Structure as Home and Challenge
*Source: Person 7org* · **● Built** *(↑ from Partial)*

**What we have built.** The Mission Hub model organises around societal challenges rather than inherited disciplinary categories — now specified precisely in the Mission Hub Challenge Framework, including a hard rule that a Hub may never be named for a discipline alone. *(Third Edition)* The gap this principle named is now closed, and exceeded: the Permeability Architecture specifies the internal Hub-to-Hub interfaces originally asked for, and the Advisory Panels specify an external Hub-to-society counterpart beyond what was originally requested.

**What we have deferred.** Both architectures are specified but unoperated. Whether either mechanism produces genuine encounter rather than performative compliance is unproven.

**What we commit to becoming.** After the first Mission Hub's first full annual cycle: publish both the Permeability Statement and the Advisory Panel Communiqué in full, unedited, as the first real test of either mechanism.

#### SP-4 — Governance as Designed Counterbalancing
*Source: Person 7org* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** The G1–G4 architecture creates genuine counterbalancing between strategic direction, academic quality, and operational execution. *(Third Edition)* The OKR Framework's boundary — that G2 may note concerns about a G3-relevant Key Result but may not direct the academic response — is now written directly into statute: Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 10 requires Senate, not Rektorat, interpretation of academic Key Result findings before they can become grounds for Mission Hub closure.

**What we have deferred.** The counterbalancing design holds in the architecture. The test will come in the first genuine G2/G3 boundary dispute.

**What we commit to becoming.** After the first significant G2/G3 boundary dispute: document the resolution process and publish the institutional learning as a governance precedent.

#### SP-5 — The Learning Organisation
*Source: Person 7org* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Bug Protocol treats underperformance as a system signal rather than individual failure. The Strategy Formation Protocol creates a strategic learning loop. The three-layer Partnership Crossing Point brings operational intelligence into governance.

**What we have deferred.** Protected experimental space — explicit institutional permission for actors to try non-standard approaches without reputational consequence — is not yet specified.

**Recommendation.** This already exists as a category, just not under this name: Portfolio Governance's Class A/B change categories are, in substance, a standing, pre-authorised sandbox — local, reversible, no approval required, documented for learning rather than compliance. Recommend formalising "one protected experimental space per Mission Hub" as an explicit standing Class A/B allocation rather than inventing a separate protection mechanism that would duplicate one already built.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before the first operational year: designate at least one formally protected experimental space per Mission Hub where non-standard approaches can be tried, observed, and either adopted or retired.

#### SP-6 — The Heart and Mind of Leadership
*Source: Person 7org* · **◑ Partial**

**What we have built.** The Leadership and Stewardship Profiles specify competency requirements that explicitly include both academic legitimacy and organisational design capacity as jointly required conditions of appointment. The Leadership Development and Induction Framework provides a structured development programme.

**What we have deferred.** The development programme is designed but not yet delivered. It does not yet specify how both dimensions are measured rather than merely completed.

**Recommendation.** Apply the OKR Framework's own Key Result design rules directly to this: measurable, time-bound, 2–4 per objective, not an activity ("attend training") but a result ("demonstrates X in a structured assessment"). The institution already has a working discipline for turning a vague aspiration into a checkable outcome; there's no reason leadership capability assessment should be designed by a different, looser standard than everything else the OUG measures.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before first roles are filled: add capability assessment to the Leadership Development and Induction Framework. Both the scientist's heart and the manager's mind must be measurably developed, not only formally introduced.

#### SP-7 — Leadership as Legitimate Vocation
*Source: Person 7org* · **● Built**

**What we have built.** Term limits are constitutionally specified for all governance roles. Return pathways are specified. Governance roles are filled through competency-based appointment. The Proactive Capacity requirement creates structural protection that prevents the role from being consumed by operational demands.

**What we have deferred.** The career pathway dimension — what governance experience at the OUG means for a person's long-term professional trajectory — is not yet formally specified.

**What we commit to becoming.** Before the first appointment cycle: specify governance career recognition explicitly — how governance experience is formally recorded and valued in subsequent career decisions.

#### SP-8 — Audit Over Accreditation
*Source: Person 7org* · **● Built** *(marker corrected — see note)*

> **Correction note.** The Second Edition's status marker read "○ Gap — committed" while this entry's own commitment text already read *"Closed by OUG_Developmental_Audit_Framework.docx."* The marker and the prose contradicted each other. This edition resolves the contradiction in favour of the more specific, more recently written prose.

**What we have built.** The OUG's quality architecture is designed for the existing accreditation system. Beyond that, the Developmental Audit Framework specifies a genuine peer-based review of whether the OUG is achieving its own stated purpose, independent of accreditation compliance: first audit at end of Year 1, Development Reports published publicly, a Development Tracker maintained quarterly.

**What we have deferred.** The Framework is specified; no audit has actually run, since the institution has not yet reached the end of a first year. This is the one entry in this edition where "Built" describes the specification, not yet the practice.

**What we commit to becoming.** The first Developmental Audit, at the end of Year 1, published in full — the accountability mechanism that makes every other commitment in this document externally verifiable rather than internally asserted.


## III. The Signature as a Commitment

Forty-two principles from six dialogue sources. **Corrected count, Third Edition:** twenty-four principles built into the founding design with strong structural anchoring; eighteen partially present — in design, in intention, or in the document stack, but not yet fully operationalised; **zero named gaps.**

> **Note on the Second Edition's summary.** The Second Edition's Section III stated "seventeen built... twenty partially present... two named gaps... three principles where the proof is in the doing" — a fourth category that never appeared in the legend or the table, and numbers that did not sum to the table's actual contents (19 built, 21 partial, 2 gap, by direct count). That inconsistency predates this edition and is corrected here rather than carried forward.

This is the first edition with no remaining named gap, and the first edition where every Partial entry carries a concrete Recommendation rather than only a future commitment date. Both are worth stating plainly and immediately qualifying: **zero gaps and eighteen recommendations do not mean the work is done.** A specification existing is not the same as that specification surviving contact with actual operation. The Governance Review Calendar has never run a cycle. The Mission Hub Advisory Panels have never issued a Communiqué. The Developmental Audit has never been conducted. Several of the recommendations above point at mechanisms — the Community Circle's two-tier pattern, the Conflict Escalation Framework's route differentiation, Portfolio Governance's Class A/B sandbox — that are themselves only weeks old and equally untested. The honest reading of this edition is: the design work that can be done before the institution opens is now essentially complete, with a stated path for what remains, and the next Design Signature will be the first one that can report on practice rather than only on specification.

> The distance between 'built' and 'committed' is not a failure. It is a map. And a map is only useful if you publish it — including the parts that are incomplete, the parts of a prior map that turned out to be wrong, and, in this edition, the specific next step for closing what remains. An institution founded on transparent design principles should document its own incompleteness, its own corrections, and its own path forward with the same rigour it brings to documenting its achievements.
