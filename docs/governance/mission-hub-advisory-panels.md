> **Position in the document architecture:** this is the outward-facing counterpart to the [Mission Hub Permeability Architecture](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/mission-hub-permeability-architecture.md), which governs how a Mission Hub stays connected to its sibling Hubs. This document governs how a Mission Hub stays connected to the society whose challenge it claims to organise around — the regional employers, alumni, and public actors closest to that specific mission domain, distinct from the institution-wide [External Cooperation Councils](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-architecture.md#43-participatory-bodies-and-their-functions) and from the partner-institution-facing Programme Development Forum.

> **Why this document exists.** A Mission Hub is organised around a societal challenge, not an inherited discipline (Strategy Constitution § 2, Grundordnung § 8). That commitment is easy to state at founding and easy to lose quietly over time — a Hub can drift from the challenge it was named for without anyone inside the Hub noticing, because the people best positioned to notice are outside it. This document gives each Mission Hub a standing, annual encounter with exactly those outside people, and — deliberately — gives that encounter teeth through publication rather than through formal veto power.

---

## I. Composition

Each Mission Hub convenes its own dedicated **Advisory Panel** — not a shared institution-wide body, and not interchangeable across Hubs. Panel membership reflects the specific societal, regional, and professional domain the Hub organises around: regional employers who hire the Hub's graduates, alumni working in the Hub's mission domain, and public or civil-society actors whose work intersects with it.

The Steward of Missions nominates Panel members, reflecting the Hub's specific mission area; the Senate is notified of the composition at its annual academic session. A Panel should be small enough for genuine deliberation — as a guide, no larger than the Hochschulrat's external membership (five) — and no smaller than three, since the Duty to Voice defined below requires enough independent perspectives in the room that silence from one member does not read as consensus.

Panel members are external to OUG and to the Hub's own membership under Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 3. **This is deliberately an advisory body, not a statutory one:** external stakeholders cannot become *Mitglieder* of a Mission Hub any more than they can of the Senate (Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 3, mirroring the LHG § 22 Absatz 3 membership categories). What this Panel offers is not a vote — it is standing, and a public voice.

---

## II. The Duty to Voice

Advisory bodies commonly fail in one specific way: they are asked for input, they give polite, hedged input, and the institution hears what it already believed. This Panel is designed against that failure specifically.

**Panel membership carries an explicit duty to raise concerns proactively — not only to answer questions put to them.** A member who observes the Hub drifting from its mission, underserving a population it claims to serve, or losing relevance to the region it claims to connect with is expected to say so without waiting to be asked, and the Panel's convening format must make room for this before any scripted agenda. Silence from the Panel is not treated as a neutral or reassuring signal — an unremarkable annual communiqué, year after year, is itself a legitimacy question the Governance Review Calendar should ask about (see Section V).

This is not adversarial by design, but it is not decorative either. **Real deliberation happens before any public statement is drafted** — the Panel meets, debates candidly, and only then produces the communiqué described below. A Panel whose members read a prepared statement and sign it has the form of this mechanism without its substance.

---

## III. Annual Convening and the Public Communiqué

**Cadence.** Each Panel convenes annually, aligned with the Hub's academic plan review and its Permeability Review cycle (see the [Permeability Architecture](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/mission-hub-permeability-architecture.md) § I) — the Hub's internal and external accountability rhythms run on the same calendar, so both questions ("are we connected to our sibling Hubs" and "are we still relevant to the challenge we exist for") are asked in the same season, not scattered across the year.

**The Communiqué.** Following each convening, the Panel issues a **public Communiqué** — published on the OUG platform, not filed as an internal memo. The Communiqué states the Panel's genuine assessment: whether the Hub remains relevant to its mission domain, what has changed in the surrounding region, industry, or society that the Hub should know about, and any concerns raised during deliberation, attributed to the Panel as a body. The Communiqué is signed by the convening Steward of Missions as received, not authored or softened by them.

---

## IV. Response Obligation

A communiqué that disappears into a drawer defeats the entire mechanism. Where a Communiqué raises a concern, the Steward of Missions must issue a **public response** within 60 days, addressing each point raised — confirming action, explaining why no action is appropriate, or committing to a specific development plan with a timeline. This mirrors the Justification Duty already established for participation more broadly (Governance & Decision Framework § 6.4): documented consideration and a reasoned response, not a veto.

Where a Communiqué's concern is not meaningfully addressed for two consecutive annual cycles, this triggers an automatic review by the Senate — the same threshold and logic the Permeability Architecture uses for a Hub with no active cross-Hub project for two consecutive quarters. The trigger is structural, not a judgment of anyone's performance: it exists to catch drift before it calcifies, not to assign blame.

## V. Relationship to Other Bodies

- **Permeability Architecture** — internal, Hub-to-Hub. This document is its external, Hub-to-society counterpart. Both are reviewed on the same annual cycle by design.
- **External Cooperation Councils** — institution-wide, annual, four generic stakeholder categories, feeding the Community Circle. This Panel is Hub-specific and domain-relevant; where an External Cooperation Council's input actually concerns one Hub's specific mission relevance, it should be redirected here rather than handled institution-wide (see Governance & Decision Framework § 4.3).
- **Programme Development Forum** — partner-*institution* academic input into curriculum design. This Panel is not about curriculum mechanics and does not include partner institutions in their teaching-delivery capacity; it is about whether the Hub's mission itself still holds up against the region and society it claims to serve.
- **Hochschulrat** — institution-wide, statutory, real G1 authority. This Panel has no comparable formal authority and does not report into the Hochschulrat directly; its accountability mechanism is publication, not escalation to a decision-making body, except via the two-cycle trigger in Section IV.

---

## VI. Status and Review

This is an advisory mechanism and does not require Grundordnung amendment — it creates no *Mitglied* status, no Wahlrecht, and no decision authority; it operates within the Mission Hub's existing collegial self-organisation under Grundordnung § 8 Absätze 7–8. It is tracked in the [Governance Review Calendar](https://github.com/eebyak/OUG/blob/main/docs/governance/governance-review-calendar.md), reviewed every 2 years alongside the Mission Hub Steering Groups, with a legitimacy criterion specifically distinct from a performance question: is the Duty to Voice actually being exercised, or has the Panel quietly become a silent circle after all?
