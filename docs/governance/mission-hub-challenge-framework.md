> **Position in the document architecture:** this is the third leg of the Mission Hub design, alongside the [Permeability Architecture](mission-hub-permeability-architecture.md) (how a Hub stays connected to its sibling Hubs) and the [Advisory Panels](mission-hub-advisory-panels.md) (how a Hub stays connected to the society it exists for). This document answers a prior question: **what is a Mission Hub actually organised around, and what stops that answer from quietly becoming a disciplinary faculty name again?** It is the non-statutory elaboration of [Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 2](grundordnung.md#-8-mission-hubs-fakultäten), which states the naming principle and one example only, deferring the full rationale here so the statute never needs amending as global challenges evolve.

> **Why this document exists.** A Mission Hub named "Digital Health" and a faculty named "Medizininformatik" can end up staffed identically and taught identically. The difference is not cosmetic. A faculty is organised around a body of disciplinary knowledge; a Mission Hub is organised around a problem the world actually has, which by construction requires people from several disciplines to solve — and which can, in principle, close when the problem is solved or reframed, in a way a faculty named after a discipline never quite can. This document exists because that distinction is easy to state once and easy to lose by the second or third Hub, when someone reaches for a familiar departmental name instead of a challenge. It happened once already, in an earlier draft of the Grundordnung, which listed "Mission Hub 1: Informatik" and "Mission Hub 2: Wirtschaft" — both disciplinary names, in direct tension with the design principle they were meant to implement. This document is what should have existed before that mistake was possible, so the next one doesn't happen quietly.

## I. The Anchoring Principle

**Every Mission Hub is chartered around one or more recognised global challenges, not a discipline.** This is not a branding preference. It is a structural claim: a Hub organised around *Digital Health* necessarily draws on computer science, medicine, ethics, policy, design, and data protection law at once — interdisciplinarity is a consequence of the naming, not a separate commitment layered on top of it. A Hub organised around *Informatik* draws on computer science, and interdisciplinary work becomes something the Hub does in addition to its core identity rather than something built into what it is.

**External legibility matters as much as internal design.** Rather than inventing a bespoke taxonomy of "global issues," Mission Hub charters are anchored to the **UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** — a reference framework ministries, accreditation bodies, industry partners, and prospective students already recognise. A Hub charter should name which SDG(s) it primarily addresses; this is a legibility choice, not a claim that the SDGs are the only valid framing of a global challenge, and a Hub may address a challenge that spans several SDGs at once.

**The hard rule, stated plainly, from [Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 2](grundordnung.md#-8-mission-hubs-fakultäten):** a Mission Hub may not be named for a discipline alone. "Informatik," "Wirtschaft," "Medizin" are not valid Hub names under this framework, however natural they feel to reach for. "Digital Health," "Sustainable Cities and Communities," "Trustworthy Digital Infrastructure" are.

## II. Illustrative Charter Areas

**This list is illustrative, not exhaustive, and not binding.** It exists to make the naming principle concrete and to give a home to specific cross-cutting themes that don't belong inside a governance procedure document but need to live somewhere citable. The actual roster of Mission Hubs at any given time is whatever has been opened and not yet closed under [Grundordnung § 8 Absätze 9–10](grundordnung.md#-8-mission-hubs-fakultäten) — this section does not itself open or close anything.

| Illustrative charter area | Primary SDG anchor(s) | Interdisciplinary draw |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Digital Health** *(the OUG's founding Mission Hub, per Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 2)* | SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-Being) | Computer science, medicine, health policy, data protection law, human-centred design |
| **Women in MINT / Gender Equity in Technology** | SDG 5 (Gender Equality), SDG 4 (Quality Education) | Computer science, education research, sociology, organisational design |
| **Sustainable Cities and Communities** | SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) | Urban planning, computer science, public administration, environmental science |
| **Trustworthy Digital Infrastructure** | SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure), SDG 16 (Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions) | Computer science, law, public policy, cybersecurity |
| **Climate-Resilient Systems** | SDG 13 (Climate Action), SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure) | Engineering, environmental science, data science, economics |

**Interdisciplinary stewardship** — the expectation that a Hub's leadership and membership actively cultivate contribution from multiple disciplines rather than treating interdisciplinarity as an aspiration — is not a separate charter area. It is a standing expectation of every Mission Hub, reinforced structurally by the [Permeability Architecture](mission-hub-permeability-architecture.md)'s cross-Hub mechanisms and by the [Advisory Panels](mission-hub-advisory-panels.md)' external accountability, and is a legitimate line of inquiry in both the annual Permeability Review and the Governance Review Calendar's Mission Hub Steering Group review.

**International cities and communities**, named specifically because it doesn't map neatly onto a single illustrative area above, belongs conceptually under Sustainable Cities and Communities (SDG 11) where the federation's international partner network gives it a genuinely global rather than domestic character — worth naming explicitly here so a future Hub proposal in this space has a citable anchor rather than needing to invent one.

## III. What This Framework Does Not Do

This framework does not open, close, rename, or resize any Mission Hub — that authority and procedure belong exclusively to [Grundordnung § 8 Absätze 9–13](grundordnung.md#-8-mission-hubs-fakultäten). It does not assign final decision authority anywhere; per the [Decision-Rights Matrix](decision-rights-matrix.md), portfolio-scope decisions of this kind sit with G2 (Rektorat), with Senate consultation where mission-alignment or academic implications arise. This framework is interpretive and illustrative, not a source of institutional authority in its own right — consistent with the "binding" distinctions already drawn in [Governance & Decision Framework § 0](governance-architecture.md).

## IV. When Societal-Need Input Is Required — and When It Isn't

Not every change to a Hub's academic offering needs to go back to society for a fresh mandate. Confusing the two — treating routine curricular growth as if it required the same justification as founding a new institutional unit — would make the mechanism too heavy to survive contact with actual practice. The table below is the single place to check which applies.

**Two different instruments, not one.** The Advisory Panel's Communiqué is a qualitative instrument — it answers "does this Hub still matter, and does anyone outside it think so." The OKR framework's Objective and Key Result, assigned to every Hub at opening (Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 9), is a quantitative instrument — it answers "is this Hub actually becoming what its societal-need case said it would become," the same distinction the OKR framework draws institution-wide between KPI health-monitoring and OKR transformation-tracking. A Hub can pass one and fail the other: a Panel can find a Hub's mission still matters while its Key Result has stalled, or a Hub can be hitting its Key Result numbers while the Panel senses the underlying challenge has quietly shifted. Neither instrument alone would catch both failure modes; that is why both feed the trigger table rather than either being treated as sufficient on its own.

| Decision | Requires societal-need input? | Mechanism | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening a new Mission Hub | **Yes** | Documented societal-need case, informed by the relevant External Cooperation Council, expressed as a stated Objective with at least one measurable Key Result | Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 9 |
| Closing a Mission Hub | **Yes** | Recent [Mission Hub Advisory Panel](mission-hub-advisory-panels.md) Communiqués **and** the Hub's Key Result standing considered together; either a repeatedly unaddressed Communiqué **or** a sustained sub-Bug-Protocol-threshold Key Result is independently sufficient grounds for an unscheduled review | Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 10 |
| Renaming a Mission Hub | **Yes**, unless correcting a disciplinary name back to a challenge name | Most recent Advisory Panel Communiqué referenced | Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 12 |
| Assigning a genuinely new degree programme to a Hub | **Yes**, as awareness input, not a veto | Advisory Panel's most recent Communiqué feeds Portfolio Governance's Class C awareness step | [Portfolio Governance](portfolio-governance.md) § 4.3, Class C |
| Organic growth of an existing degree already assigned to a Hub | **No** — delegated entirely to the Hub | Mission Hub academic bodies, light coordination only, no escalation | [Portfolio Governance](portfolio-governance.md) § 4.3, Class B |
| Ordinary annual Hub review | **Yes** — this is where the Communiqué originates | Annual convening and public Communiqué, read alongside that cycle's OKR dashboard entry | [Mission Hub Advisory Panels](mission-hub-advisory-panels.md) § III |

The line that matters most: **a Hub growing what it already does does not need to re-justify its existence every time it grows.** Only decisions that touch the Hub's identity, scope, or continued existence trigger the societal-need input — which is also why the Duty to Voice matters as much as the trigger table does. A Panel that only ever gets asked the same annual question produces the same annual answer; a Panel that knows its Communiqué actually feeds four real decisions has a reason to say something that isn't reassuring when something isn't going well.

**On the OKR connection specifically:** confirmed against the OKR Framework's actual text (`strategy/okr-framework-dashboard.md`). Two things worth stating precisely rather than left implicit:

1. **Where does a Mission Hub's Objective sit in the OKR cascade?** The OKR Framework's four-layer table doesn't name Mission Hubs explicitly among its G3 or G4 example owners (G3: Academic Senate / Senate Committees; G4: programme teams, Research Clusters, Partnership Office) — but the [Governance Review Calendar](governance-review-calendar.md) already classifies Mission Hub Steering Groups as **"G3/4,"** straddling both. A Mission Hub's Objective is therefore owned at that same boundary: the Steward of Missions and Steering Group hold it operationally (G4), while its academic content is subject to G3 authority as described below. This isn't fully specified anywhere yet and is flagged as a genuine, small gap rather than resolved by inference here.
2. **A Key Result touching academic judgment is not the Rektorat's to interpret.** Per the OKR Framework's own "OKRs and Academic Freedom" section: *"Where an OKR touches areas of academic judgment... the Academic Senate has exclusive authority to interpret and respond to the data. The G2 layer may note concerns but may not direct academic responses to OKR findings."* Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 10 reflects this directly — a sub-threshold Key Result only becomes closure-relevant after the Bug Protocol's Flag/Diagnose/Classify steps have run, and, where the cause is academic, after the Senate — not the Rektorat — has interpreted the finding. The confirmed threshold is a Key Result score below **0.4** (scored 0.0–1.0; 0.7 counts as good).

## V. Relationship to Other Instruments

- **[Grundordnung § 8](grundordnung.md#-8-mission-hubs-fakultäten):** the statutory naming principle (Absatz 2) and the opening/closing procedure (Absätze 9–11) this framework elaborates.
- **[Mission Hub Permeability Architecture](mission-hub-permeability-architecture.md):** internal, Hub-to-Hub connection — a standing requirement regardless of which challenge a Hub is chartered around.
- **[Mission Hub Advisory Panels](mission-hub-advisory-panels.md):** external, Hub-to-society accountability — the Panel's Duty to Voice includes, implicitly, checking whether the Hub still serves the challenge named in its charter.
- **[Governance Review Calendar](governance-review-calendar.md):** Mission Hub Steering Groups are already reviewed every two years against "is the Hub still organised around a societal challenge with strategic relevance" — this framework gives that review question a concrete external reference (the SDGs) to check against, rather than a subjective judgement call each cycle.
- **[Design Authority Constitution](design-authority-constitution.md):** the founding Design Authority's domain-expert seats are chartered per Mission Hub (currently Digital Health); as new Hubs open under Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 9, new domain-expert seats follow per that document's own expansion pathway.
- **OKR Framework & Governance Dashboard** (`strategy/okr-framework-dashboard.md`): every Mission Hub's Objective and Key Result, assigned at opening (Grundordnung § 8 Absatz 9), tracked and scored there — Section IV above draws on that framework's described KPI/OKR distinction without restating its full mechanics.
