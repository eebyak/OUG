This is the constitutional layer: the documents that establish what the OUG legally is, who decides what, and how decisions travel between the people closest to the work and the people accountable for the institution's mission. Two things distinguish this domain from a conventional university's statutes. First, the legal foundation is written explicitly as a public-benefit institution — not a discount version of a commercial one — because that status is what makes its funding model lawful under European state-aid law. Second, the governance architecture treats feedback as a structural obligation, not a cultural aspiration: there are documents whose only job is to specify how information gets from the people running a programme to the people who can change the strategy around it.

Nineteen documents live in this folder. Read individually, several of them are dense. Read as a system, they're more legible than the count suggests — most of what looks like 19 separate things is really six clusters, each answering a different question.

## How to Read This Folder

Six clusters, each doing a different job. The order below is roughly "read this first" — constitutional bedrock, then the machinery that runs day to day, then the documents that explain the institution to people outside it, then the two documents that grade everything else's homework.

### 1. Constitutional Core

*What the OUG legally is, and who holds which authority*

- **[Grundordnung](grundordnung.md)** — the founding statute. German, because it's a legal document that will eventually be submitted as one. Establishes the organs (Rektorat, Senat, Hochschulrat), their composition and elections, the Mission Hub structure, and — since a recent completion pass — a fully drafted Kanzler role (§7) and Gleichstellung mandate (§10). § 16 is the hinge: it's what formally pulls the two Anlagen into the statute rather than leaving them as unattached "separate documents."
- **[Federated Legal Model](federated-legal-model.md)** — the constitutional argument underneath the statute. English, and considerably more detailed than the Grundordnung on one specific point: the separation of employment authority, academic authority, degree-awarding authority, and operational administration into four distinct, non-mergeable domains. This is where the G1–G4 layering that shows up everywhere else in this folder actually gets its constitutional grounding — nine Parts, twenty-one numbered sections, plus appendices covering everything from the Legal Responsibility Matrix to an Academic Affiliation Contract template.
- **[Anlagen](anlagen.md)** — the bridge between the two documents above and everything below. Anlage 1 is meant to carry the institution's First Principles and Design Principles, and honestly says so: that content lives in the book, not here, and this page names that as a real, unresolved scope question rather than pretending it's settled. Anlage 2 is in much better shape — it's a working index into the operating-architecture cluster below, and it's most of the reason that cluster exists as a legible set rather than a pile of documents.

**Start here if:** you need to know who has authority to do what, or why a decision can't be overruled by a different layer of the institution.

### 2. Legal Compliance

*What has to be true, under German and EU law, before the OUG can operate*

- **[Allgemeinwohl Verfassung](allgemeinwohl-verfassung.md)** — the public-benefit legal case. German. Argues, with the actual EU state-aid case law cited, why the OUG's €25/ECTS fee is a cost contribution rather than a market price, and why that distinction is what makes public funding and federation resources legally compatible at all. This is not a formality — it's the difference between the OUG being lawful and not.
- **[Rechtliche Implementierungsvoraussetzungen](legal-implementation-requirements.md)** — the master checklist. Seven legal readiness points, each with a status marker, each tied to a deadline relative to the first partner contract. Point 1 is a compressed version of the Allgemeinwohl Verfassung's argument; points 2 through 7 cover state recognition, teaching-capacity law, examination authority, GDPR across a federated model, copyright in jointly-produced modules, and accreditation readiness.

**Start here if:** you're a lawyer, a ministry contact, or anyone who needs the "is this actually legal" answer before anything else matters.

### 3. Operating Architecture

*How decisions actually get made, day to day*

- **[Governance & Decision Framework](governance-architecture.md)** — the hub of this entire folder. Nearly every other document in this cluster either implements a piece of it or gets cited by name as "the Governance Architecture." Defines the G1–G4 layers in operational terms, nine decision domains (D-A through D-I, each assigned to exactly one layer), the Participation and Agenda-Setting Architecture, and the "dual-identity" model — the Senate is legally a Senate but constitutionally a Community Assembly, the Rectorate is legally a Rectorate but constitutionally a Coordination Council, and so on.
- **[Committee Information Architecture](committee-information-architecture.md)** — solves a specific failure mode: committees that each do careful work but never talk to each other. Specifies designed membership overlaps between Senate, Doctoral Studies Committee, Research Ethics Committee, Mission Hubs, Programme Committees, and Research Clusters, plus the Cross-Committee Coordination Session and Shared Findings Register that keep information moving laterally, not just up and down.
- **[Inter-Layer Feedback Protocol](interlayer-feedback-protocol.md)** — the vertical counterpart. Specifies how implementation findings travel from G4 up to G1, with a four-level signal classification (Green/Amber/Red/Black) that determines who has to respond and how fast, plus a full Constitutional Concern Protocol for the rare case where a finding suggests the institution's founding commitments are actually being compromised.
- **[Portfolio Governance](portfolio-governance.md)** — the framework for academic and curricular change specifically. Four change classes (A through D, local-and-reversible through normative-and-institution-wide), each routed to the governance layer with the appropriate authority — so that a didactic tweak inside one module doesn't require the same process as revising the Prüfungsordnung.
- **[Design Authority Constitution](design-authority-constitution.md)** — the body that holds curriculum standard authority specifically at the federation boundary. Answers a question the other documents in this cluster don't: when a partner institution wants to contribute a module, who decides whether it's good enough, and can that decision be overridden by anyone with a commercial or political interest in saying yes? (Answer: no — not even by G2 executive authority.)

**Start here if:** you want to understand how the institution actually runs once it's operating, not just how it's legally structured.

### 4. Gap-Closure & Culture Mechanisms

*Specific fixes for specific, named failure modes*

- **[Governance Gap Closure v2](governance-gap-closure-v2.md)** — the record of five design decisions made in direct response to a structured stress test. Its most consequential decision draws a single line at the OUG/partner boundary: anything touching the official credential record belongs to OUG; anything touching how a student earns that credential belongs to the partner. Everything else in this cluster is, in some sense, a fuller build-out of one of this document's five decisions.
- **[Code of Collegial Conduct](code-of-collegial-conduct.md)** — not a rulebook. A shared compact signed by everyone in a governance role, addressing something structural documents can't: what collegial behaviour actually looks like in a distributed, mostly-asynchronous institution where hierarchy calcifies more easily in writing than in a room.
- **[Governance Review Calendar](governance-review-calendar.md)** — makes structural revision a scheduled, routine event instead of a crisis. Every significant governance structure — Hochschulrat, Senate, Quality Commission, Mission Hub Steering Groups, and others — has a review date on which it has to justify its continued existence against its current purpose.
- **[Incentive Conversion Statement](incentive-conversion-statement.md)** — a discipline for a specific danger: external funding or policy mandates that produce reorganisation dressed up as transformation. Forces three questions to be answered, on the record, before the Hochschulrat approves any structural response to an external incentive — and forces an honest follow-up once the incentive ends.
- **[Mission Hub Permeability Architecture](mission-hub-permeability-architecture.md)** — the OUG replaced disciplinary faculties with Mission Hubs specifically to avoid one kind of silo. This document exists because a strong, healthy Mission Hub community can quietly become a new kind of silo if nothing forces it to stay porous — four mandatory interfaces (a cross-Hub seminar, a cross-Hub project, a rotating governance seat, an annual review) keep that from happening quietly.

**Start here if:** you want to know what specific problem a specific document was written to solve — this cluster is the OUG's own list of things it caught itself almost getting wrong.

### 5. External-Facing Positioning

*How the OUG explains itself, to three different audiences*

- **[Governance Artikel v2](governance-article-v2.md)** — academic and ministry framing. German. Argues the OUG isn't a bildungspolitische Einzelmaßnahme but a structural synthesis of the Wissenschaftsrat's system-level recommendations and organisational governance principles developed independently through expert dialogue.
- **[Strategic Positioning — The Missing Layer](strategic-positioning-missing-layer.md)** — EU policy framing. Argues the OUG occupies a specific, currently-empty architectural position in the global skills-first education system: the trusted "Layer 2" credential infrastructure between where learning happens and where labour markets need to read it.
- **[Strategic Clarity Statement](strategic-clarity-statement.md)** — public framing, for students, partners, and anyone else. Five guiding principles (Access by Design, Public Not Private, Quality Without Compromise, Federated Not Isolated, Governed by Its Community), each translated into what it concretely means for a student, a partner, and a policymaker. Also where the Mission Integrity Review — the mechanism that checks the OUG is still serving who it says it serves — is defined.

**Start here if:** you're drafting something for an external audience and need the version of the argument that's already been made for that audience.

### 6. Self-Assessment Scorecards

*The OUG grading its own governance, in public*

- **[Governance Principles Assessment](governance-principles-assessment.md)** — scores the institution against twelve unified principles drawn from two expert dialogues. Honest about where it lands: five strong, five partial, two genuine gaps (participatory strategy formation, transformation-oriented performance management) — both of which point at documents in the `strategy/` domain that don't exist in this repository yet, so whether those gaps are still open is currently unverifiable from here.
- **[Design Signature Founding](design-signature-founding.md)** — the full scorecard: forty-two principles across six dialogue sources, each rated Built, Partial, or Gap-with-a-committed-closure-date. The most rigorous single document in this folder for answering "how much of this is real versus aspirational" — including one place where it disagrees with Governance Gap Closure v2 about whether a specific gap (HP-7) is actually closed, which is noted rather than quietly resolved.

**Start here if:** you want the unvarnished version — what's actually built, versus what's still a stated intention.
