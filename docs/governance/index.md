This is the constitutional layer: the documents that establish what the OUG legally is, who decides what, and how decisions travel between the people closest to the work and the people accountable for the institution's mission. Two things distinguish this domain from a conventional university's statutes.

First, the legal foundation is written explicitly as a public-benefit institution — not a discount version of a commercial one — because that status is what makes its funding model lawful under European state-aid law. Second, the governance architecture treats feedback as a structural obligation, not a cultural aspiration: there are documents whose only job is to specify how information gets from the people running a programme to the people who can change the strategy around it.

Twenty-nine documents live in this folder. Read individually, several of them are dense. Read as a system, they're more legible than the count suggests — most of what looks like twenty-nine separate things is really six clusters, each answering a different question.

## How to Read This Folder

Six clusters, each doing a different job. The order below is roughly "read this first" — constitutional bedrock, then the machinery that runs day to day, then the documents that explain the institution to people outside it, then the ground-truth registers and the two scorecards built from them.

### 1. Constitutional Core

*What the OUG legally is, and who holds which authority*

* **[Grundordnung](grundordnung.md)** — the founding statute. German, because it's a legal document that will eventually be submitted as one. Establishes the organs (Rektorat, Senat, Hochschulrat), their composition and elections, the Mission Hub structure, the Kanzler role (§7), the Gleichstellung mandate (§10), and — as of § 5 Absatz 14 — the Community Circle, the Senate's quarterly deliberative convening distinct from its formal voting sessions. §16 is the hinge: it formally pulls the two Anlagen into the statute rather than leaving them as unattached separate documents.

* **[Federated Legal Model](federated-legal-model.md)** — the constitutional argument underneath the statute. English, and considerably more detailed than the Grundordnung on one specific point: the separation of employment authority, academic authority, degree-awarding authority, delivery responsibility, and administrative authority into five distinct, non-mergeable domains.

 Structured as seven Parts (Status & Hierarchy, Allocation of Authority, Academic Affiliation, Quality Assurance, Partnership Principles, Safeguards, Transitional Provisions), plus a consolidated Legal Responsibility Matrix. It no longer carries its own escalation ladder or amendment procedure — those are incorporated by reference from the **[Conflict Escalation Framework](conflict-escalation-framework.md)** and **[Amendment Procedure](amendment-procedure.md)** below, to avoid maintaining two competing versions of either.

* **[Degree and Examination Authority](degree-examination-authority.md)** — companion to the Federated Legal Model, split out as its own document: exclusive degree-awarding authority, the OUG-only/dual/joint award taxonomy, and the objective ECTS-threshold formula that triggers (but does not automatically grant) dual or joint degree eligibility.

* **[Academic Affiliation Agreement Template](academic-affiliation-agreement-template.md)** — the contractual template implementing the Federated Legal Model's academic-affiliation provisions, distinguishing what an affiliated academic is entitled to under a standard Lehrauftrag versus a Freistellungsvereinbarung.

* **[Conflict Escalation Framework](conflict-escalation-framework.md)** — six distinct escalation routes (horizontal coordination, competence-boundary review, academic, administrative/legal, constitutional, and individual legal remedies), each with its own first step and resolving body. Referenced by name throughout this folder wherever a document needs to route a dispute rather than invent its own ladder.

* **[Amendment Procedure](amendment-procedure.md)** — the default amendment procedure for any instrument in this repository that doesn't define its own (the Strategic Constitution and the Grundordnung both already have theirs). Closes a gap the Governance & Decision Framework had: it described itself as binding without ever specifying how it could be changed.

* **[Decision-Rights Matrix](decision-rights-matrix.md)** — the nine Governance & Decision Framework domains (D-A through D-I), consolidated into one operational table: final authority, who sets the agenda, who must be consulted, who executes, and which Conflict Escalation Framework route applies if it's disputed.

* **[Anlagen](anlagen.md)** — the bridge between the documents above and everything below. Anlage 1 addresses the institution's First Principles, Derived Institutional Capabilities, and Design Principles as the constitutional basis for interpreting the architecture.

 Anlage 2 is a binding index into the operating-architecture cluster below, connecting the Grundordnung to the governance layers, decision classes, escalation principles, subsidiarity, academic independence, feedback protocols, and portfolio-governance mechanisms through which it is implemented.

**Start here if:** you need to know who has authority to do what, or why a decision can't be overruled by a different layer of the institution.

### 2. Legal Compliance

*What has to be true, under German and EU law, before the OUG can operate*

* **[Allgemeinwohl Verfassung](allgemeinwohl-verfassung.md)** — the public-benefit legal case. German. Argues, with the relevant EU state-aid case law cited, why the proposed ECTS fee is a cost contribution rather than a market price, and why that distinction matters for the compatibility of public funding and federation resources.

* **[Rechtliche Implementierungsvoraussetzungen](legal-implementation-requirements.md)** — the master checklist. Seven legal readiness points, each with a status marker, each tied to a deadline relative to the first partner contract. Point 1 is a compressed version of the Allgemeinwohl Verfassung's argument; points 2 through 7 cover state recognition, teaching-capacity law, examination authority, GDPR across a federated model, copyright in jointly-produced modules, and accreditation readiness.

**Start here if:** you're a lawyer, a ministry contact, or anyone who needs the "is this actually legal" answer before anything else matters.

### 3. Operating Architecture

*How decisions actually get made, day to day*

* **[Governance & Decision Framework](governance-architecture.md)** — the hub of this entire folder.

 Nearly every other document in this cluster either implements a piece of it or gets cited by name as "the Governance Architecture." Defines the G1–G4 layers in operational terms (with an explicit Institutional Mapping to the Grundordnung's actual organs), nine decision domains (D-A through D-I, each assigned to exactly one layer), the Participation and Agenda-Setting Architecture, and the "dual-identity" model — the Senate is legally a Senate but constitutionally a **Community Circle**, convened quarterly and distinct from its statutory voting sessions; the Rectorate is legally a Rektorat but constitutionally a Coordination Council; and so on.

* **[Committee Information Architecture](committee-information-architecture.md)** — solves a specific failure mode: committees that each do careful work but never talk to each other. Specifies designed membership overlaps between Senate, Doctoral Studies Committee, Research Ethics Committee, Mission Hubs, Programme Committees, and Research Clusters, plus the Cross-Committee Coordination Session and Shared Findings Register that keep information moving laterally, not just up and down.

* **[Inter-Layer Feedback Protocol](interlayer-feedback-protocol.md)** — the vertical counterpart. Specifies how implementation findings travel from G4 up to G1, with a four-level signal classification (Green/Amber/Red/Black) that determines who has to respond and how fast, plus a full Constitutional Concern Protocol for the rare case where a finding suggests the institution's founding commitments are actually being compromised.

* **[Portfolio Governance](portfolio-governance.md)** — the framework for academic and curricular change specifically. Four change classes (A through D, local-and-reversible through normative-and-institution-wide), each routed to the governance layer with the appropriate authority — so that a didactic tweak inside one module doesn't require the same process as revising the Prüfungsordnung.

* **[Design Authority Constitution](design-authority-constitution.md)** — the body that holds curriculum standard authority specifically at the federation boundary. Answers a question the other documents in this cluster don't: when a partner institution wants to contribute a module, who decides whether it's good enough, and can that decision be overridden by anyone with a commercial or political interest in saying yes? The answer is no — not even by G2 executive authority.

**Start here if:** you want to understand how the institution actually runs once it's operating, not just how it's legally structured.

### 4. Gap-Closure & Culture Mechanisms

*Specific fixes for specific, named failure modes*

* **[Governance Gap Closure v2](governance-gap-closure-v2.md)** — the record of five design decisions made in direct response to a structured stress test. Its most consequential decision draws a single line at the OUG/partner boundary: anything touching the official credential record belongs to OUG; anything touching how a student earns that credential belongs to the partner. Everything else in this cluster is, in some sense, a fuller build-out of one of this document's five decisions.

* **[Code of Collegial Conduct](code-of-collegial-conduct.md)** — not a rulebook. A shared compact signed by everyone in a governance role, addressing something structural documents can't: what collegial behaviour actually looks like in a distributed, mostly-asynchronous institution where hierarchy calcifies more easily in writing than in a room.

* **[Governance Review Calendar](governance-review-calendar.md)** — makes structural revision a scheduled, routine event instead of a crisis. Every significant governance structure — Hochschulrat, Senate, Community Circle, Quality Commission, Mission Hub Steering Groups, Mission Hub Permeability Architecture, and others — has a review date on which it has to justify its continued existence against its current purpose.

* **[Incentive Conversion Statement](incentive-conversion-statement.md)** — a discipline for a specific danger: external funding or policy mandates that produce reorganisation dressed up as transformation. Forces three questions to be answered, on the record, before the Hochschulrat approves any structural response to an external incentive — and forces an honest follow-up once the incentive ends.

* **[Mission Hub Permeability Architecture](mission-hub-permeability-architecture.md)** — the OUG replaced disciplinary faculties with Mission Hubs specifically to avoid one kind of silo. This document exists because a strong, healthy Mission Hub community can quietly become a new kind of silo if nothing forces it to stay porous — four mandatory interfaces (a cross-Hub seminar, a cross-Hub project, a rotating governance seat, and an annual review) keep that from happening quietly. Internal, Hub-to-Hub.

* **[Mission Hub Challenge Framework](mission-hub-challenge-framework.md)** — the third leg of the Mission Hub design: what a Hub is actually organised around. Anchors Hub naming to global challenges (UN SDGs) rather than disciplines, with the hard rule stated plainly — a Hub cannot be named for a discipline alone. Written after an earlier Grundordnung draft named its first two Hubs "Informatik" and "Wirtschaft," directly contradicting the principle it was meant to implement.

* **[Mission Hub Advisory Panels](mission-hub-advisory-panels.md)** — the outward-facing counterpart to the Permeability Architecture above: one dedicated external panel per Mission Hub, deliberately run on the same annual cycle, built around a Duty to Voice — members are expected to raise concerns unprompted, not just answer questions — and a public Communiqué rather than a private report.

**Start here if:** you want to know what specific problem a specific document was written to solve — this cluster is the OUG's own list of things it caught itself almost getting wrong.

### 5. External-Facing Positioning

*How the OUG explains itself, to three different audiences*

* **[Governance Artikel v2](governance-article-v2.md)** — academic and ministry framing. German. Argues the OUG isn't a *bildungspolitische Einzelmaßnahme* but a structural synthesis of the Wissenschaftsrat's system-level recommendations and organisational governance principles developed independently through expert dialogue.

* **[Strategic Positioning — The Missing Layer](strategic-positioning-missing-layer.md)** — EU policy framing. Argues the OUG occupies a specific, currently-empty architectural position in the global skills-first education system: the trusted "Layer 2" credential infrastructure between where learning happens and where labour markets need to read it.

* **[Strategic Clarity Statement](strategic-clarity-statement.md)** — public framing, for students, partners, and anyone else. Five guiding principles (Access by Design, Public Not Private, Quality Without Compromise, Federated Not Isolated, Governed by Its Community), each translated into what it concretely means for a student, a partner, and a policymaker. Also where the Mission Integrity Review — the mechanism that checks the OUG is still serving who it says it serves — is defined.

**Start here if:** you're drafting something for an external audience and need the version of the argument that's already been made for that audience.

### 6. Ground-Truth Registers & Self-Assessment Scorecards

*The primary-source foundations, and the OUG grading its own governance against them, in public*

* **[Principles Register](principles-register.md)** — every principle articulated across the book's dialogue sources, extracted directly from the source text rather than from any prior summary, and re-sorted by meaning into nine thematic sections instead of by which dialogue produced them. Fifty-eight dialogue-derived principles plus three founding principles reconstructed from the University of Bologna's original governing arrangement — sixty-one entries in total, each keeping its original source code only as a citation. Exists because the earlier, more familiar count of "forty-two" describes the derived pattern catalogue below, not the actual principle base underneath it — a distinction this register made explicit for the first time.

* **[Design Pattern Register](design-pattern-register.md)** — the companion register for the book's 42 antipattern/design-pattern pairs, same treatment: re-sorted from the book's seven source blocks into thirteen thematic groupings, each pattern's full primary text read directly rather than summarised from the catalogue index. States plainly where this session's own governance work already implements a pattern by name, and flags at least one place where a pattern's own book citation pointed at a document this repository has since corrected (Pair 36's four-year Rector term, superseded by the Grundordnung's actual six-to-eight-year term).

* **[Governance Principles Assessment](governance-principles-assessment.md)** — scores the institution against twelve unified principles drawn from two expert dialogues. It identifies five areas of strong alignment, five partial areas, and two gaps. Several of the mechanisms proposed in the assessment — including participatory strategy formation, an OKR framework, structured inter-layer feedback, committee information architecture, and leadership development — are now represented elsewhere in the repository. The assessment should therefore be read as a diagnostic document whose individual findings must be checked against the subsequent architecture.

* **[Design Signature](design-signature-founding.md)** — the full scorecard, rebuilt on the Principles Register above rather than on the earlier forty-two-principle count. Fifty-eight principles, organised into the Register's same nine thematic sections, each rated Built or Partial — the first edition with zero named gaps, and the first where every Partial entry carries a concrete Recommendation naming the existing mechanism that should close it, not just a future commitment date. States its own limits plainly: "Built" mostly means the specification exists, not that it has survived a full year of actual operation.

**Start here if:** you want the unvarnished version — what's actually built, versus what's still a stated intention, and what the claim is actually derived from.

Unresolved questions and matters requiring further reconciliation are maintained separately in the **[known gaps register](known-gaps.md)**.
