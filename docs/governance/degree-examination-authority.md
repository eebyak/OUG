# Part I — Degree Authority

## § 1 Exclusive Degree-Awarding Authority

(1) OUG holds exclusive authority to confer academic degrees in all programmes accredited under its responsibility. Degrees are conferred solely in the name of OUG, except under a formally established dual or joint degree arrangement (Part III).

(2) This authority includes determination of graduation eligibility, issuance of diplomas and transcripts, maintenance of official academic records, and revocation in accordance with due process.

## § 2 Signature Authority

Degrees conferred by OUG bear the official institutional name and seal and the signatures of the **Rector** and the **Kanzlerin/Kanzler**, in their statutory capacity as representatives of the institution (Grundordnung §§ 3–4, § 7). No additional partner-institution signatures appear on an OUG-only degree.

## § 3 Central Academic Registry

(1) OUG maintains a central, legally controlled academic registry for all degrees awarded in its name, including final grades, degree classifications, award dates, and archival examination records.

(2) OUG retains exclusive responsibility for verification of degree authenticity, correction of academic records, certification copies, and digital authentication. Partner institutions do not maintain parallel official registries for OUG-awarded degrees; they may maintain local operational records, which are not the authoritative record (Federated Legal Model § 13).

## § 4 Revocation Authority

OUG retains authority to revoke a degree awarded in its name where fraud is proven, academic misconduct materially affected eligibility, or statutory revocation conditions are met, following the due process defined in the Prüfungsordnung.

# Part II — Award Taxonomy

**This corrects the previous version of this document, which asserted OUG retained "all substantive academic authority regardless of award arrangement" while simultaneously describing partner-assessed ECTS contributions — an internal contradiction.** Three distinct award models exist, each with different legal consequences:

## § 5 OUG-Only Award

OUG has sole awarding authority. The partner contributes delivery within OUG-approved frameworks. OUG registry, diploma, and revocation authority are exclusive. This is the default model.

## § 6 Dual Award

Each institution issues its own award under coordinated requirements. Responsibilities and records are contractually aligned; **each awarding institution retains legal responsibility for its own award** — OUG does not claim exclusive academic authority over a partner's own dual-award instrument, only over the OUG award itself.

## § 7 Joint Award

A single joint award is issued. Shared academic and legal responsibilities are expressly defined in the formal agreement required under § 9. **No institution's share of responsibility may be inferred solely from an ECTS contribution percentage** — the formal agreement, not the threshold calculation in Part III, defines what each institution is actually responsible for.

## § 8 Delivery, Assessment, and Academic Authority Are Distinct

A partner may deliver teaching and provide first-line assessment under an OUG-approved academic framework and an OUG examination board without thereby holding "academic authority" over the programme in the § 5 of the Federated Legal Model sense. The distinctions are:

- **Delivery responsibility** — teaching, supervision.
- **First-line assessment contribution** — marking under OUG regulations and OUG examination board oversight.
- **Formal academic authority** — curriculum standards, examination regulations, quality assurance (OUG, G3).
- **Final grade confirmation** — the OUG Examination Board.

# Part III — Joint and Dual Degree Threshold

## § 9 Formal Agreement Requirement

A dual or joint degree may only be established through a formal written agreement between OUG and the partner institution, approved by the Senat, defining degree title, signature order, applicable examination regulations, quality assurance coordination, registry coordination, revocation procedures, and dispute resolution.

## § 10 Objective Threshold — Review Trigger, Not Automatic Entitlement

**This corrects the previous version, which treated the threshold as automatically eligibility-creating.** Where a single partner's contribution exceeds the threshold defined below, **OUG must initiate a formal dual/joint-award review** under § 9. Any resulting arrangement remains subject to OUG's degree-awarding authority, accreditation compatibility, Senate approval, and the formal agreement — the threshold triggers a mandatory conversation, not an automatic outcome.

## § 11 Calculation

For each partner institution *i*: **Partner Share (Sᵢ) = (Pᵢ / T) × 100**, where *T* is total programme ECTS and *Pᵢ* is ECTS both delivered **and** assessed under that partner's academic responsibility (per § 8's distinction — this excludes ECTS where the partner only delivers without assessment responsibility). Recognised prior learning, external credit transfer, and non-assessed participation credits are excluded from *Pᵢ*.

The review-trigger threshold is **Sᵢ > 50.000%**, calculated to three decimal places, per accredited programme structure, cohort-specific where structural variation exists, documented and auditable, and verified annually by the Quality Commission. Equal to 50.000% does not trigger review; no rounding may be used to artificially exceed the threshold.

## § 12 Stability

Once a dual or joint arrangement is formally established for a cohort, it applies consistently to that cohort; subsequent structural changes do not retroactively alter eligibility, and partner withdrawal does not invalidate degrees for a qualifying cohort already in progress (Federated Legal Model § 12).

# Part IV — Diploma Supplement and Transcript Governance

## § 13 Central Issuance and Standardised Format

All diplomas, transcripts, and diploma supplements for OUG degrees are issued exclusively by OUG, in a standardised format aligned with the EHEA framework. The diploma supplement transparently describes the federated structure of the programme, participating partners, ECTS distribution, applicable examination regulations, and the quality assurance framework, without diluting OUG's degree-awarding authority.

## § 14 Digital Authentication

OUG implements secure digital verification for degree authentication, transcript validation, and registry access control, compliant with applicable data protection and cybersecurity regulation.
