The OUG document architecture is substantial but not yet fully reconciled, legally reviewed, operationally implemented, or externally validated.

Detailed gaps are maintained within the domain to which they belong. This page provides a repository-wide orientation and identifies issues that cross the boundaries between several domains.

The presence of an item in a gap register does not necessarily mean that the underlying subject is absent. Some gaps concern contradictions between existing documents, missing implementing instruments, incomplete evidence, or decisions that have not yet been made authoritative.

## Cross-Domain Priorities

### Constitutional and Founding Coherence

The repository currently contains different assumptions about:

- the legal form and founding pathway of the OUG;
- whether G1 authority rests with the Hochschulrat, a Constitutional Council, or a defined combination of bodies;
- the number and terms of Rectorate members;
- the status and electoral rights of partner-employed affiliated academics;
- the relationship between direct OUG employment and federated academic staffing.

These issues affect Governance, Leadership, Partnership, Strategy, and legal implementation and should be resolved as one coordinated constitutional decision rather than through isolated textual edits.

### Mission Hub Architecture

Mission Hubs are central to Governance, Leadership, Curriculum, Quality, Research, and Partnership, but their provisions are distributed across several documents.

A coherent account is still needed of:

- formation and dissolution;
- membership and leadership eligibility;
- internal governance;
- academic and programme responsibilities;
- relationships with the Senate, Rectorate, Quality Commission, and partner institutions;
- resourcing, reporting, and review.

### Academic Architecture and Degree Integrity

The programme architecture is conceptually well developed, but the Modulhandbuch, Programme Document, Didactic Framework, machine-readable module data, Prüfungsordnung, and quality framework do not yet form one fully reconciled academic record.

The main work concerns:

- authoritative module titles and codes;
- module and stream totals;
- prerequisites and progression;
- the 150- versus 160-ECTS threshold;
- assessment formats;
- the relationship between narrative documents and machine-readable records;
- formal doctoral and research-ethics regulations.

### Finance, Partnership, and Delivery

The financial model, partner compensation framework, staffing assumptions, and programme-delivery architecture are not yet fully aligned.

The repository still needs authoritative decisions or implementing instruments for:

- ECTS tariffs and partner remuneration;
- funding and growth assumptions;
- partnership and provider agreements;
- partner exit and teach-out;
- the Partnership Office;
- delivery responsibilities across OUG and partner institutions;
- service ownership when a provider cannot continue.

### Operational Readiness

Several essential operational systems are distributed across the architecture but are not yet consolidated into end-to-end operating models.

The most significant are:

- **Student Support and Learner Success** — onboarding, advising, pathway planning, recognition support, accessibility, re-engagement, wellbeing referral, and case ownership;
- **Programme Delivery and Services** — scheduling, enrolment, assessment logistics, records, communications, complaints, provider continuity, and teach-out;
- **Institutional Digital and Data Architecture** — identity, student records, interoperability, data governance, security, service ownership, continuity, and preservation.

These may eventually justify a separate **Operations and Implementation** domain, but such a domain should only be created once the relevant documents exist.

### External Readiness

The Accreditation Reader's Guide, ministry materials, market claims, financial claims, and presentation decks should be reconciled only after the underlying constitutional, financial, academic, and partnership decisions are stable.

External-facing documents should distinguish clearly between:

- a proposed design;
- an internally coherent draft;
- an externally reviewed model;
- a legally or academically approved instrument;
- an implemented and evidenced practice.

## Maintaining This Register

This page should remain concise. Detailed issues belong in the relevant domain register.

Add an item here only when it:

- affects more than one domain;
- requires a coordinated institutional decision;
- concerns operational readiness not yet assigned to a domain; or
- materially affects legal, accreditation, financial, or external credibility.

Remove an item when the relevant domain documents have been reconciled and the decision has been reflected consistently across the architecture.
